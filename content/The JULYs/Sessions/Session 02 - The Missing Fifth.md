# Session 02—The Missing Fifth

## The Chessboard, and What Came After

In Moscow, [[Nanaya Rei]] sat across a half-played board from a retired coach who'd spent four months teaching him nothing and telling him everything. Zurov answered the same patient opening with a Sicilian Dragon instead of his usual Caro-Kann, sharp where the old man always played safe, and the message landed the way it was meant to: handlers dark, proceed with caution. A dead window across the boulevard. A woman who'd stopped letting her dog pull her forward. Whatever Nanaya decided to do with any of it, he was still deciding when the gold streetlight started bending toward a single point that wasn't the lamp.

Then the light took him somewhere else first. A flat world. A man at the center of a rising formation of stone, calm the way someone is calm after they've already made the choice. The earth stopped moving under his hands. A spike rose in his place, careful, deliberate, built by someone who loved what he was saving. Every other arrival to Aenath has carried this same vision at the exact moment of transport. Nanaya had never once had it explained to him; now he didn't need it explained, he'd just watched it happen.

He never got the landing the others got. Something was already waiting on the other side of the seam that pulled him through, and it had him before the vision even finished letting go. He woke up not on a lava platform with three strangers, but already inside the Hexad, hurt, alone, and short whatever the fight cost him to get there.

## Four at the Ladder

Kai, Frankie, and Odette climbed out of the Emberveins into open air neither of them had been fully sure they'd see again. Kento came with them in body, if not in spirit; the pillar-gauntlet had left him shaken and hurt in ways that hadn't finished settling, and he held back from whatever came next rather than push through it.

What waited at the top wasn't rest. Layla was on her knees in front of a tear in the world, both hands braced against it, losing. The composure that had gotten her through the Emberveins without flinching was gone. Something had gone through that seam before she could close it behind the newcomers, a fifth Rifted, taken before he even landed, and for the first time she looked like someone who didn't have the situation handled.

The three of them got the truth in pieces, none of it comfortable: whatever had him was stronger than her anchor, the seam wasn't hers to command anymore, and it would only stay open a little longer. What she could tell them was that the tear answered to Rifted and nothing else, the same thing that had pulled all of them out of their old lives was the only thing that could still pull them through this one. Whatever decided it, one by one they stepped toward it. The wall gave up being a wall.

## The Hexad

The fall ended on a floor with six sides, and Nanaya was already inside it when they landed, hurt but conscious, and no longer alone with whatever had put him there. A stranger to all three of them by any measure that should have mattered, he didn't stay one for long; something about the place made trust a formality nobody had time to stand on. The four of them worked through it together, room by room, until the door count ran out and the last one closed behind them into something that had never been a door at all. A circle, cut clean through the wall, with actual daylight on the other side.

## Out Through the Light

It wasn't alone in there with them. Something had been waiting on the mundane side of the seam since it first tore, prowling for the one signal it needed, and the portal reopening was that signal. It filled the dark corners of the last room the instant the way out appeared, planted squarely between the party and the exit.

They didn't stay to finish it. The circle behind it was already spilling real daylight into the room, and whatever was made of shadow wanted no part of standing in it. The four of them broke for the light instead of the fight, crossed the threshold with it still at their backs, and came out the other side into cold, ordinary, actual morning. Nanaya included, saved as much by the exit as by anyone's hands.

Kento was still there where they'd left him, upright, some of the shake worn off in the waiting. Five of them, in one place, for the first time since any of this started.
