# Session 06—What Marl Forgot

## Two Memories, One Street

The session began with a breathing exercise and two memories. [[Odette Langford|Odette]] went back to a childhood kitchen and a caretaker who taught her to bake. Kai, asked about the run through the streets of [[The Exodus/00_My Notes/Locations/Aenath/Corranor/Coldford|Coldford]] with the basket of bread, spoke about working alone.

## Home, and a Closed Door

[[Ruairí]] came up out of the sewers in a side lane, a long way from the building. He smelled of refuse, blood and worse, and he was still a gnome. His rat trotted beside him. Before he changed back he checked the lane for watchers. Nobody was there to smell him, and nobody had seen the bracers. [[The Tallhouse]] was an hour's walk across town. Petra had already put the word out that nobody was to touch the three of them, though whether anyone listened was another matter.

[[Kai]] and [[Odette Langford|Odette]] got there about fifteen minutes ahead of him. [[Petra Hale]] looked up and asked what had happened. Kai told her Ruairí was on his way back and had run into someone with two studs, and that he had sent word he would take another exit. Petra was growing more alarmed with every thin detail when the door opened on a filthy gnome and a rat. "Wait, Ruairí, is that you? And what is that rat? Get in." She slammed the door. The Tallhouse is shielded, so the street noise vanished the moment it closed.

The three took a short rest. Ruairí bathed, the windows stood open for an hour and a half to clear the smell, and he came out in his cat face again. Petra was waiting behind her ledgers with a mug in front of her. It was the seventh day. She had held off asking for reports because the three needed time to get among the people, and she was done waiting. "What do we have? Put it on the table."

## Everything on the Table

Ruairí went first. He had overheard [[The Claret|the Claret]] preparing to start taking tieflings, and the word was that it came from a man called Corvin. Petra leaned in. A silence spell covers the doors, but she still checked the room for scrying eyes, which sometimes show up as small balls of light. Then she said the Claret does not act without orders from above. Corvin, she said, sits one step below a leader nobody has seen. Four studs mark a lieutenant, and the lieutenants sit in [[Crystal City]], around four of them. If this really came from a lieutenant, the whole Claret presence in [[The Exodus/00_My Notes/Locations/Aenath/Corranor/Coldford|Coldford]] would be set moving. She asked for a timeline. Ruairí had none, only the rat.

The rat took some explaining. Ruairí had asked it to watch the building and to come running if a crowd gathered or left. Petra could not follow why a rat, and Ruairí's answer was that it was all he had. She slapped him hard on the back anyway and said she supposed she only had to watch for a rat at the Guild door.

Odette told her about [[Sister Margaret]]'s divination, which put the tieflings first in line, and said she did not think she had met anyone from House Colwyn. She also told her about the talk of [[The Exodus/00_My Notes/Pantheon/Selûne|Selûne]] and [[Shar]], and how Shar's followers had once kidnapped priests to stop the [[The Exodus/00_My Notes/Lore/Feast of the Moon|Feast of the Moon]]. Petra had heard that a high priest was taken in Crystal City on Feast night. Odette asked whether tiefling priests exist. Petra thought so, though she knew of none in Coldford, and none had gone missing.

Kai said the harbour talk fitted in. There were rumours of an extra bell rung after midnight, and of whole boats going missing after it. [[Dagny Rusk|Dagny]] thought it a prank. Petra did not like that at all.

Ruairí named the building's quarter, the Backrows, which lies behind the Tallyrows and away from the harbour. Petra said even the city guard does not walk there. Then she laid out what they were looking at: a lieutenant's order from Crystal City, the man in the nice hat, the extra bell, someone in the Tallyrows who was working with the Claret, someone tied to the bell codes who was doing the same, and whatever Lord Colwyn was doing. She had one sending stone to Crystal City, good for about twenty-five words a day. She could not guard every tiefling in Coldford, and said so.

The three counted their angles: the building, the man in the hat, Sister Margaret and a hiding place for tieflings, the Aerie and the harbour bells, the tiefling lanes, the Guild itself, and the stalls and counters behind the Tallyrows. The days ahead were counted in slots, morning and afternoon, with a night slot for anyone who wanted it and the extra risk it carried. A week in Coldford is ten days, so their pay was about three days off.

## Morning at the Pier

Kai went back to Dagny on the eighth morning. She was down the row of bells, ringing them in practice although no ship was due. She taught him how the piers talk to each other: when a ship is coming in, a pier signals the others at once, and each pier has its own come-in code. The hold-off warning, which turns a pilot away when the tide or the water is bad, was the one he learned that day, along with the night set for the first sixty feet. She described the emergency codes too: if a ship ignores the piers and keeps coming, boats go out to block it. Kai learned that a pilot has to know about seven or eight come-in codes, and that day bells are simpler, since even in thick fog a boat can be seen for the first sixty feet. Night bells are a second set of codes, and at night boats are guided in from about two hundred feet out. The most important job, she told him, comes if a ship arrives that knows none of the codes: Kai must signal every pier at once.

A pier nobody knew about would need its own variation of every code, especially come-in and hold-off. Whether there was an emergency code for a false pier was left open. She asked Kai whether he was sure the odd night bell was not a prank. He did not think so, and she said they would need evidence, or one of them would have to hear it. Kai left with perhaps ten to fifteen percent more of the codes than he had come with.

## What Marl Forgot

Ruairí took a morning at the Tallyrows and read the room first, with Guidance behind it. He knew the harbour's emergency piers, where boats about to collide are sent, serve the fishermen and the coast guard and handle no cargo. The man in the hat had come again that morning, with the same easy manner as the day before. Ruairí watched [[Marl Dunning]]'s stall, which sits near the far end of a row of about twenty counters, as far from [[Hobb Abbe|Hobb]]'s platform as it can be. Marl's daughter was on the second stool.

Ruairí went over to say hello. Marl was wary of the brass on his wrist, and when Ruairí asked about the fancy gentleman who kept stopping by, the old man stiffened. He said only that this was a port town and only merchants came by. Ruairí saw that he was lying. He pushed on, said he was new and wanted work, and rolled a natural 20.

Marl looked at his daughter and told him the rest. His wife had died, and she had taught the girl to read. He had met the man in the hat only in the past week, never learned his name, and did not like the way the man looked at his daughter. The man's papers were in order, and he sometimes came with goods and sometimes without. Marl had been losing pieces of his memory, and the red chalk check marks kept coming back on one leg of his counter after each visit, though he rubbed them off. "A job's a job," he said, and he did what his House told him. He would pay any price to get her out of this.

Ruairí offered to find her a place at the Tallhouse, though that would need Petra's say. He asked Marl to let him know whenever the man came by. Then he asked the girl whether she remembered everyone who stopped at the stall, and Marl broke in to say that his daughter was to be left out of it.

The girl answered anyway. Whenever the man in the hat handed over a paper and told her father to mark off the goods, Marl marked them without checking. She said he sometimes forgot to call the count. Marl, who never forgot to call a count, was furious. Ruairí cast Detect Magic and rolled a 22. He heard the answer in his head, in his patron's mischievous voice. Memory had been altered in that place, and not just once, and suggestion had been laid on it too. His own read of the stall had put the magic there at two or three days old, which lined up with the man in the hat arriving only a few days before. Ruairí decided not to tell Marl what he had found.

It was settled that the girl would stay at the stall through the busy morning and come to the Tallhouse in the afternoon. Her name is Deffra.

## The Man in the Hat, Described

Odette had gone to [[Hester Crane]]'s bakery and tried to talk her into letting her carry bread to House Colwyn. Hester said no, kindly. Odette was already doing good work at the ovens, she said, and did not need to be a delivery girl.

After the baking, Odette met [[Tamsin Vell]] over the leftover loaves meant for her guardsmen and asked her about a man in a really fancy hat. Tamsin knew him at once. He stuck out, she said, and he moved like a shadow. He was a regular around the Tallyrows and the back roads, and on the Manifest House side, near a pub called the Old Bearded Man. She had seen him the morning before, talking to Marl. He was slender and clean-shaven, tanned, with deep, bright violet eyes, and he always walked alone. Tamsin did not find him suspicious.

It struck Odette as strange that a guard could be so untroubled by him. She cast Detect Magic and found a residual shimmer on Tamsin, left by an enchantment that was not aimed at her. It felt like something that encouraged people to think everything was fine.

Odette's mornings also included time with Sister Margaret, who had been giving her reading. Selûne's voice has not been heard in about two hundred years, Margaret said, any more than any other god's, but her followers feel her strongly at every full moon. This one had not passed yet, though it soon would. In the waxing, she said, her scriptures call for vigilance, because even a little shadow has influence and the faithful must stay alert in the dark even when the moon is bright.

## A Girl Who Reads

At the Tallhouse that afternoon, the three told one another what they had found, and Ruairí brought Deffra along. Petra took one look at her and was already showing her the papers she goes through all the time. Deffra stared at the Guild branch leader like a girl at a hero. The pile of papers would be a lot smaller by evening. Odette said she was nearly the same age and offered to tutor her. Ruairí was glad to hand that over, since he was not good with kids.

## The Temple

Before they left, the table went through Ruairí's features. His Arcane Artist feat, taken at level 4, lets him give an ally Heroic Inspiration once a day when he casts an Illusion spell, and Minor Illusion counts. It works in the way a bard's inspiration does: a small delight that reminds someone how much good they have done. Ruairí made a nice-looking rat run past Odette. As it faded she remembered the last few days and took the inspiration.

Kai was exhausted and in no mood to visit a temple about it. Odette went at him with every argument she had, including that the people who had seen the scene in the street knew her as his wife and she would look odd walking into the temple alone. He gave in. The three went to the [[Temple of Selûne]], where Sister Margaret was meditating before the statue of the goddess. She put her hand on Kai's brow. Cool air moved through his head, and he felt, for a moment, understood. The exhaustion melted away.

Over cups of night-lily water they told her the Claret was about to move on the tieflings. Margaret said she probably could send out the Moon Dancers, and that she could hide people in a temple district too crowded with temples for the Claret to work openly. But she had too few people to reach every tiefling. Odette suggested starting with the tieflings who lived alone, worked alone or walked at night, and Margaret agreed. Tiefling clerics were safe enough, she said. Sister Sildrell, who goes out to help the harbour workers at night, is known to everyone.

Margaret pointed Odette to the washerwomen by the bakery, who love to talk and would spread a warning fast. She set aside the [[Azure Tamers]], whose Aerie is eleven stories up. Ruairí then told her about the man who was changing people's memories. She had not heard of it. She asked to have Marl brought to her quickly, because memory can set, and undoing it would take greater restoration, a fifth-level spell. Ruairí got the sense that she thought she could do something about it, but that the timeline was very important. Ruairí could spend a night slot looking for him.

## A Window and the Moon

That evening Deffra had gone home and Petra was whistling. Odette went to a window where she could see the full moon, sat cross-legged on the sill and cast Augury. It cost twenty-five gold. She asked whether going out to find the man in the fancy hat, and flirting with him, would go well. A cool breeze came through the shut window, like a hug. Odette meant no harm to anyone by the asking, and the goddess shone her light on it as much as she could. The omen was both good and bad, and the good was stronger.

## Into the Backrows

For the night they chose the Old Bearded Man. The plan leaned on invisibility, a silent whistle for signals and a minor illusion to change Odette's face, because the lookouts around the tower had seen her with a basket of bread and a husband. Kai would scout the pub from the ground first and then climb, under Ruairí's invisibility, while Ruairí walked the area as a lookout and Odette would be the social cover. Everyone would dress to suit the Manifest House side. Ruairí made an Investigation check and rolled a 22.

The session ended on the night of the eighth, with the plan made and the pub still ahead of them.
