# Session 07—Danger Behind Pretty Eyes

## The Plan at the Tallhouse

The common room of the [[The Tallhouse|Tallhouse]] smelled of lamp oil and wet wool, and the contracts board by the door still rattled in the wind. Nobody had taken a contract down since the three of them arrived. [[Deffra]]'s stool behind the ledger desk stood empty. [[Petra Hale]] was at the shuttered window with a mug, looking out at fog the colour of a bruise, and far off the tally bells of the [[Tallyrows]] were still counting.

Tonight was the night for the [[Old Bearded Man]], and Petra wanted the plan. [[Odette Langford]] and [[Ruairí]] would both be in the pub, Odette going in first as the social cover, while [[Kai]] scouted from the dark. Petra pointed out the weak spot. Most of the Tallyrows people knew Ruairí, but the lookouts around the [[Backrows]] had seen Odette's face and Kai's on the day of the bread and the black cat. They did not know whether the pair were friends or enemies, only that two strangers had walked in looking for a cat and trying to give out bread.

Ruairí's answer was Invisibility on Kai, good for an hour, and a Minor Illusion over Odette's face. He would have to stay near Odette to keep the illusion going, and he would cast it on her right before she went in, outside. Petra asked who carried her sending stone. The three agreed it should be one central point of contact. Odette, who would be busy talking, said Ruairí should hold it, so that he could call Petra back up if anything happened to her. Kai set his whistle signals: one blow meant he had found the man, two meant he was retreating or had found nothing, three meant he needed help. They also talked about [[Marl Dunning]], whom [[Sister Margaret]] needed brought to her. If they happened to see him they might snatch him up, but it could wait until morning, since they still had about a day.

The Tallhouse door opened onto cold air, wet slate and the smell of fish. The fog sat so low that lantern light carried perhaps ten feet. A bell rang once somewhere to the east, an ordinary pilot's bell and not the strange one. [[Dagny Rusk|Dagny]] was practising. The Augury Odette had cast on the eighth still held for tonight: a good omen and a bad one, the good stronger.

## Through the Fog

The three cut across the city. Ruairí's own map of Coldford let him find the large open streets, and they weighed the choice aloud. More people in a quiet town meant less notice, while the back lanes were harder to be seen in but easier to be noticed in. They settled on cutting across the open way. A group Survival check came out at 18.

They met a lamplighter on a short ladder, a stooped man in his sixties in a coat with neat patches, feeding oil to a lamp through a long spout. The fog around the lamp turned the colour of weak tea as the wick caught. He wore a short sword on his left hip and a bundle of daggers on his belt, and he was courteous. The three said they were out for a walk to see the view. He told them to walk beneath his lamps and follow the line of them, and added that on nights when the fog settles this low, people disappear.

Before the open square Kai split off. Ruairí cast Invisibility on him, and Kai went on alone with advantage on his Stealth. Odette wrapped herself in a blanket over her good dress and played a mysterious lady of some distinction, rolling a 15 for it. As they crossed the central square, some of the guards recognised the pair as Guild people and greeted them. At the crossing beyond it, the unlit lanes of the Backrows began on the left, while the lamps ran on toward the Tallyrows. Ruairí used Minor Illusion to give her a plain, serious face, and Odette chose the lit way. Further on, as storekeepers packed up and greeted Ruairí, the street split again. Left lay the Old Bearded Man, and straight on lay the Tallyrows.

Ruairí would stay within thirty feet of her, and Odette could drop the illusion once she was through the door. The lookouts' recognition mattered on the street, and the odds were that none of them drank inside.

## Kai Alone

Kai ran down the dark lanes unseen and saw Dagny at the end of them, testing every bell. She did not see him. He cut across to the harbour side of the city. The harbour was dry, with boats leaning on wooden cradles along a steep slope and the fish market on the quay shut for the night. A chain hung beside the fifth ferry step, and below the slope lay a drop with no bottom, so that anyone who fell would fall off the side of the continent. He heard a wrench turning somewhere along a pier and found a weathered man in an oilcloth cap tightening bolts by lantern light. Kai did not know him.

The Tallyrows were shut for the night. [[Hobb Abbe|Hobb]] had covered his platform with a tarpaulin, and only a couple of people were still packing up. At Marl's stall Kai found a red check mark on the table. Marl always rubbed it off when he saw it, so he had either missed it or already gone home. Kai's Survival check came out at 20, and he picked out one set of very good boots among the cheaper prints of the workers. The boots began in front of Marl's stall and turned away from the side street, then followed the lamps to a cross junction and turned right, toward the Old Bearded Man. They were lost among the fine shoes at the pub door. Kai had used about thirty-five to forty minutes of his hour.

## The Old Bearded Man

Ruairí said hello to the Tallyrows storekeepers as they packed up, and told them he was off to try a pub he had heard about. Odette went in first. The sign over the door showed an old man whose beard was cotton wool pasted to the board and trailing down the post, swinging in the wind. The door knob was warm.

Inside, the long room was hot and loud and golden. A fiddler was losing an argument with the dice table by the door. The bar ran down the right wall, with a cracked mirror behind it that showed the whole room twice. Benches lined the front windows, an alcove with curtains lay to the left, and at the back a half door stood ajar on a narrow stair. Under the turn of the stair, in the far corner, one table sat empty with a lit lamp, a single chair and a fresh cup on the table. Every other table was taken.

The bartender was not what Odette had expected. He was very tall and lanky, dark-skinned, with pointed ears and violet eyes. He told her the local specialty was rum, seven or eight kinds of it, and that there was ale and lager but no wine. She asked for his best, and he poured a clear rum into a short glass and put it on her tab. A green-skinned bouncer with two tusks stood in the corner in good clothes, with brass knuckles and a club. Odette had taken off her Guild bracer.

Ruairí came in a little later, and a group of foremen from the Tallyrows spotted him at once. "Look who it is," they said, "the hero of the Tallyrows. Without you, Hobb couldn't see or hear anything." They dragged him over to a table and pressed mugs on him. His Constitution save came out at 10, and he barely held it in.

Odette, meanwhile, made a mistake at the bar. When the bartender asked whom she was waiting for, she described the man: fancy dress, a rugged tan, a handsome fellow, beautiful violet eyes. The bartender's whole manner changed. He asked what she wanted with a man so specific. She cast Enhance Ability and talked her way back with a Deception check above 25. He relaxed and spoke with almost a look of concern. Young city girls always went looking for the next handsome face, he said, but some men carry a little danger behind them, and danger sometimes hides behind pretty eyes. "It's your funeral," he told her.

## The Man in the Good Hat

The door swung open fast, and the man came in: about six feet tall, tanned, with violet eyes and a very nice hat. Two men flanked him, one carrying a bow and one a crossbow. They sat down by the door near the windows, and he went to the back table where the fresh cup waited. A barmaid swapped the cup every five minutes, and this was perhaps the fifth or sixth change. The mulled wine steamed, heated back in the kitchen. Neither Ruairí nor Odette recognised his two men.

Kai reached the front of the pub about a quarter of an hour after the man had gone in. He could not tell who was inside, so he blew his whistle twice, the signal that he had found nothing, and went looking for the places where the Claret might keep the people it took. Kai found a house with cellar doors in front of it, one slightly ajar, and nobody was guarding them. Kai went in and found a cellar with a bolted room and manacles and chains fitted to the walls. While he was inside, the front door of that house opened and two men wearing a single stud each came out and headed for the harbour. His invisibility ended just after they passed him. He tracked them for ten or fifteen minutes. They walked down to the dry harbour, looked left and right, and went out beyond the city wall and further on. Kai chose not to follow. On the way back he found a second house, far more obviously guarded: two archers on the roof, guards at the front of the house and at the end of the street by a gate that never opened, and a very dirty house behind them. As he watched, a man came out carrying two heavy bags that jingled, put them in another man's hands, and went back inside while the other walked away.

At the bar, Odette had caught the man's eye across the room. He smiled, tipped his head, and raised his cup. She raised her glass in return. A natural 20 told her he would not mind her joining him. She went over with her drink. He looked at her Selûne pendant and asked, in a gruff voice, what a pretty sister of Selûne was doing in a place like this. He told her to sit. Two men at the door stood up and began to walk over. He asked whether she was new here, and she answered that she was, kind of, and that it was refreshing to see someone dress so well. He ordered the waitress to bring something from his own stash, and she came back with a warm mug of good mead instead of mulled wine.

He called himself a businessman from Crystal City who handled some of the side businesses in the area, and said it was a group effort. Odette said she was a sister of Selûne from Crystal City, that her family worked in medicine and herbs, and that she wanted to explore other medicines. She said she believed in Selûne and wanted to bring her light to a foggy place. He traded in goods, he said, because Coldford was well known for its Manifest Houses. He came to load up on stock, maybe stayed a couple of weeks, and went home as the goods flowed. She asked what sort of goods he handled and rolled a 17 on her Persuasion check. He loved the rarest sort of things, he said, spell components for the Academy, gems, and he was thinking of taking mulled wine and mead back home. Her family, she said, wanted to start importing herbs into Coldford, and she asked whom to contact at the harbour. A natural 20 earned her his full name, Quentin Ashby, and Odette did not see the two studs on his ears. He leaned forward and said he could be of much assistance. Was this for her family, he asked, or for the church? She said her family supplied the herbs, but this was her own side business, which would also help Selûne's work. "So you are a cleric and an entrepreneur," he said, and Odette said the temple needed funding. He told her the name of his inn, around the corner and not in the Backrows, and said to send word to the porter there. She suggested meeting again at the pub, and he said it was noisy. She offered his place instead, and he said he would bring out the best in the house if she did. Tonight, unfortunately, was not possible: he would head back to his warehouse interests after dinner and turn in early.

Ruairí, in the corner with his mug, cast Message to Odette and asked whether the man had anyone else working with him. She asked Quentin whether he had business acquaintances in the city, and whether he had a wife. He said he might have one in every city, and laughed. He had come alone with two armed men, he said, men at arms, since people like him were large targets. He had warehouses in the city and knew some of the Manifest Houses, though he would not hand over his contacts for nothing. Most of them knew him, at least by reputation, and when he said so he wore a sardonic grin. Odette's Insight came out at 18, and she read it as the grin of a man the Houses step around. He said there was one House that may deal in herbs and maybe some spices from Sunhold, and he might put her in touch, but he was busy, so she should leave a message at the inn and he would come by. He said the next couple of days might not be good, and that maybe they could meet in two days.

Odette agreed to meet in two days. She walked around behind him and tried to kiss him on the cheek. He flinched. His left hand went inside his coat, and then he saw what she was doing and slowly let go. It was the flinch of a man trained to defend himself, an automatic reflex. "My lady," he said, "have a great night."

## The Door

Odette now had to walk out past the two men by the door. She had already clocked the crossbow man. He was the one who had pointed at two of the party from the rooftops, and he would know her if she was careless. She thought of sleight of hand and of casting a spell, but the other guard was watching so closely that anything sneaky would be very hard, and everyone in the room avoided the pair, so there was no drunk to hide behind. She met the crossbow man's eye, turned around fast and went to the toilet instead. Ruairí, who had not seen the two men at the door, rolled an Insight of 19 and guessed there was a problem with the crossbow man.

Ruairí's plan was to be a drunk. He told the Tallyrows workers at his table that he was from a small village, that he had moved around a lot, and that in Coldford people looked at him funny. He asked whether the two men at the door had anything against him. His Deception check convinced them, and the whole table stood up. The crossbow man laid his crossbow across his lap while four or five angry workers crowded in front of him. Ruairí pretended to pass out, and sent Odette a Message telling her to get out.

In the toilet, Odette realised what she had pulled off. She had won over a man like that, and the thrill of it carried her to level 4. She stepped out into a room full of men watching the confrontation, and nobody had eyes on her. She walked out.

Ruairí got up to join the quarrel, wanting to be hit and thrown out. The crossbow man asked who he was looking at funny, a first punch landed and the fight began. Four hits landed on Ruairí for 19 damage, and a Performance check of 16 sold the knockout. The man in the hat did not stand. He sat at his table and seemed untroubled. Ruairí and his drinking companions were thrown out of the pub, about six people and one black cat, and the two guards stayed inside with their boss. The three met outside and walked back to the Tallhouse, where Petra looked at Ruairí's bloody face and said she did not want to know, only that all three were back safe.

## Back at the Tallhouse

At the debrief Odette gave Petra the name Quentin Ashby. Petra did not know it. She said she would check with her Crystal City contacts, and that there were about seven Manifest Houses, the main power in the city, each dealing in different things. She would do some research the next day. Odette said she had a meeting arranged with him at his place in two days. Petra told her to be careful and to have a way out, and when Odette asked for a second sending stone, Petra said such stones were very rare. Put beside what Ruairí had overheard of the hunt starting within a couple of days, Quentin's busy days suggested the man would try something soon.

Kai gave his report: the cellar house with its manacles, where it seemed people might be held, the money house, the lookouts' posts, and the pair who left the city by the harbour edge.

Then everyone took a long rest, and the spell slots and hit points came back.

## The Morning of the Ninth

The morning was for planning, and the day had many loose ends. Deffra would come to the Tallhouse around lunch every day, and in the mornings she was with her father. Ruairí had asked her to listen in and remember what passed between her father and the man in the hat. Odette feared they were on a clock with Marl's memory, since nobody could tell how often Modify Memory had been cast on him, and the answer she got was that, left uncured for too long, the loss becomes permanent. Ruairí, who had worked the Tallyrows for a few days, knew the man in the hat had come by Marl's stall in the morning, and thought of waiting for him to pass before bringing Marl to the temple.

Margaret had said she would start that day looking for the more isolated tieflings in the city, and Odette could help with her own network of the bakery, the washerwomen and the aunties. Kai thought about going back to the Aerie to ask [[Torun]] for help, and about taking one of the big birds there. Ruairí considered Find Familiar for scouting, and a changeling's copy of a guard, or of Quentin himself. The question left on the table was whether the three should keep working alone as the stakes rose.
