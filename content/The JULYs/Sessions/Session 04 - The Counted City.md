# Session 04—The Counted City

## Yielded

The yard went quiet. [[Petra Hale]]'s boots scraped as she stood up out of her fighting stance and lowered the javelin. She was bleeding from the crossbow bolts, and she had let them press her for about half the fight. Every one of them, she said, had earned their braces. She admitted she should have listened to her colleague [[Rhona Voss]] up in [[Stormberg]], who had told her Rifted were stronger than they looked and had run this same test on a party of five.

Then her manner changed. She had been watching what the three of them carried and how it worked. Even when the trinkets sat unused, their spells hit harder than they should have. Inside her pocket dimension, she told them, nobody else could get at them. Outside it, people would kill for such things. She wasn't sure she could take them away, and she wouldn't risk trying. She asked them not to show them. She would tell everyone in the city that the three were Rifted, and hope the Guild's name was enough to keep hands off them. She couldn't promise it would be.

## The Common Room

She led them back through the portal, where the cold brushed past their faces like water, into the common room. She set out ale, mugs and a huge roast that looked like lamb, then sat down to drink with a bleeding face. The beer was watered down, and she apologised for it. Not many of them had seen that much meat in a long while.

When someone asked, the date was the first of Nightal: the day after the Moon Festival, and the first day of the last month of the year. Snow had started outside the window, in big clumps like cotton wool. Landfall, Petra said, should arrive within ten days or so. If they saw people cheering in the streets for no reason, they should grab something, quickly. The Guild had promised to take in every Rifted on the continent, as long as none of them broke the law.

## What Petra Knew About Layla

The talk turned to [[Layla]]. One of the three called her a stressed unpaid intern. Petra knew the name only as the assistant to a member of the Council in Crystal City, and she explained the rest as if to newcomers. [[Corranor]] is ruled by the Council of Seven, a council of archmages. One of them, [[Garm]], is close to the Guild and sponsors it. He had two assistants, [[Perrin Quickquill|Perrin]] and Layla. He had wanted to find a way to teleport people from city to city, but nothing in this world teleports farther than thirty to sixty feet, so news moves at the speed of carts and flying creatures. Garm had set the two assistants to study it. On the day the first Rifted appeared, Layla accidentally sent nine people at once into the Spire in Crystal City, in Petra's telling, and then vanished. Rifted had been turning up in pockets ever since, and a manhunt was on for Layla.

Petra drew out a sending stone, good for one use a day. Artificers are not easy to find in Corranor, she said, and she promised to give the three one later. Rifted across the continent were investigating, and the Guild and the military were both hunting Layla. The three were the only Rifted in [[The Exodus/00_My Notes/Locations/Aenath/Corranor/Coldford|Coldford]]. She wished they were somewhere easier, she said, but perhaps the gods had meant them to be here.

She laid out what she could give them: a room, the Guild's protection, and a silver a day, a gold at the end of the week, with the Guild coming first. The brass bracers would make most people think twice, unless the wearer looked easy to take down. She could introduce them to anyone else in the city they wanted to meet, and she urged them to settle into Coldford as fast as they could. In the morning she would send word to Lord Colwyn, who would pass it down to the [[The Manifest Houses|Manifest Houses]].

The Manifest Houses, she said, are the true power in the city, the merchant houses that control all trade. The fog suits discretion, and it keeps fish and meat from spoiling. For reasons the wizards haven't worked out, it also keeps things dry. Coldford's common folk are good folk who learned the hard way to mind their own business.

## The First Night

Each of them had a room in the dimension and spent the night in it. Kai heard a knock and went to the door with a dagger in his hand. He opened it a crack. It was Petra, and the caution didn't seem to bother her. She handed him a rapier. One of his party, she told him, needed more protection than a crossbow could give from across the room, and he would have to learn to get closer.

## The First Morning

In the morning a floating table stood among their doors with floating chairs. A drink poured out of whatever liquid you thought of, a platter gave whatever food you pictured, and nothing from it could leave the dimension. Petra was out in the common room, and the job board out front was open to them. She told them to keep their glamour on as much as they could, and to drop it for no one they didn't trust.

One of them asked how news reaches Coldford first. Petra said the western roads, the ones they had come in by, lead down to Crystal City, the capital, and the northern roads lead up to Stormberg. Westward was too far away to count. From the window she pointed at a tall plastered tower near the gates: the Aerie of the [[Azure Tamers]], rangers and druids who raise griffins and giant eagles and fly people between cities for a price.

Odette asked where a person might go to unwind. The Old Streets, Petra said: shops, a court, a wash court, and the seat of House Colwyn, all close to the Tallhouse. The Guild's crest, she explained, is a scroll for Oghma, a gauntlet for Helm and a coin for Waukeen. Everyone has the right to grow, to be safe, and to learn. The Guild were not the militia or the city's soldiers. They were nice mercenaries, paid for their work and sponsored by the Council, and some days that meant killing the rats in an old woman's house. Most of her people were out at sea, which was why she was alone with her paperwork. The ranks run brass, iron, mithril and adamantine, and her own gauntlet was silver mithril.

Kai slipped out early. Petra noticed and let him go.

## The First Days

Kai went up to the high place he had seen from the window. The city walls turned out to be nearly useless, because the fog does the defending. Inside the Aerie's tower there was no fog at all. He climbed eleven stories of ladders and platforms and came out on a plank platform slick with rain and loose feathers. A griffin the size of a horse sat on the perch. It shifted its weight, looked him over once, and went back to preening. Coldford lay open below him like a wet ledger, grey slate roofs and chimney smoke blending into the fog. Past the city to the east, the harbour was dry. Boats sat on a slope with no water under them, and beyond the slope was miles of open air. The boats rested on land. The three of them were on a continent floating thousands of feet above the sea.

An archer in leathers who smelled of griffin said hello. His name was [[Torun]]. He explained the bells: each pier has one, and when a ship is coming in only that pier's bell may ring, so ships can steer by sound through the fog. Torun pointed out the three districts, the harbour, the Tallyrows and the Old Streets. Among the poor lanes of the Old Streets stood a walled, well-built castle with no name on it. The Aerie keeps one bird in the sky at a time, he said, in case a hungry sea monster comes in at landfall, and the fishing boats are the city's lifeline. The Manifest Houses run the place. Even Lord Colwyn, a nice man and almost useless, doesn't command as many soldiers as some of them. The griffin was a greater griffin named Tiffy. Higher up, on the top landing, sat a huge old eagle named Bertha, partly blind, brooding her eggs. If Petra sent word, Torun said, he would gladly take Kai on.

Odette took the Old Streets one lane at a time. The tally-bells carried up from the docks, thin and steady, and the lanes narrowed into wet stone and washing lines. She moved slowly, a word at the pump, a compliment at a doorstep. By the time the fog went pale, people were saying good morning to her first, and some took off their hats at the bracer on her arm. At the Wash Court she found the gate guard she had flirted away from Ruairí, washing his leather armour, and beside him a broad woman in the uniform of the Landgate with rank cords at her belt, drinking tea and counting jerkins aloud as he worked. That was [[Tamsin Vell]]. They knew the new guildies from the night before. Tamsin told Odette not to look so rich in Coldford and sent the gate guard running to Emery's for plain wool clothes, with the bill going to Petra. The wool, when it came, was ugly and warm, and Odette felt fewer eyes on her.

Odette asked about the nobles. Lord Colwyn pays Tamsin's wages, but he has little power; every Manifest House holds more. The Houses grow rich stockpiling food, she said, and they are not above a little smuggling of spell reagents and rare ores. Some of the ores were meant only for the Council, such as the flight stones that keep airships aloft. By law those stones belong to the Council or to one great house, the only two airships on the continent. Anyone who found a stone and kept it became a smuggler. Odette pressed on, and Tamsin stopped her. She was asking sensitive questions in a very open place, and four or five pairs of eyes had turned. If Odette saw anyone with a ruby earring, Tamsin said, she should stay away. Two studs, run. Three, leave the city. She would come by the Tallhouse later in the week to sit down with Petra and talk it through. She also pointed Odette to the [[Temple of Selûne]] in the faith district next door, where [[Sister Margaret]] would be.

Tamsin came by the Tallhouse that week, as she had said, and sat down with Petra and the party. She gave them one word: the Claret. It is the biggest criminal organisation on the continent, and its people wear red garnet studs: one for a low-level member, two for a sergeant, three for a lieutenant. They are vicious and keep a thieves' honour. Coldford is where the Claret makes its money, Tamsin said, so they don't trouble the people there. Petra and Tamsin argued the way old friends do, about smuggling and human trafficking, and Odette heard that the Claret had lately been trafficking magic reagents.

Ruairí wanted a map of his own, marked with the streets to avoid. He asked Petra and Tamsin everything they knew about the city and drew it out on paper and ink Petra gave him. They filled in the little streets the Guild's wall map missed. The map came out well. With it in his hand he could tell which places were safe to walk by night and by day, and he and Kai could cross the city faster. Petra pointed to the board out front, mostly escort and freight work for the Manifest Houses, with the usual advice: don't walk alone after dark, and don't take a stranger's kindness at face value. The gate hours came up too. After a certain hour, anyone outside the walls isn't let back in. There was work for a caster in the city, she and Tamsin said, but that could wait.

## The Middle Days

Kai went down to the harbour. At Fogbell Pier, the Gull Steps fish market and the ferry stairs, the stairs ran down into dust and sand, and past the third bollard the quay simply stopped. Further down lay the slip yard, where boats are repaired, and the Slack Room, where the dock workers drink. The dock workers watched a painted line on the stones to know where they were. Kai listened. Cats had gone missing, in a fish market where there should have been plenty. A drunk in the Slack Room told of a bell that rings in the night with a pitch no pilot's bell has. The first time someone answered it by accident, four crew vanished, and search boats and the Guild found nothing. And a buoy chain hung at a strange angle by the fifth ferry step, snagged on something below, so that the pier was nearly unusable and people would pay to have it freed.

A lean woman in her sixties with a leather guard strapped on was working down the row of bells, tightening them and hammering out dents. Her name was [[Dagny Rusk|Dagny]]. She was a bell pilot, one of the few who know every code that guides a ship to its pier. She thought the odd bell was a prank, some kid or punk, and didn't believe the stories about the lost boat, though she admitted the bell rang only once every few weeks. If Kai was so interested, she said, she could teach him to be a bell pilot.

Odette got up ten minutes before the dawn bell to work at the bakery one lane below the Lamp Stair. The baker was [[Hester Crane]], in her forties, with flour in the creases of her knuckles. She kept time by the tally-bells ringing at the docks. "You're cutting it close, my dear," she said. Odette learned to fold butter into dough for the city's version of the croissant. Hester was behind on the morning loaves, and the Wash Court notices when bread runs late. There are no clocks in Coldford, only bells, and a person who hears the dawn bell is already late. The pay was about two silver a day if the loaves were out on time, one if Odette came late, and nothing if the bread was already out. By the end she could shape, fold and time a loaf, but Hester's real craft, how much butter to use, still escaped her. The work put her within reach of the steward of House Colwyn, and bakers know the people at the Widow's Orchard, where apples come from.

Ruairí was offered two jobs for a caster. Petra recommended the bell-keeper [[Hobb Abbe]], and Tamsin recommended Edric Prewitt, House Colwyn's local steward. Hobb rings the tally-bells for a whole run of desks from a raised platform in the Tallyrows. Edric works from two rooms above a candle shop and collects every petition from the Old Streets. He tells everyone the House is informed, and nothing happens after that. Ruairí chose the Manifest Houses, because the man nominally in charge stood on shaky ground. On the platform he met Hobb, a man of about eighty, deaf in one ear, with a right arm bigger than Ruairí's thigh. Hobb counted tallies under his breath and wrote them down with his left hand, and when someone called from the far end of the row he lost his place. Ruairí cast Faerie Fire along the row so Hobb could see through the fog, and used Message to pass count changes down the line. Hobb still would not hand over the mallet. He paid about a silver a day, made chowder at the end of it, and gave Ruairí a seat on the platform with one of the best views of the Tallyrows. The Manifest Houses noticed. They wondered who the black cat was and why Hobb had let him help.

## The Sixth Morning

On the morning of the sixth day Petra gathered them in the common room with a serious face. The Rifted in the capital had left and were heading north to Stormberg, she said, and had found a couple of leads.

She asked how much they knew about the planes. The Material Plane is where they stood. Next door are the Feywild and the Shadowfell, easy to slip into and out of. The Ethereal Plane surrounds them, where the elements are, she said. Beyond that lie the outer planes, the celestial ones on one side and the demonic ones on the other, among them the Nine Hells, the Abyss, Celestia and Arborea, where the gods are said to hold court. But the gods don't speak, and nobody knows why.

A colleague at the Academy in Crystal City had told her that teleportation means punching a hole through reality, which needs access to the outer planes. No one has walked the planes, she said, though a few people have dropped by accident into the Feywild or the Shadowfell. The theory was that you would need something from an outer plane, and the easiest way to get it was blood from a creature with a connection to one: tieflings and aasimar. They are people, she said, not evil because of their blood and not good, just people. And someone was buying up a great deal of tiefling blood through the Claret. People were going missing. She prayed to [[The Exodus/00_My Notes/Pantheon/Selûne|Selûne]] that those taken were not suffering.

The Tallhouse is shielded, so no one outside can hear, and Petra spoke freely in it. Layla, or whoever Layla worked for, was trying to buy up as much blood as she could, and every Rifted on the continent was trying to track the shipments. Coldford, with its smugglers, was either the best place to be or the worst. Petra asked the three to warn any tieflings or aasimar they knew and to watch for illegal spell reagents, since "spell reagents" can mean almost anything and can cover blood. She said the Fey had slipped to second priority in the city, and that she had been hearing good things about the three from Dagny, from the bakery, and from Hobb. She still wondered about Layla, who had been a very stressed-out person. Something didn't tally. Was she really evil, or was something else going on?

Two of the three tried to read what they could from it. One felt a memory stir. Petra was closer to the truth than she knew: a barrier must block the outer planes, which would explain why no god answers. The way the Rifted had been brought here looked like plane shift, which needs an item from another plane, and someone had found a way past the barrier, past the gods who guard the outer planes, to send them here. The blood was probably part of it, and probably the life force of whoever it came from was part of the spell. They couldn't tell how much blood it took, or whether the donor lived. They also realised the shadow creatures had zeroed in on them the moment they arrived, which suggests teleportation can be tracked. The Temple of Selûne might know something about that.

## The Last Days

As the days in Coldford went on, each of them weighed what to do with what they knew.

Kai's search of the harbour had left him suspicious of the strange bell. It could be pulling ships toward a pier that wasn't even in the city. Odette worked out that Coldford has no representative of the Council and no Temple of Tyr, so no judge and no paladin. Something unnamed was keeping the peace in a city that should have been cutting throats. Ruairí, watching Hobb's tally row through Faerie Fire, saw some clerks quietly let a tally pass and usher the next person in instead of signalling Hobb. After six days of watching, one or two studs had been spotted around the city, and none on anyone above the lowest rank.

Kai spent his last stretch memorising the bell-pilot codes. It was hard work. He learned every code, but it took him so long that he earned nothing, and it left him worn through. If he took bell-pilot work when landfall came, it would be harder than it was now.

Odette brought bread to the Temple of Selûne. The moment she walked in, her body relaxed. Sister Margaret, a very tall woman with elven and celestial blood, a hundred and thirty years old at least, stood near the goddess's statue. She saw the bracer and asked after Petra, whom she hadn't seen in weeks. Odette said Petra was a little stressed out and could use a visit. They talked about the goddess. Selûne waxes full at the Feast of the Moon, Sister Margaret said, and her ally [[Sehanine Moonbow]], a sister of twilight, sends her own strength to guard her as she waxes. Selûne is light in the dark, and the oldest goddess. In the beginning there were only Selûne and her sister [[Shar]], two sides of the moon. Worshippers of Shar in Crystal City had even kidnapped a few priests at the Feast to try to stop it. The temple has no paladins, only clerics and a sorcerer, because Selûne is also a goddess of magic.

Odette asked about the Temple of Tyr. Its main temple is in Crystal City, Sister Margaret said, and Tyr has temples nearly everywhere else, but Coldford has none, and nobody here seems willing to worship the god of justice. The Temple of [[Kelemvor]] is the most important of the others. Every city has one, because nobody wants to vanish into nothing when they die. Tyr is the god of justice for the living and Kelemvor for the dead, and Coldford has two of his paladins. [[Lathander]] has a temple here too.

A small community of aasimar lives in Coldford, Margaret said, one working for the Azure Tamers and one as a dock worker. Odette told her, in confidence, what Petra had told her about the trade in tiefling and aasimar blood. Margaret made a sign and cast a spell to commune, then looked up. "This is not good," she said. There were a few tieflings in the city as well, and the tiefling community, she sensed, would be hit first. She asked whether Odette had seen ruby studs around, and Odette said she hadn't. Margaret set a Silence over the temple's entrance and windows against eavesdroppers, and stayed outside its reach. She said she would warn Selûne's worshippers who were tieflings or aasimar and spread the word, and told Odette to watch for the Claret, who she felt were starting to move fast. Then she lifted the Silence from the door. Petra would work from the Guild's side, and she would work from the temple's. She would speak to Kelemvor's paladins and the Monks of the Long Death as well, once Petra agreed.

Ruairí chose to watch the Tallyrows for illegal reagents because he wouldn't be alone there: Hobb and the other clerks were close by. From the shadow of an awning at the east end, he watched two small stalls ring their bells early and skip tallies, and figures carry sacks down the street. Late in the morning a man in a good hat stopped at the stall of Marl Dunning, bought and sold nothing, touched his hat brim, said something pleasant, and moved on. Marl looked at his brass scales, put a weight on one, and counted something off his ledger. It happened three times that day. From where he watched, Ruairí could see two studs on the man and not much else.

## Still Unfinished

The days in Coldford weren't over. The three were to pool what they had learned and decide how much to bring to Petra. A man with three studs is never seen outside Crystal City, and anyone who saw one had better expect trouble. Sister Margaret would send word to the tieflings, a much larger community than the aasimar. Kai's insight into the bell had settled: it did not ring in the rhythm of the pilots' bells, and where Dagny thought it a prank, Kai did not. Whoever was behind the trade would learn they were watched if the three moved too loudly, and a frightened ring might scatter before they learned more.
