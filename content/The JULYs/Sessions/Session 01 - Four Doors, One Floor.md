# Session 01 — Four Doors, One Floor

## Four Doors Closing

In Chengdu, [[Kai|Kai]] was three cups of jasmine into a conversation Old Zhou had been building up to for a year — *"Who is it? Old debt? Old family?"* — when the evening picked a worse moment to ask him what he was made of. Two men had a delivery boy backed against a lantern post at the lane's far end, going through the motions of a shakedown that only works on someone too scared to call for help. Kai didn't stay at the mouth of the lane telling himself to keep walking. He went in, deck of cards already in hand, and flicked one hard enough to make a point before he could find out whether it landed. He never got to see if it did. The lantern light over the post went a color that had no business being in that lane.

In Singapore, [[Frankie|Frankie]] stepped out of Dr. Priya's clinic to take a call she'd been dreading all week: her regional manager, Farid, running through the same script about the Toa Payoh clinic and numbers she hadn't hit. She let it ring out or answered it — either way, Farid never found out what he wasn't getting an answer to. She turned to go back in through the clinic's own door, and the door she walked through wasn't the one that let her out.

In Mayfair, [[Odette Langford|Odette Langford]] ducked into the coat check with ninety seconds to spare before the Ambassador's table, ostensibly to check her coat for a spare compact. Rosa, the attendant working the rotation, wasn't fishing for a quote or managing her brand — just making small talk on a slow shift. Odette helped herself to a snack off Rosa's table, one small, unscripted thing nobody had cleared with Naomi first, and that was the last ordinary decision she got to make that night.

In Tokyo, [[Masamune Kento|Masamune Kento]] was alone in the workshop after Endo-san had locked up, turning Fujita-san's pocket watch toward the bench lamp for a better look at a maker's mark his hands recognized before the rest of him could say why. The watch was still warm in his palm when the lamp's light stopped behaving like light.

Four lives, four cities, the same instant. [[Nanaya Rei]] wasn't among them — wherever he was that night, it wasn't here.

## Four, Not Five

They landed together on a slab of black rock the size of a tennis court in [[The Emberveins]], lava threading through the cavern far below and throwing its glow up onto stone arches that curved between platforms like ribs. A woman crouched at the platform's edge, red-gold light catching off her horns, writing fast in a notebook that had seen better decades.

*"Four,"* she said, without looking up. *"Last batch was four."* A beat, and then she did look up. *"You're not dead. Good, means the anchor held. I'm Layla. Whatever this looks like, I didn't do it out of malice."* Whatever question was building on their faces, she got ahead of it: *"Short version, you're not on your world anymore. Neither am I, originally."* She gestured, not quite looking up from the page, toward a far arch where a door-shaped absence in the rock waited. *"Long version's not mine to give, and there's no time for it anyway. Exit's that way. There's transport waiting on the other side, for whoever makes it. I'd move. Something else down here already knows you landed."*

On the shadows already gathering at the edges of the light, she offered exactly one more sentence before going back to her notebook: local wildlife, more or less, and they wanted the newcomers, not her.

## The Pillars

The cavern didn't want a stand-up fight. It wanted feet moving. Three stone pillars rose out of the black rock at intervals across the platforms, each crowned with a crystal, and the shadow creatures poured up out of the dark between them the moment the party moved. Reaching a pillar and setting its crystal alight scattered whatever had gathered near it, opening just enough room to cross, one platform at a time, toward the far side of the cavern.

Nobody made it across untouched. The shadows fell back from a lit crystal only to regroup at the next one, moving with a coordination that read as strategy, and the party learned quickly that outrunning a cluster cost less than standing to trade with it. By the time the third crystal caught, the way to the exit was clear enough to take.

## The Ladder

The last stretch of the cavern narrowed into a rough tunnel, and at its end, a ladder climbed up out of the dark toward a seam of paler light overhead. Whatever had been chasing them fell away behind, the way Layla said it would.

Nobody's climbed it yet. The session ends here, four strangers at the foot of a ladder with no idea what's waiting at the top.
