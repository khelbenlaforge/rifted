# Session 03—Three of Six

## The Vision That Fought Back

[[Ruairí]] lived the Calamity Vision from inside a fey creature at the edge of the same catastrophe: a spire taller than sight itself, ringed by rocks that circled it instead of falling, while everywhere else the sky fell in earnest. A single boulder took hundreds of tiny screaming figures at once. A whole crowd of pixies vanished under another. Taller fey stood near him, calling a name the vision refused to let through no matter how loudly they screamed it. Magic came as naturally to the body he was borrowing as breathing does to his own, and when the next rock bore down on the group in front of him, that instinct answered on its own: an Eldritch Blast, a bolt of dark lightning that went wide. The rock finished the job anyway, crushed the group and the borrowed body both, and the vision ended the way it always ends—someone who couldn't help, reached this time from inside the failure instead of only made to watch it.

He woke into his own skin. No borrowed shape, no disguise pulled on out of habit, just a changeling in the form he was born with, on ground close enough to the others that finding them wouldn't take long.

[[Layla]] found him first. Horns, that particular amber burn behind her eyes, the same weight she always carries. She put a bag in his hands: six brass bracers, each stamped with the [[Adamantine Guild]]'s crest, and a single instruction to get them to the rest. She was gone before he'd finished asking what that meant.

The reunion, when it came, wasn't the first thing that happened to [[Kai]] and [[Odette Langford]]. They'd already found their own abandoned campfire on the road, Odette building snowmen while Kai got the fire properly going, a long rest taken before Ruairí's driver ever caught up to them. When he did, Kai barely looked up, filing the stranger away as someone he'd read properly later. Odette took one look at him, still pale and wraith-thin before the cat shape ever stuck, and went straight for it: "Have you heard of Foundation?" Ruairí's answer came right back: "Have you heard of manners?" Introductions, handled. The driver watched all of it like it explained nothing, the same baffled look he'd been wearing since the three of them first opened their mouths and he heard a language that meant nothing to him and somehow still landed as words.

## The Road to Coldford, and the Tallhouse

They left on the Feast of the Moon, the intercalary day that belongs to no month at all. The carriage was hired, not conjured: an older driver who knew the road well enough to stop narrating it, and his grandson asleep against the far wall for most of the crossing. Kai, Odette Langford, and Ruairí spent the ride in whatever quiet the wheels left them, broken once by the sky itself: past midnight, a spread of pale lights lifted from the southwest and wove themselves across the face of the rising moon until it stood full and covered, then peeled away and sank back out of sight. Odette watched it longer than the other two, unsettled by something about it she didn't try to name.

The driver used the same stretch of road for something more practical: a warning. Cover yourselves before Coldford's gate, keep quiet about what makes you strange, and don't expect the city to be gentle about anything it can't immediately place. Rare things get sold before they get understood there, he said, plainly enough that nobody needed to ask who he meant by rare. Two sessions of being near [[Coldford]] ended somewhere on that road, and on the 1st of Nightal, they were finally in sight of it.

The bracers carried the Adamantine Guild's own crest, scroll and gauntlet and coin stamped into the metal, but that wasn't enough on its own to get Ruairí through unnoticed. He tried to make himself forgettable and failed at it badly: the man overseeing entry took an uncomfortable interest, the kind that sizes someone up like a delivery still needing a weight and a price attached. Odette saw it before Ruairí could pull his hood any lower and did what came naturally to her, stepping in and flirting with the man until his attention had somewhere better to go. It worked. Coldford opened up past the wall the way a city does when you've only ever seen its outskirts: wider, louder, and entirely unconcerned with the three of them once the man behind the gate had something else to look at.

Ruairí didn't forget what she'd done for him. Walking to the Tallhouse, Odette drifted off the road partway there, chasing something that caught her eye, and the street noticed her before anyone else did: heads turning, half a dozen unhurried glances that all landed on the same person and stayed a beat too long. Kai went after her fast enough that it barely looked like worry. Ruairí followed slower, partly because he already owed her one and partly because he could feel exactly what kind of attention this was. Nobody said anything once Odette was back on the road, but the debt only grew.

The Adamantine Guild's local chapter kept a building called the Tallhouse, and its branch leader met them there in person. [[Petra Hale]] ran the branch the way she talked about it: understaffed, and honest about it, with no polish spent covering the gap. She logged the bracers already on their wrists, welcomed them to the roster, and then gave them the kind of orientation a newcomer only gets once: two hundred and twenty-two years since the Awakening, the cataclysm nobody currently walking Corranor remembers living through firsthand. The local calendar ran on intercalary days like the one they'd just traveled on, feast days wedged between months to keep the count honest.

Then came the part that actually reordered how they understood the ground under them. Corranor doesn't stay in the sea. It rises, drifts, and eventually sinks back down to it, and has been doing this on its own schedule since long before any of them arrived. Petra put the next landfall somewhere in the ten-to-twenty-day range and left it there, unwilling to sharpen a guess that nature hadn't sharpened for her.

Petra had numbers too, rougher than she'd have liked: nine Rifted confirmed at the guild's main branch, five more up in [[Stormberg]], and six now counted in Coldford. She didn't pretend the count was solid; word traveled slower than the people it counted. But the shape of it was clear enough: whatever was pulling people through wasn't done, and it wasn't happening in just one place.

What she didn't leave rough was the warning underneath all of it, aimed mostly at Ruairí: rare things sell in Coldford before anyone bothers asking questions, and a changeling's face was exactly rare enough to draw the wrong kind of attention. "Have you tried changing?" she asked, plainly, already past the point of being delicate about it. Ruairí didn't need long to settle on an answer. He thought *cat*, and the shape held, black-furred, sharp-eared, considerably less alarming to Coldford's eye than the pale, wraith-thin thing he'd arrived as. It stuck for reasons that had nothing to do with comfort and everything to do with staying unsold.

## The Pocket Dimension, and the Evaluation

Petra Hale didn't stop at the Tallhouse. She led them through a fold in the building that wasn't a hallway at all, into a private pocket of space that belonged to her and no one else, a privilege of full standing in the Adamantine Guild. Rest before anything else was asked of them, she told them, and the space itself seemed built to make good on it.

The dimension read each of them and answered in kind. Ruairí's room came back as a cliffside cottage he'd never actually had: stone walls, a hearth already going, the Irish coast his grandparents came from and told him about long before he was born, rebuilt down to the smell of it. Kai and Odette Langford found their own rooms too, quieter reflections shaped around whatever home meant to each of them, asking nothing more than the chance to sit still for a while.

Ruairí found something else first. Reaching inward for whatever his patron actually gave him, checking his own reach before the fight Petra had promised, he came up against a second vision instead of a spell list: a misty lake, still as glass, and somewhere inside the mist a shape he couldn't quite resolve, humanoid, antlered, watching him back without moving closer. It didn't explain itself. It didn't need to; the same instinct that had thrown an Eldritch Blast at a falling rock in someone else's body answered to whatever this was, and that was enough for now. He filed it away next to the pact he still couldn't name and went to go get hit by a javelin instead.

Quiet didn't last. Petra Hale tested every new member in person before she'd back them, bracer or no bracer, and for a party this small she meant to run it the way she'd heard [[Rhona Voss]] ran hers up in Stormberg rather than improvise something harder. She came at the three of them with a javelin in one hand and a longsword in the other, never planted anywhere long enough to punish. This was a legendary fighter taking them seriously enough to actually try, and the room felt it: she stepped clear of blows that should have landed, cut the gap again before anyone reset their stance, and made the whole bout feel like a question she was still deciding the answer to.

Ruairí opened with Mind Spike, three dice of psychic pressure aimed at cracking her focus, and she gave up only part of the hit rather than take it whole. Elsewhere in the same exchange, a bolt of guiding light found her before she could answer with steel of her own. She punished a wide miss with a strike back before its owner could recover, never staying still long enough to be caught twice by the same trick. When she'd seen enough, she called it, no grudge in how she ended it. Whatever the scoreline actually was, she was already backing their place in the Guild before the last blow had cooled.
