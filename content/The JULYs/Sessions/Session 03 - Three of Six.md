# Session 03—Three of Six

## The Vision That Fought Back

The vision took [[Ruairí]] the way it takes everyone who crosses over: a flat world, a man holding a spike of stone against the earth's own heaving, calm the way someone is calm after they've already decided what they're willing to lose. Every arrival gets shown that same failure and made to watch it. Ruairí got handed a spell instead. He found himself inside the collapse with an Eldritch Blast already leaving his hands, aimed at a falling shard of stone he thought he could still stop. He missed. The rock came down anyway, and the vision closed on the same fact it always closes on, he couldn't help, reached this time by trying and failing rather than only watching.

He woke into his own skin. No borrowed shape, no disguise pulled on out of habit, just a changeling in the form he was born with, on ground close enough to the others that finding them wouldn't take long.

[[Layla]] found him first. Horns, that particular amber burn behind her eyes, the same weight she always carries. She put a bag in his hands: six brass bracers, each stamped with the [[Adamantine Guild]]'s crest, and a single instruction to get them to the rest. She was gone before he'd finished asking what that meant.

The reunion, when it came, was short on ceremony. [[Kai]] and [[Odette Langford]] had already lived through their own end to the last crossing; Ruairí arrived into what came after rather than the fight itself, carrying a bag of bracers with no destination yet beyond "the rest."

## The Road to Coldford, and the Tallhouse

They left on the Feast of the Moon, the intercalary day that belongs to no month at all. The carriage was hired, not conjured: an older driver who knew the road well enough to stop narrating it, and his grandson asleep against the far wall for most of the crossing. Kai, Odette Langford, and Ruairí spent the ride in whatever quiet the wheels left them. Two sessions of being near [[Coldford]] ended somewhere on that road, and on the 1st of Nightal, they were finally in sight of it.

The gate didn't ask much of them. The brass bracers on their wrists carried the Adamantine Guild's own crest, scroll and gauntlet and coin stamped into the metal, and the guard who checked them waved the party through without a second look or a name taken. No queue, no trouble, no story to tell about getting in. Coldford opened up past the wall the way a city does when you've only ever seen its outskirts: wider, louder, and entirely unconcerned with the three of them passing through its gate.

The Adamantine Guild's local chapter kept a building called the Tallhouse, and its branch leader met them there in person. [[Petra Hale]] ran the branch the way she talked about it: understaffed, and honest about it, with no polish spent covering the gap. She logged the bracers already on their wrists, welcomed them to the roster, and then gave them the kind of orientation a newcomer only gets once: two hundred and twenty-two years since the Awakening, the cataclysm nobody currently walking Corranor remembers living through firsthand. The local calendar ran on intercalary days like the one they'd just traveled on, feast days wedged between months to keep the count honest.

Then came the part that actually reordered how they understood the ground under them. Corranor doesn't stay in the sea. It rises, drifts, and eventually sinks back down to it, and has been doing this on its own schedule since long before any of them arrived. Petra put the next landfall somewhere in the ten-to-twenty-day range and left it there, unwilling to sharpen a guess that nature hadn't sharpened for her. Something was already stirring near the city in the meantime: creatures the locals called rain-drops, born of the same cycle, spotted close to Coldford but not yet causing harm. Nobody in the Tallhouse treated it as urgent.

## The Pocket Dimension, and the Evaluation

Petra Hale didn't stop at the Tallhouse. She led them through a fold in the building that wasn't a hallway at all, into a private pocket of space that belonged to her and no one else, a privilege of full standing in the Adamantine Guild. Rest before anything else was asked of them, she told them, and the space itself seemed built to make good on it.

The dimension read each of them and answered in kind. Ruairí's room came back as a cliffside cottage he'd never actually had: stone walls, a hearth already going, the Irish coast his grandparents came from and told him about long before he was born, rebuilt down to the smell of it. Kai and Odette Langford found their own rooms too, quieter reflections shaped around whatever home meant to each of them, asking nothing more than the chance to sit still for a while.

Quiet didn't last. Petra Hale tested every new member in person before she'd back them, bracer or no bracer, and she came at the three of them with a javelin in one hand and a longsword in the other, never planted anywhere long enough to punish. This was a legendary fighter taking them seriously enough to actually try, and the room felt it: she stepped clear of blows that should have landed, cut the gap again before anyone reset their stance, and made the whole bout feel like a question she was still deciding the answer to.

Ruairí opened with Mind Spike, three dice of psychic pressure aimed at cracking her focus, and she gave up only part of the hit rather than take it whole. Elsewhere in the same exchange, a bolt of guiding light found her before she could answer with steel of her own. She punished a wide miss with a strike back before its owner could recover, never staying still long enough to be caught twice by the same trick. When she'd seen enough, she called it, no grudge in how she ended it. Whatever the scoreline actually was, she was already backing their place in the Guild before the last blow had cooled.
