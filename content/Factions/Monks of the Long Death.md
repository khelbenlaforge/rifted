## Description

The Monks of the Long Death occupy the eastern ambulatory of the [[Temple of Kelemvor]], and they are the hardest of the temple's three orders to explain to someone who has not sat with one of them long enough to feel the particular quality of their silence.

The name comes from their foundational principle: death is not a moment. It is a process — the long approach, the transition, the aftermath — and that process contains information that most people refuse to examine because they are afraid. The Monks are not afraid. They study death the way a naturalist studies a river: observing the current, the depth, the specific quality of a given crossing, the differences between this death and the last one and the next one. The work is contemplative. The affect is serene. The serenity is not indifference — it is the product of having looked at the thing directly enough times that the dread stops arriving before the observation does.

They are called Monks of the *Long* Death specifically because they are against shortcuts. Resurrection pulls a soul back before its passage is complete. Plane-shifting to avoid death circumvents the process entirely. Soul trafficking redirects a transit that was already underway. All of these are, in the Monks' view, a violence against the natural order — not a moral violence but a structural one, the way redirecting a river is a violence against the watershed. The river doesn't care. The damage is real regardless.

## Leadership & Members

The order's internal structure is contemplative rather than hierarchical in any outward sense. Elder Monks are distinguishable by manner more than marking — a specific quality of attention, a way of being still that junior members have not yet mastered.

No named member of the order has been introduced to the party. Their reputation within the temple is for approachability in matters of metaphysical inquiry, if you can tolerate the pace of the conversation.

## Operations

The Monks study. That is the primary operation, and it is taken seriously. They maintain a contemplative practice around death — observing transitions, attending to the recently deceased without intervention, documenting the specific phenomenology of various kinds of dying. The database they have accumulated over centuries has no equivalent.

Secondarily, they are the order most likely to respond when soul trafficking incidents are brought to the temple's attention. The [[The Gravelords|Gravelords]] provide the records; the Knights provide the enforcement; the Monks provide the assessment — their familiarity with the process means they can identify when a transit has been redirected in ways the administrative records won't catch.

## Current Status

Present and active in the eastern ambulatory. The Monks have noted the increased frequency of [[Shadow Creatures]] incidents in [[Crystal City]] without comment. The specific nature of their response, if any, is not visible to outsiders.

Their institutional position on resurrection remains unchanged and firm. The party has not yet been in a situation where this matters — but [[Belak]]'s potential vampiric resurrection via the [[Gulthias Tree]], and the broader question of what happens to the dead in [[Aenath]], are both subjects the Monks would have views on if asked directly.

## Membership

**How to Join:** Contemplative vocation; demonstrated philosophical alignment with the order's principles; typically a multi-year novitiate. Not open to individuals who have recently raised the dead or who hold active resurrection magic. The requirement is not punitive — it is simply that those who treat death as reversible have not yet adopted the foundational premise.
