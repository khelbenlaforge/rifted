## Description

Something tore this temple out of the ground whole, and it never came back down. What's left drifts in slow, silent orbit over the main [[Corranor]] continent to the northeast, nearer to [[Stormberg]] than [[Crystal City]] — a broken ring of consecrated stone hanging in the cloud, visible from the ground only as a smudge against the sky that doesn't move like weather should.

The heart of it is a great rose window, cracked clean through, its stone tracery still holding a disc of pale blue crystal that was never glass — it doesn't shatter, doesn't dull, and it hasn't stopped glowing since whatever broke this place first broke it. A stairway climbs to what was once the sanctuary door, flanked by two carved figures worn past recognition, robes and faces alike sanded to suggestion by however many centuries of wind. Beyond the window's ring, the rest of the temple didn't survive the fall in one piece: smaller shelves of stone and rubble hang loose around the central mass, threaded through with the same veins of blue crystal, connected back to the heart of the ruin — and to each other — only by narrow, half-collapsed causeways of the original temple floor. Step off the stone and there is nothing beneath you but cloud.

Moss and creeping vine have taken the parts sunlight still reaches. The parts it doesn't — the stairwells, the lee sides of broken archways, the long shadow the central dome throws across its own rubble — stay dark and cold no matter the hour, and something about the quality of that dark doesn't feel like the ordinary absence of light.

## Features

- **The Choir Window** — the great cracked rose window and the pale blue crystal at its heart, source of the resonance and light that mark this place from a great distance. Whatever it is, it isn't decorative — it hums, faintly, if you put a hand on the stone near it.
- **The Sanctuary Stair** — the main stairway and worn guardian statues leading up to the temple's central chamber; the widest, most stable ground on the whole ruin.
- **The Causeways** — narrow, broken stone bridges linking the outer rubble-islands back to the central mass. The only way to cross between platforms without falling — no gap here is safely jumpable, and nothing here can fly.
- **The Outer Shelves** — five or six smaller floating fragments of the original temple floor, trailing off from the central ruin, each barely large enough to stand a fight on.

## Current Events

On a normal day, nothing happens here at all — no one has lived on the Sundered Choir in longer than anyone alive remembers, and it drifts far enough from any trade route that it's more rumor than landmark. That changes the day something on the other side of the Choir Window answers a pull from somewhere else entirely, and the temple becomes, briefly, a door.

## Scene Features

**Atmosphere:** the Sundered Choir wants to be forgotten — it has spent centuries falling slowly out of the world's memory, and anything that lands here uninvited gets treated like an intrusion.

### Ambient Effects
- **Dusk-dim light throughout**, regardless of time of day — the cloud cover and the ruin's own shadow keep the whole site in dim light or darkness. (Relevant: creatures with a sunlight-weakness trait suffer no penalty here, by default.)
- **The Causeways are difficult terrain** — cracked, narrow, and missing sections; a failed DEX save or check crossing one risks a fall to the cloud below.
- **No safe line of retreat off the central platform** except back the way you came, unless someone can fly.
- **Drifting cloud banks** roll across the outer platforms unpredictably — at the start of any round, the DM may declare a random Outer Shelf or Causeway heavily obscured until the start of the next round. Use it to let a struggling PC vanish for a beat, or to let something else close the distance unseen.

> *What does the party hear first — wind through broken stone, or something moving in the dark stairwell they haven't reached yet?*

### Lair-style Features *(the location's own toolkit — no boss creature here, so these are DM-optional environmental tools, not a mandatory initiative-20 action; pull 0–1 per round, only when it serves the scene)*

- **The Choir Window can be made to flare.** Any creature touching the crystal (an action, or free if narratively justified — climbing onto the Sanctuary Stair and slapping a hand to the stone is enough) makes it flood a 30-ft radius with real, physical bright light for 1 round. This is the single most interesting lever on the map, because of an exact rules asymmetry already baked into the two stat blocks in play: **Shadow Spawn's Sunlight Weakness trait triggers on "sunlight or equivalent bright light"** — the flare counts — **but the true Shadow's trait triggers only on "sunlight,"** which the flare is not. Flaring the window gives every Shadow Spawn on the field disadvantage on attacks, checks, and saves for the round, and does *nothing* to the Shadow itself. A sharp-eyed party (Arcana or Investigation, DC 12) can work this out by watching how the Spawn flinch and the Shadow doesn't — it's discoverable, not just DM fiat.
- **A Causeway can be collapsed on purpose.** Any PC dealing at least 10 damage to a section of Causeway (AC 10, treat as an object) in one hit, or spending an action prying at it with a tool, drops that stretch of bridge — cutting off whatever's on the far side until it finds another way around (there usually isn't one). This is a legitimate way to sever a wave before it arrives; reward the idea if a player has it.
- **The Guardian Statues remember.** A PC who studies the worn figures flanking the Sanctuary Stair (Religion or History, DC 13) recognizes fragments of an unfamiliar liturgical style — not one practiced anywhere the party has been. Pure flavor/hook, feeds the open "what tore this place loose" thread; no mechanical effect.

> *Which of the four Understudies notices the flare-and-Spawn trick first — and do they say it out loud, or test it quietly?*

### Triggered Effects
- *Condition.* When a creature without a way to cross the Causeways is forced off the stone (shove, knockback, failed check), it falls toward the cloud below and is removed from the fight — treat as a serious complication requiring rescue, not an instant kill, unless the DM wants real stakes on the table.
- *Condition.* If the Choir Window is touched by an Earther specifically, its glow briefly deepens to something closer to violet before settling back — noticeable, unexplained, never called out mechanically. A seed for later, not a puzzle to solve this session.

> *What does this place look like the one time in a generation someone actually needed it to still be a temple?*
