## Description

Something put this temple in the ground and never let it back out. A single tower rises through the heart of it, five stories of worn stone stair spiraling up toward a tip that pokes past the surface somewhere far overhead — the only crack this whole buried structure has to the sky, and nobody who's woken up down here yet has climbed high enough to see through it. Water threads in from somewhere unseen and pools shallow across the floor, loud in a space this quiet.

The braziers give the place away as sacred rather than merely old: iron pillar-sconces ring the central chamber, but what sits in each cradle isn't wood or coal — it's crystal, cut rough and dark, the kind of thing a temple to a god of light shouldn't need if the god were still speaking to it. None of them are burning. In a house built for dawn, the absence of light isn't decoration. It's the whole problem.

At the center, inlaid into the stone floor, a circle of geometry no one recognizes glows on its own schedule and goes dark again after — precise, layered, deliberate, and utterly foreign. Somebody's first instinct is to call it a summoning circle. Somebody else calls it something worse. Both are guessing.

## Features

- **The Rune Circle** — the inlaid pattern at the chamber's center, source of whatever pulled five strangers in from somewhere else entirely. It goes dark once its work is done and gives nothing away about how it started.
- **The Four Bridges** — narrow stone spans crossing the underground channel in four directions from the central chamber, meeting the rune circle like spokes on a wheel. Only one has been walked so far.
- **The Sconce Pillars** — a ring of crystal-topped braziers around the chamber and up along the tower's lower landings. Unlit by default; at least one sits low enough to be half-submerged, awkward to reach without getting wet.
- **The Spiral Stair** — the tower's interior climb, five floors, wrapping clockwise as it rises. The only way up found so far, and the only lead toward the surface egress and whatever transport is supposedly waiting there.

## Current Events

On a normal day there is no normal day — nothing has needed this place in longer than anyone currently breathing could confirm, and the water keeps filling in wherever the last visitors left dents. That changes the moment the rune circle decides it has work to do: a flare of light, a handful of strangers hit the floor with no memory of how they got there, and whatever's been living in the dark since the braziers went out notices before the newcomers do.

### Session 1 Update (The AUGs)

- Feast of the Moon, Year 222 PA: five AUGs PCs (Mike Kanbei, Ruan Myrt Zaltana, Rachel Tan, Gregory Yang, and Rismawati Bayu) arrived here via the rune circle. [[Layla]], observing from the tower above, miscounted them against an expected sixth arrival that never materialized at this site.
- Feast of the Moon, Year 222 PA: shadow creatures rose out of the dark and pressed the party from multiple directions at once; the party fought them off, lighting several of the crystal sconces with radiant damage in the process. By the fight's end the chamber had regained enough light that the remaining shadow creatures froze and the temple itself read as, briefly, whole again.
- Feast of the Moon, Year 222 PA: the session closed on the party climbing the Spiral Stair toward the promised surface transport. The DM narrated it as a montage rather than a played-out scene, so whether they actually reached the top, and what's waiting there, went unshown.

## Scene Features

**Atmosphere:** the Drowned Dawn wants to remember how to shine, and it is testing whoever wakes it for whether they're worth relighting for.

### Ambient Effects
- **Dim light or darkness throughout** except within the radius of a lit sconce — treat unlit ground as heavily obscured for creatures without darkvision.
- **The channel floor is difficult terrain** wherever the water pools; the one submerged sconce is worse, close to unreachable without wading in.
- **Sound carries strangely** down here — a shout at one bridge is audible at the others, but pinpointing direction by ear alone is unreliable.

> *What does the party hear first — water, or something moving in the dark beyond the light they've made?*

### Lair-style Features *(the temple's own toolkit — no guardian currently in residence, so pull 0–1 per round, only when it serves the scene)*

- **A sconce answers radiant damage.** Any radiant hit landed on an unlit crystal ignites it, flooding a growing radius with real bright light — shadow creatures caught in newly-lit ground want out immediately and will burn a turn retreating rather than stay in it.
- **Lit ground is hostile ground, to them.** Shadow creatures actively avoid crossing a lit sconce's radius even to reach an easy target. Let the party carve safe lanes with light instead of just holding a static line.
- **The rune circle doesn't reopen on demand.** Whatever's tried on it — touching, standing on, forcing — does nothing outside its own schedule. It brought the party here. Nothing in the room suggests it works as a way back.

> *Which of the five notices the sconce trick first — and do they say it out loud, or just start aiming for the crystal?*

### Triggered Effects
- *Condition.* When enough of the chamber's sconces are lit at once, the temple's own dormant wards stir — the remaining shadow creatures present freeze in place rather than flee, and the room's light briefly reads as full daylight regardless of what's actually burning. Treat the exact threshold as a DM call in the moment, not a fixed count (see DM Notes).

> *What does this place look like the one moment it remembers being a working temple — and how long does that memory hold once the fight's over?*
