## Description

Kelemvor is the god of death and the dead, and by most accounts the fairest. Kelemvor's whole theology rests on one idea: death is a natural part of life, understood and met with dignity rather than feared or dreaded as punishment. Die with dignity, he teaches — neither raging against it nor courting undeath to escape it — and honor the dead, because what they did in life is what brought the living to where they now stand. He judges souls and forwards them on, chaotic evil to the Abyss, lawful evil to the [[The Exodus/00_My Notes/Locations/Cosmology/Nine Hells|Nine Hells]], neutral or godless souls onward to whatever waits beyond. He opposes undeath in every form, treating it as a fundamental violation of the order he keeps.

His faithful picture him as a darkly robed figure behind a silver death mask, though the walls of his temples are carved with a man in peasant's clothes.

His church in [[Crystal City]] runs three factions under one roof: the Monks of Long Death, the general Priesthood, and the **[[Knights of the Eternal Order]]**—the paladins who stand watch at the temple's three Threshold Gates (Abyss, Nine Hells, Outer Planes), not to prevent passage but to ensure it goes the right way. Elite priests carry the title **Doomguide**; the clergy at large dress in somber gray and favor the bastard sword as their weapon of choice. The Church of Kelemvor feuds openly with the churches of [[Mystra]] and [[Oghma]] over a real philosophical disagreement: Kelemvor's clergy holds that knowledge pursued without responsibility is exactly what destabilizes the cosmos.

> Look not upon our sins, Master of Scales, but measure the worth of your most grateful dead.
> — a basic prayer to Kelemvor

## The Wailing Wall

The clearest example of Kelemvor's reform in practice. His predecessors once mortared godless souls into a wall as punishment for dying unclaimed. Kelemvor broke the Wailing Wall down and promised every soul somewhere to go, worshipper or not; freely made soul-contracts, including bargains with the Lower Planes, are still honored regardless (see [[The Soul Economy]]).
