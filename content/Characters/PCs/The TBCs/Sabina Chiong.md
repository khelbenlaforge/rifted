## Description

There's a ring-light quality to her even without one nearby: chin angled a half-degree too perfect, a pause before she speaks that reads less like hesitation than timing. Dark auburn hair falls in low pigtails, pinned back with a lilac bow, framing a face dusted with freckles and a beauty mark she's never bothered concealing. She used to sell that kind of imperfection as proof her glow was natural. Her ears taper to sharp points, warm-toned to match her eyes. Even at rest, she holds her expression like a lens might be on her. She dresses the same way: an off-the-shoulder pink top, a black choker, heart-shaped drop earrings. Curated. Nothing left to chance. Fey blood doesn't show up in any single feature. It shows in how the whole look shifts with her mood instead of the season it's supposed to answer to. It's a performance so practiced it's stopped feeling like one, even to her.

## Origin

Back on Earth, Sabina ran her whole life out of a one-to-two-bedroom condo in Newton, the kind of upper-crust postcode that did half her branding for her. She built a following posting pilates form checks and green-juice mornings, fronting a wellness-and-supplements line she swore up and down wasn't a pyramid scheme, no matter how much it looked and paid like one. She believed her own pitch most days—clean living, glow from within, boss-girl independence—and on the days she didn't, she posted twice as hard. Somewhere under the filter she knew she was smarter than the business she'd built, and knew just as clearly she was too many downlines deep to walk away from it.

Then Aenath took her mid-scroll, and she surfaced Eladrin—fey-touched, changeable, dangerously charming in a way that used to be a brand voice and is now, unmistakably, something closer to real. She remembers Newton, the followers, the supplements, all of it, with the particular ache of a life she can't get back to and isn't sure she misses as much as she should. What she's found here instead is a stage with actual magic behind it, and she's slotting into that discovery the same way she slotted into influencing: convinced, against a lot of evidence, that she's finally found the thing she was always good enough for.

Whatever fey instinct now runs under her magic, it answered something in her that never needed convincing to perform in the first place.

## Abilities

The line between Sabina's old hustle and her new magic has gone thin enough that she doesn't always notice which side of it she's standing on. Deception, Sleight of Hand, and a forger's steady hand were her trade before any of this—faked credentials and doctored lab reports for a supplement line that needed to look legitimate—and those old instincts still serve where honest ones might not. But her charm has since become literal: she keeps a charm and a mirror-image trick ready at all times, and casting either one, or another spell in that same fey vein, can leave a target genuinely charmed or frightened before they know what happened. When she rallies a room, it isn't just hype-woman energy anymore—spending her own reserve of stage presence can armor her allies in a burst of vigor and let them slip clear of a blow that should have landed. She sells the difference between coaching and spellcasting as though there isn't one. Increasingly, there isn't.
