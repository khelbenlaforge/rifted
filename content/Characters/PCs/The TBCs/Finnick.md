## Description

Dark curls that won't sit flat frame a face gone a shade past stubble at the jaw, with warm brown eyes that crinkle a beat before the smile does—that's the first impression, and it's usually the only one people get before their attention drifts to whatever he's just lifted off them. He's small even by halfling standards, built for slipping through a crowd and vanishing before anyone thinks twice. Leather wrist wraps cover both forearms under a green sweater and a scuffed vest, and a silver key hangs from a chain at his throat. Not his, going by the look of it, though he'll tell you three different stories about how he came to wear it and none of them will match.

A leather satchel sits open at his elbow, coins and odd trinkets spilling over the top—the sort of bag built for things going in and rarely coming back out.

## Origin

Before Aenath, Finnick was a young man out of West Cork, the kind of Irish lad who'd charmed his way through half of Skibbereen by the time he was old enough to shave properly. He grew up singing in the parish choir, an angel's voice in a devil's pair of hands, and by his early twenties he'd traded hymnals for whatever else wasn't nailed down. The morning everything changed, he was out on Lough Hyne in a borrowed boat, putting distance between himself and a church that had gone quiet about something missing from the vestry. He genuinely couldn't say where it had gone, and he rowed like a man in no hurry to find out whether anyone believed him.

Then the lake did something lakes don't do, and Finnick came ashore somewhere that wasn't Ireland at all, folded down into a frame small enough to hide in doorways and quick enough to disappear from them just as fast. Whatever crossed with him didn't touch the parts that mattered: the smile stayed sharp, and so did the itch to check what's behind a locked door. What it added was magic that answers to nobody, not even him. It's unpredictable, and it goes off exactly when he least wants it to. For a man who's spent his whole life staying one step ahead of consequences, that's either the universe's idea of a joke or its way of finally catching up with him.

He hasn't decided which yet. What he has decided is that a world this strange is worth robbing carefully—the coin's nice, but it's never really been the point. Every artifact has a maker, and every ward a reason worth knowing before anyone notices what's missing from the case.

## Abilities

Finnick's talents split cleanly between what he trained and what just happened to him. The trained half is old-fashioned grift: a forger's steady hand and a liar's steadier nerve, backed by Sleight of Hand quick enough that marks rarely notice the exchange until well after he's gone, all of it helped along by a halfling's knack for ducking into hiding behind anyone built bigger than him. The half that just happened to him is Wild Magic. Tides of Chaos buys him a stacked d20 roll exactly when he needs one, but the debt comes due the next time he casts a Sorcerer spell with a slot: the magic surges on its own terms whether he likes the result or not. He's also started shaping spells on purpose, learning to bend a cast to fit whatever trick the moment calls for—when he bothers, which isn't always. A spell goes sideways on its own often enough regardless, an effect nobody ordered, least of all him. He treats both kinds of luck, the trained kind and the magical kind, the same way: spend it carefully, and never apologize.
