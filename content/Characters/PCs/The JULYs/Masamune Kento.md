## Description

Silver hair, worn long and half-braided so it stays clear of a loupe and a set of tweezers. Not for show. Elven ears he still isn't quite used to seeing in a mirror. He's thirty-three, though his body reads younger than that; a decade of holding his hands steady over movements smaller than a grain of rice left him with a particular kind of stillness. Even standing still, his weight is settled. Nothing about him moves without reason.

His armor is built with the same discipline he brought to his workbench. Leather worked with gear-tooth engravings, restrained and geometric, closer to a watchmaker's precision than a warrior's flourish. He looks, more than anything, like a craftsman who wandered into a fight he didn't choose. He hasn't decided yet whether he minds.

## Origin

Kento apprenticed at Seiko in Tokyo from the age most boys are still deciding what they want to be. He was skilled from the start, the kind of hands senior artisans noticed and rarely mentioned aloud, and it didn't matter. Seniority moved at the speed of seniority, not talent. He watched men with half his skill get called *sensei* while he was still called *deshi*, still sweeping the floor of a workshop he could have run blindfolded. Hard work didn't buy him anything the calendar hadn't already decided to give him. Years of that wear a groove into a person. It leaves a specific, quiet fury: the fury of being told to wait your whole life for a door that was never going to open on its own.

No one ever told him that his blood ran back to one of the legendary swordsmiths of Japan, a name spoken with reverence in exactly the tradition that kept dismissing him for not paying his dues long enough. The craft in his hands ran deeper than apprentice diligence: inheritance he'd never claimed, sitting in him the whole time the guild made him wait his turn.

Whatever pulled him to Aenath found a man already primed for it. Someone who spent his life watching injustice get excused as how things are done, and who finally has hands free to do something about it instead of just enduring it.

## Abilities

Kento fights with the same exacting patience that made him a craftsman. Every motion is accounted for; he's built to notice the one flaw that would ruin the whole mechanism. His Oath of Vengeance reads less like rage and more like verdict, a lifetime of cataloguing injustices he wasn't allowed to answer, now finally answerable. He doesn't second-guess a judgment once he's made it.
