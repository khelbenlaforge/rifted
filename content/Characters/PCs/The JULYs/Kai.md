## Description

Kai moves like a man who has already picked his exit before he's picked a seat. He's slight and quiet, the kind of build that slips into a doorway or a crowd without trying. A faint webbing between his fingers and a sheen to his eyes mark the aquatic blood in him, though everything else about him reads as plain human caution. He doesn't take up space. He'll watch a room longer than he'll speak in it, and even once he's decided you're safe, that doesn't buy much conversation.

## Origin

He was eight when his parents left him in a market town too poor to feed one more mouth. Hunger taught him fast that it doesn't wait for anyone to grow up. Kai became a thief because thieves ate and honest children in his position didn't, and his hands learned the trade before the rest of him understood what he was doing.

A stolen purse nearly got him killed and instead got him a future. The man he'd targeted turned out to be General Ren, who noticed the boy had cased the guard rotation before he'd ever gone for the coin. Ren took him in, trained him as a scout, and became, without either of them naming it that way, the father Kai had never had. For years Kai was sent ahead of the army to find the safe road, and for years he found it.

Then he didn't. He told Ren a route was clear, sure enough of his own read not to check it twice. It wasn't clear. The ambush killed the army, and it killed Ren, and Kai has never stopped hearing the silence where the general's order to retreat should have been. He resigned before anyone could relieve him of duty and disappeared into the wilderness, convinced he'd forfeited any right to be trusted with someone else's life again. What he can't shake is the training itself — he still won't walk past a stranger in trouble, whatever he's decided he deserves.

## Abilities

Kai fights from angles no one's watching and is usually gone before the fight realizes it started. He reads a room's exits the same instinct he reads its people, and he'd rather end a threat with one strike from hiding than trade blows out in the open. The streets taught him how to disappear; the general's training turned that into something closer to a discipline, where the first move matters more than the tenth.
