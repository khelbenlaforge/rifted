## Appearance

An old man who dresses like a modest countinghouse clerk and has never once been mistaken for one. His horns have gone the same ash-grey as his temples, worn smooth and blunted at the tips — deliberately, the way an old soldier peens down a blade rather than let it catch on something. Skin a dull, banked-ember red that's faded like old brick in the sun; eyes that were probably striking once and have long since dimmed to something closer to embers under ash than open flame. His tail stays still, tucked and controlled, the same discipline as his hands. A cane he doesn't need to lean on. He wears no jewelry of his own choosing — no rings or chains. The only exception is the four studs in his ear, the Claret rank marker required of all lieutenants. He has never needed to advertise what he is — the horns already do that, and he's spent forty years making sure the rest of him contradicts what people assume when they see them.

## Personality

Courteous. Unfailingly, disarmingly courteous — he asks after people's families, remembers the names of children he's met once, pours the tea himself. None of it is performance exactly; he genuinely prefers business conducted without raised voices. He has simply never needed to raise his. Everyone in the Garnet knows what happens to the rare person who mistakes his manners for softness, and none of them are around to warn you personally.

He does not enjoy violence. He is extremely good at it, which is a different thing, and the difference is the entire reason he's survived long enough to be old.

## Background

Corvin built his stake in the Claret the hard way, in an era before it had six districts or an Inner Circle — when it was two men, a handful of debts, and a willingness to do the ugly work himself that his rivals weren't willing to do. He is the last of that generation still standing, and everyone under him knows it, whether or not they've ever seen it. The stillness in his hands isn't age. It's discipline learned from a career of not needing a second attempt.

He built the Garnet side of the Claret on a simple premise: the Watch doesn't come when the Garnet calls, so someone has to. Protection, debt collection, dispute resolution — extraction, yes, but predictable extraction, the kind a neighborhood can plan its life around. He believes, and isn't wrong, that he built something more stable than what the city ever offered the district on its own.

What worries him now — quietly, and he'd never say it aloud — is watching Vashti's Amethyst play and the Sapphire nobility work pull the organization he built toward something colder. Eastern-merchant money and noble-house secrets don't answer to a neighborhood the way his own territory does. He can't stop the growth without breaking the thing he spent forty years building, and he knows it.

There is one rule in the Garnet side of the business that has never once been written down and has also never once been broken: no Tiefling blood moves through Corvin's territory. Not for Layla, not for anyone. He's never had to explain why. Nobody who's been with him more than a year has to ask.

## Role in the Story

**Archetype:** The Old Don *(Vito Corleone — courteous, controlled, terrifying precisely because he never has to raise his voice; a former enforcer who no longer needs to prove it)*

Corvin is [[Tavia Orn]]'s actual superior and the Inner Circle seat controlling the Garnet/Sunstone territory the party has been operating in all session. *(Pre-Session 25: he had not yet met the party.)* If tonight's Low Flagon scene escalates past what Tavia and the floor can settle — the party pushes for more than a mid-level contact can grant, or gives the Claret a real reason to take them seriously as a threat or an asset — Corvin becomes the answer to that escalation, not a bar full of bruisers. That escalation happened: he met them in person in Session 25.
