## Description

The most devoted servant [[Ashardalon]] ever had. Gulthias was the cult's high priest — not merely an administrator of doctrine but the living bridge between the dragon and his followers. He did not worship Ashardalon the way a man worships a distant god. He *organized* the worship, shaped it, made it operational. Where Ashardalon destroyed, Gulthias rebuilt. Where the dragon inspired terror, Gulthias converted it into devotion.

He was, by every account, a fanatic. Not the raving kind — the methodical kind. The kind who plans decades ahead and finds the failures of others simply insufficient data for the next attempt.

He has been dead for centuries. What remains of him is the [[Gulthias Tree]] in the Twilight Grove.

## History

### High Priest of the Cult

Gulthias established the cult's central temple at **Nightfang Spire** — a massive stone structure that served as the operational heart of the Dragon Cult. From there he coordinated Ashardalon's followers, conducted arcane research in the dragon's service, and served as the intermediary through whom the great wyrm's will was communicated.

When Ashardalon was mortally wounded by the druid **Dydd**, the cult fractured. Gulthias did not. He preserved what remained of the dragon's power — Ashardalon's still-warm heart — and devoted himself entirely to a single purpose: preparing for his lord's return.

### The Ritual and the Transformation

Fearing he would die before Ashardalon could return, Gulthias performed a ritual at Nightfang Spire. He gathered his faithful, spoke the rites, and at the moment Ashardalon's preserved heart gave a single impossible beat — the cultists took their own lives. Gulthias, nearest to the heart and bound to it through years of devotion, was not simply killed. He rose as a vampire, his soul tethered to the organ he had dedicated his life to preserving.

An extraplanar agency of unknown origin — some force that should not have been present — supercharged the event with negative energy. What Gulthias became was something more than a standard vampire. He and the heart were intertwined: he believed the heart retained Ashardalon's life; in truth, he had been sustaining it through his own necromantic will, too devoted to recognise the difference.

### Life at Nightfang Spire

As a vampire, Gulthias raised his dead cultists as undead servants and continued his work. He conducted experiments — increasingly extreme — in pursuit of his unfindable master. He inscribed leafless trees across the Spire's walls, a symbol whose meaning he may not have fully understood yet. He grew the cult's undead power base and waited, certain that if he could extend his reach far enough, build a sufficient foundation of devotion, Ashardalon would return.

### The Journey South

At some point Gulthias left Nightfang Spire and traveled to the [[Sunless Citadel]]. His reasons are not recorded. Perhaps he sought an artifact. Perhaps the citadel's necromantic properties called to him. Perhaps something in the dragon's preserved instructions, encoded in the heart he carried within him, drew him there.

He died in the Twilight Grove beneath the citadel. An adventurer staked him to the ground. The stake did not destroy him — it transformed him. Vampire lore holds that a stake drives the undead into death. What happened to Gulthias was different. The wood took root. The tree grew.
