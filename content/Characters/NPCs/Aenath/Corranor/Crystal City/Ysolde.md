Ysolde is the stone-faced human Keeper of the Works, responsible for the realm's arcane infrastructure—and one of the two confirmed votes against the party at the Council's Session 29 status hearing.

## Appearance

A human woman, stone-faced through every proceeding the party has seen her at—Session 28's address to the city, and again at the status-hearing vote a session later. Nothing about her gives anything away.

## Personality

Whatever Ysolde thinks, she doesn't let it reach her face. Stone-faced through the Council's public address in Session 28, stone-faced again casting her vote in Session 29—that is the entirety of what the party has to go on. No visible reaction. No tell.

## Background

As Keeper of the Works, Ysolde holds the realm's arcane infrastructure: glyph networks, civic workings, and the engines that keep the Magocracy's airships running. Her portfolio sits deliberately split from two other Keepers'. She builds and maintains the wards that [[Doran]] commands, and she keeps the airship engines running while the Keeper of the Sky Roads decides where the ships actually go. No long-distance transit magic runs through her office; whatever the Works moves, it moves by ward-craft and engine, never by teleportation circle.

## Role in the Story

At the Council's first public appearance in Session 28, Ysolde was one of two Keepers still unnamed to the party—a stone-faced human woman, unreadable through the entire proceeding, seated beside a shorter, visibly shocked gnome Keeper. She stayed nameless until Session 29, when [[Perrin Quickquill]] delivered the party a summons to a status hearing and, pressed for details, admitted the Council's vote stood split fifty-fifty: **Ysolde**, alongside [[Doran]] and [[Nerise]], against; [[Garm]], [[Wystan]], and an unnamed gnome Keeper for; [[Oriel Blackstaff]] holding the deciding vote and giving nothing away. [[Istar Silverlock]] stepped in before the party had to answer for themselves, and a persuaded Perrin agreed to tell the Council he'd never found them. The hearing was evaded, not resolved—Ysolde's vote, like the rest of the Council's, has never actually been tested against a response.
