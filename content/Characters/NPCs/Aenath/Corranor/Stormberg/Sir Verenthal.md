## Description

House Verenthal's fox-like, ostentatious Stormberg-side representative—identified by an elaborate duck-bill-shaped hair coif and a rose pinned to his clothes that he constantly smells—overseeing Wall-gang labor rostering and pay; money-focused but not cruel, he favors workers with city family or dependents for safer near-city posts while insisting that is the whole reason. He has promised the Wall gangs a forum on the roster before Nightal is out, and the gangs are back at the sea gate on the strength of it.

## Personality

Money-focused and not cruel. He dislikes trouble, likes to be obeyed and likes money, and loves the word *ledger*: every dispute is lines that must balance, one up and another down. He says he never forgets anything he has paid for, though the forum had slipped his mind. [[Roberto]] read him as cagey about why families in the city get the safer posts, and irritated that the Guild takes noble commissions yet answers to no one. His servants, a veritable platoon, look well treated.

## Background

Quill Verenthal, nephew of [[Zarantha Verenthal]], runs Wall labor rostering and pay for [[House Verenthal]] from an office by the docks that smells of brine, lamp oil and roses. [[Rhona Voss]] says his reputation is not the best, and that a rich aunt in Stormberg helps. He takes much of the public blame for the house.

## Role in the Story

Hosts the forum at the end of Nightal, in his own office. He has handed its organizing to Roberto and will answer a hazard-pay case only if it names which ledger line to lower.

## Session 7 Update (The Understudies)

Confronted by [[Roberto]] over a Wall-gang pay and safety dispute, he agreed to hold a forum for the whole roster by the end of Nightal if the gangs returned to work immediately, and paid Roberto 40 gp. He insisted family in the city was the whole reason for the safer posts, though Roberto and [[The Exodus/The Understudies/PCs/Keith Poe|Keith Poe]] both read it as only part of the truth.

## Session 09 Update (The Understudies)

- 11-12 Nightal, Year 222 PA: [[Rhona Voss]] told the party his name is Quill, that he is the man at the docks, and that he is the nephew of [[Zarantha Verenthal]], who heads House Verenthal's Stormberg branch. He takes most of the flak, and his reputation is not the best.
- 12 Nightal: [[Roberto]] visited his dock office (brine, lamp oil, roses, a coif like a duck's bill). The forum had slipped his mind. It is set for the end of Nightal in his own office, Roberto is organizing it, and Quill will send money to the Guild in advance for the work.
- He said pay is a sum with limits: he can raise one line in a ledger by lowering another, so a hazard-pay case has to say which line to lower. His costs are equipment, meals at the Wall, transport, and administrators' salaries. He would hear suggestions in writing.
- Roberto's Insight (28): he dislikes trouble, likes to be obeyed, likes money, stays cagey about why families in the city get the safer posts, and is irritated that the Guild takes noble commissions but answers to no one. His many servants looked well treated.

- 13 Nightal: [[Roberto]] convinced [[Sister Priya Dawnwell]] that Quill shows favouritism in the shift locations he assigns to Wall workers.

## Session 11 Update (The Understudies)

- Session 11, 14 Nightal: [[Kili]] and [[Roberto]] went through the worker rosters in his dock office, which was full of roses. Laborers sent to the inner columns had their families written beside them. Laborers sent to the outer columns mostly had a blank.
- His clerk [[Ambrose Tallweather]] said the household's own books were not his department. The household ledgers looked balanced except fifteen gold pieces a week to an entry with no label, only "Crystal City" written beside it, which Ambrose said Lady Verenthal enters herself, for the family's holdings. The family office alone cost 213 gold; the whole family's expenses came to 640, so the office took about a third. The recap did not keep whether those were weekly or monthly figures. Roberto noted about thirty or forty servants in the office, all faces he had not seen; the first visit had shown another thirty or forty, different faces.
