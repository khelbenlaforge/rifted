# Session 20 — Strange Peace

The city had learned to be ordinary around them again. Two weeks of it — the particular mundanity of living somewhere rather than passing through — and [[Crystal City]] had started to be a place instead of a destination, which was not entirely comfortable and was probably progress.

---

## Finding Ground

[[Sir Gareth Ironhand]] was where he always was, in the same alcove of the [[Hall of Justice - Temple of Tyr|Hall of Justice]] that he used when he wasn't actively needed elsewhere. [[Augustus Kennedy|Augie]] had been putting off the visit since they returned. Not because he was avoiding it — because he was not sure what he would find when he got there, and he preferred certainty to the alternative.

Gareth looked at the Brand the way he always looked at things he had already figured out and was waiting for the other person to understand. He had recognised the Lyonsbane panther energy in [[Kelemvor's Brand|the Brand]] when Augie first arrived. He had said nothing while there was nothing useful to say. Now there was somewhere to go.

The [[Temple of Kelemvor]] is a mirror of the [[Hall of Justice - Temple of Tyr|Hall of Justice]] — same district, same general scale, the same purposeful architecture of an institution that takes itself seriously. The difference is atmospheric rather than structural: Tyr's building is for adjudication. Kelemvor's is for transit. People move through it differently.

[[The Gravelords]] met Augie at the door in the sense that institutions meet people — he was there, and then he was being seen to, and there was no moment of decision so much as a recognition of what was already in progress. The Brand settled. The panther energy, which Augie had learned to notice as a constant background hum, went quiet in the way that a sound goes quiet when you arrive in the room it came from. The Gravelords left him with one of their number — not talkative, serene in a way that was not indifference — and the word *adherent* was used in the same tone as all their other words: as a fact that had already been established and was now simply being named.

Library access came with it. Accounts of Gravelord clerics who had plane-shifted into the Nine Hells to police soul trafficking — a shelf of dry documentation that described extraordinary events in the flattest possible prose. Most of it was beyond his vocabulary. He read it anyway.

He also met the [[Monks of the Long Death]], briefly, in the eastern ambulatory. [[Gill Bates]] had spent a month sitting with them during the last downtime — working the same questions from a different angle, the same room entered through a different door. The Monks were not unfriendly. They were studying death the way a naturalist studies weather: carefully, without preference for outcomes, noting the specific quality of each event. They were against resurrection. Not furious about it — aggrieved, in the considered way of people who believe a structural error is being committed over and over. Death is not something to fear, they said, or to reverse. It is something to understand. The understanding is the whole point.

Augie left with an additional Turn Undead available once a day, as if the god had been listening and had quietly answered while the paperwork was still being processed. He returned to the [[Hall of Justice - Temple of Tyr|Hall of Justice]], thanked Gareth, and asked for work. Gareth gave him eighteen gold and opened doors: City Guard, [[Jade Spire]] soldiers, [[Citrine District]] Watchmen, [[Sapphire District]] guards. The [[Knights of the Eternal Order]] were already expecting him in a different sense — a paladin of Kelemvor with a Brand that had already announced his affiliation was not someone the order needed to think hard about.

## The Jade Spire, Two Weeks On

The visit had been planned since before they got back. [[Jonathan Jordan|JJ]] and [[Nanuk]] arrived to find [[Perrin Quickquill]] at the entrance, more settled than he had been when they'd confronted him in the street, and a tower that looked like it had recently survived something.

The fresh paint was still visible on the rune-work. [[Tensor's Floating Disc]] carried them up to a social space full of apprentices and hovering trays — a [[Garm]]-style solution to the problem of people needing to eat while working magic. Perrin explained the runes. While the party was in [[Sunhold]], the [[Shadow Creatures]] had found the Spire. The assault had been sustained enough to halve the city guard's active deployment; the mages had spent weeks in the ward-work. The runes now held. Garm had a theory: the creatures were extraplanar, but not from any plane he recognised. He was still working on the rest of it.

The moss from Sunhold had become a drink. Perrin said it was very nutritious. He seemed genuinely pleased about this.

Then he told them about the teleportation project — which he told them carefully, because it was something he and [[Layla]] had built together. Months of work. Garm had authorised a circle to link Crystal City to other cities; they had laid it in the northeast harbour and sent a test stone through. The stone appeared for three seconds. Then it was gone, without having arrived anywhere they could detect. They had not sent people through it. They would not be sending people through it.

And on the day the party materialised at the top of the [[Jade Spire]] — on High Harvestide, out of nowhere — Layla had been supposed to be in the Sanctum below them, adjusting the angles of the runic circles. Not casting a dimensional transit spell. Fine-tuning angles.

JJ said what he had been assembling, in not quite the right order and with not quite the right inflection, and it landed as an implication that Layla had done something wrong. Deliberately. Perrin's expression shifted in the way of someone discovering that the person they were talking to has a different understanding of the situation than they do, and that the difference is not fixable in this conversation. She was his only friend. He could not believe she would make a stupid mistake. He remained cooperative — said the party was welcome to bring teleportation questions to him and Garm. He said it the way you say something true that has stopped being a comfort.

They had cast Locate Person. Thousand-foot radius. She was not there.

## Blood in the Garnet District

[[Augustus Kennedy|Augie]] found [[Tinny]] in the middle of repeating himself — same pickpocket move, different mark, caught by the same person who had caught him before. The purse went back. The surrender was immediate and professional.

[[Tinny]] had information, for the same reason that people at the bottom of a supply chain always have information: he knew where the handoff happened and when. The [[The Claret|Claret]] bosses would come to the [[The Low Flagon|Low Flagon]] in four hours to collect the day's take. The supply chain itself came from outside the city — whatever was moving through the [[Garnet District]] was coming in from somewhere else and being distributed locally. Which raised a question that no one had fully answered: if the chain was external, what was [[Layla]] doing in the Garnet District? Was she buying from it, or was the Claret's intelligence about her location something other than a neutral observation?

[[Lea]] sent a message to [[Luks Borg]]: would the Blackstaff family be involved in illegal trade. Luks said no. She believed him.

Tinny gave them the code: four litres of blood to sell. The [[The Low Flagon|Low Flagon]] was the location. The Claret's street operation was about to have company it did not yet know was coming.
