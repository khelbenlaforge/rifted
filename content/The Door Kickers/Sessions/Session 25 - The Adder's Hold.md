# Session 25 - The Adder's Hold

**In-world date:** 30th Uktar, Year 222 PA (continuing directly from Session 24's cliffhanger — same night)
**Present:** [[Lea|Lea]] (Ming) · [[Nanuk|Nanuk]] (Francis) · [[Regius|Regius]] (Clarissa) · [[Gill Bates|Gil]] (Chian)
**DM-voiced (present, fighting, minimal spotlight):** [[Jonathan Jordan|JJ]] (Jonathan) · [[Shen|Shen]] (Avinaash)
**Absent:** [[Sadiah Ispahani|Sadiah]] (Veronica) · [[Terra|Terra]] (Simar) · [[Ember|Ember]] (Dora) · [[Augustus Kennedy|Augustus]] (Justin)

## What Happened

The archway from [[The Low Flagon]]'s back room sealed behind [[Tavia Orn]] the instant the last of them crossed through, and the six found themselves in [[Corvin's Hold]] — the private pocket-dimension gauntlet of the Garnet and Sunstone Districts' Claret lieutenant. Tavia gave them a warning just inside the door before she left them to it. Ten of Corvin's own people stood waiting in the Floor Test, and a flat, unbothered voice from nowhere in particular told them to show what they actually were.

The fight was messy and mostly non-lethal by design, not by luck. Lea opened with Sleep, dropping two of the ten; Shen's Suggestion took a Warden out of the fight, sending him to sit quietly in the corner, and his Keen Mind investigation check read the room fast enough to spot the trap the fight itself was — the guards weren't hunting them, something would trigger them. Partway through, Lea's Elminster's Inquiry asked the only question that mattered — *how do we get through these people unharmed* — and the Weave answered in three words: **"Don't kill me."** That single answer reframed the whole fight. These weren't loyal soldiers. They were people who didn't want to be there.

The party pulled back from lethal intent as hard as the mechanics allowed — Gil's Thunderwave caught five of them at once and threw them into a wall instead of cutting them down, Shen's Hold Person took two more out of the fight without killing them, Regius fought his echo in and out of the melee buying space for others to slip past. It wasn't clean. Two guards still died to the party's blades before the shift in tactics took hold. And then, as one guard broke and told JJ to just go — mercy, not defiance — the voice in the corner killed him for it. A lightning bolt, almost gentle: *"I pay good money for loyalty."* The party learned two things in the same breath: the guards were never really the enemy, and neither was mercy free in this house.

They spilled into the Hub: a 40-foot chamber beneath a 20-foot domed ceiling, windowless but brightly lit with no visible lamps. Six doors and an open archway lined its walls. The voice was already waiting there, delighted, calling them "the new celebrities of Coronel" and needling JJ by name about joining the Claret. What followed was a room-by-room search for a way through that didn't mean forcing every lock: a cell door whose bars rose and fell whenever JJ came near, flagged as dangerous by Nanuk's instincts and left alone; a wax-sealed door confirmed by the same instinct to be fake, not worth trying; a glass door opening on a long gallery of lit display cases, tempting JJ enough to earn a round of "don't touch anything"; a stone antechamber with three basins marked *coin, blood, truth* — copper and silver fed to the coin basin dissolved in with a slow, grudging scrape and the voice needled back, *"You think criminals only care about coin?"*; a copper offering to the truth basin did nothing at all; blood opened a hidden panel onto **[[The Marrow Ledger]]**; a curtained room where a young Claret associate — **[[Fillis]]** — had a debtor backed against a shelf mid-threat, relieved half to death when the party turned out to be his requested backup; and a plain-looking door that opened onto two armed clerks, one stud each marking them as low-rung, calmly taking inventory of the Claret's stock, slipped past without a fight.

In the Marrow Ledger, shelves of ledgers recording money transfers went back years — huge sums per transaction, five silver a month per account — and one, open on a reading stand, showed a major client's name scraped out and re-inked into code. Lea's Elminster's Inquiry asked the Weave who the client was. The answer came back in three words: **"Layla and Asmodeus."** No one in the room fully understood what that meant. Everyone understood it mattered. Older ledgers on the same shelves turned up other names in passing: **[[House Verenthal|Verenthal]]**, **[[Tinny]]** ticked off, a scattering of Academy librarians and lecturers, a few religious titles. Nothing from the Blackstaff family, and nothing tied to the Director of Antiquities.

Fillis, once the party played along as his "backup," pressured the debtor into admitting he had a stash of cash and promising payment within the week — no forgiveness, just a deferral. The debtor blamed his shortfall on Temple of Tyr paladins making the streets too hot to fence anything through; he was three weeks late on payment. Fillis, grateful, owed the party a favor afterward.

The last door opened on Corvin Adder himself: an old man with a cane he didn't need, tea already poured for exactly the right number of guests. Four studs lined one ear — the Claret's rank marker. He wore all four; the organization has only four lieutenants total, and he's one of them. He introduced himself by name, thanked them for the evening's entertainment, and got to business without ever raising his voice. He confirmed what the party suspected: Layla is a longtime, reliable client of his — pays on time, never explains herself, never has to. Over the relationship she's paid the Claret 10,000 gold total. From what he let slip, she's currently somewhere around Stormberg, expected back near the Feast of the Moon, give or take a few days. He also, without much hesitation, named the tension eating at his own organization: another Claret lieutenant, **[[Vashti Corel]]**, has been pushing her people into Garnet and Sunstone territory that isn't hers. He didn't ask the party to do anything about it tonight — just to keep their eyes open.

What he offered in return: a real lead on where Layla operates, and — pressed — a sending stone he could use to reach her directly. On top of that, 100 platinum for the evening's trouble and a spot for JJ in the Claret's first rung, reporting to Tavia. The party talked him into a condition: he won't make that call until they've returned from following the Blackstaff family to the Sunless Citadel. He agreed, and named his price — a future favor, unspecified, no questions asked, whenever he decides to collect. Somewhere in the middle of all of it, genuinely curious rather than performing it, he asked what it was like to come from a world with no magic at all, and the party found themselves describing television and advertising to an old crime lord who couldn't stop turning the idea over.

The session closed on that agreement — no blood between the party and Corvin Adder, a name added to their list of problems (Vashti Corel), a lead on Layla gated behind a trip they haven't taken yet, and a debt they haven't been asked to pay.

## Key Takeaways

- **[[Corvin Adder]] met the party in person** for the first time — courteous, controlled, genuinely curious about Earth, and willing to kill his own people over a show of mercy toward the party.
- **[[Vashti Corel]]** — a rival Claret lieutenant — was named directly to the party as a live internal fault line in the organization, tied to the existing reagent-demand-spike thread.
- **Layla lead secured but gated:** Corvin has a sending stone and is willing to use it — but not until the party returns from the Sunless Citadel trip with the Blackstaffs. Understood to currently be around Stormberg, expected back near the Feast of the Moon.
- **The Asmodeus thread cracked open, vaguely:** Elminster's Inquiry named Layla's Marrow Ledger account alongside Asmodeus. The party doesn't know what it means yet.
- **A future favor is owed to Corvin Adder** — unspecified, no questions asked, whenever he calls it in.
- **Party reward: 100 platinum, plus JJ's induction** into the Claret's first rung, reporting to Tavia.
- **2 of Corvin's men died to the party; 1 more died to Corvin himself** for showing the party mercy.
