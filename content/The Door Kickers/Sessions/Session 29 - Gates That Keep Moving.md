# Session 29 - Gates That Keep Moving

**In-world date:** 1 Nightal through 3 Nightal, Year 222 PA, continuing directly from Session 28. The party departs Crystal City on the third day.

**Present:** [[Gill Bates|Gill]] · [[Min Ji-ah]] · [[Nanuk|Nanuk]] · [[Regius]]
**DM-voiced:** [[Terra]] · [[Sadiah Ispahani|Sadiah]] · [[Shen]] · [[Ember]] · [[Jonathan Jordan|JJ]] · [[Augustus Kennedy|Augustus]]

## What Happened

### Two Gold and a Warning

Morning in the [[Citrine District]] smelled like woodsmoke and wet stone, and the [[Adamantine Guild]]'s hall was quieter than the party had ever heard it. [[Istar Silverlock]] sat at his usual table with one arm still in its sling, a quill in the other hand, three ledgers open in front of him, working through a plate of something that had clearly been breakfast an hour ago. He looked up before they'd finished crossing the floor.

"Sit. You're all still in one piece, which is more than I expected." He set the quill down like it was a relief to put it down. Officially, he told them, the Guild backed them fully, whatever they chose to do north. Unofficially, the Guild's line was five coppers and a shrug, because that was what was actually in the strongbox this month. He apologized for it anyway and handed over two gold apiece from his own pocket, not the Guild's—travel money, and a warning not to drink all of it before they cleared the gate.

He was straight with them about what waited past Stormberg's gate: nothing. No branch office, no standing arrangement, nothing the Guild could promise them once they were past the Wallwatch. If it came to it, they should find **[[Rhona Voss]]**—good people, he said, for whatever that was worth this far out. He dug a small sealed token out of a drawer and asked them to carry it up to her. Not urgent. Not dangerous. Just easier carried by someone already walking there than paid for.

On the matter of the Black Staffs' invitation to Sunhold, Istar didn't try to talk them into anything. The house wasn't a bad one, he said, and there was no shame in being in their good books, but he didn't like being told it was a done deal before anyone had asked the party's opinion. If they wanted to go, he'd back it. If they wanted to send someone from the Guild along, it would probably be Darya rather than Luks, who was still raw from leaving his own patient behind the last time he was pulled off a case. If they went their own way north instead, the Guild had nobody to spare to send with them.

Before they scattered for the day, he had two more things. Marin was expecting Gill, whenever Gill was ready. And Torem had been asking after Nanuk at the Tamers' tower, something about the sky, lately. Go find out what that meant before leaving the city, he said. That was his advice, and the last thing he told them before the warning about the road: past a certain point north, the Wall does something to sound, and the party shouldn't trust their ears out there. He hoped, only half joking, that there wasn't a breach in it waiting for them.

### What the Dice Gave Them

Before the party could leave the table, Istar remembered he still owed them a reward from the job that had cleared out the guild's back debts. He rolled for it there at the table so they wouldn't have to wait.

[[Regius]] came away with a dented, city-issued suit of plate, solid work, but it would need a day and some coin with a blacksmith before it fit for the road. [[Nanuk]] pulled the better draw: a suit of serpent-scale armor that let him add his full Dexterity modifier with no cap, which turned into an argument at the table when it revealed his own sheet had been under-calculating his Armor Class for some time. Corrected, his base sat at 14; with the new armor on, 19.

[[Min Ji-ah]] rolled into something better still. A cloak, spider-silk, sticky to the touch, the color already shifting to match whatever was behind it, that a history check placed as the make of people who don't see sunlight. A Piwafwi, old and drow-worked: resistance to fire while worn, advantage on Dexterity checks to hide with the hood up, and a corresponding disadvantage on her own Wisdom-based Perception while it's up. It loses its magic after an hour of uninterrupted daylight, a rule the table argued over until they landed on the cloak's own wording: a single minute back in the dark resets the clock, not the full hour Istar had first guessed at.

On top of the individual pulls, one more luck roll handed the whole party three thousand gold, collectively, to spend before the road. Istar didn't pretend it was charity. It was every gold piece the Guild had been sitting on, finally paid out at once.

### Held Still

Gill went to the [[Tidecaller's Sanctuary]] alone that morning. [[Tidecaller Marin Deepcurrent|Marin]] was waiting at the spring-fed pool at the temple's heart, barefoot, sleeves already rolled, no joke about fishing waiting for him the way there usually was. *The current doesn't wait for anyone to be ready,* he said, and had Gill sit at the center stone while the water climbed.

The first attempt broke. As the water rose past his shoulders and the goddess's presence thinned at the edges like a coastline losing its color, Gill reached for her, tried to hold the fading back by force, channeling divinity outward instead of letting it go. The vision ended gently, without the violence it could have had, Marin already pulling him out, and the day cracked forward without warning: two days gone, just like that, and no answer yet to what the trial was actually asking of him.

The second attempt held. The water rose the same way, the presence thinned the same way, and this time Gill let it. He prayed instead of reaching, focused on the stone under him and the water moving through everything it touched, and heard her voice come back thin and far away: let go, my son. He did. Somewhere past the edge of what he could feel, Marin cast Sending to him mid-vision, five words, don't forget to breathe, and the water dropped away all at once. He came up soaked and shaking, three days gone in total, with the first level of the Sacred Sea finally his.

He gained Create or Destroy Water as an always-prepared spell, and Marin pressed a smooth grey stone from the pool's bed into his hand before he could ask what it was for, something to hold onto, for the times letting go is the only right answer. His connection to Istishia is stronger now, small conversations where before there were none.

Before Gill left, Marin gave him a task for the road: try to feel the ocean again, the way he had in the pool, at the next landfall or at any underground spring he passes. He named [[Corran's Spike]] as one candidate, northeast of the road to [[Stormberg]]. *You'll have to choose,* was all he said about which one Gill takes.

### Eyes on the Wall

At the [[Azure Tamers]] tower, [[Torem]] had already been asking after [[Nanuk]] before Nanuk arrived. He laid out the travel math for Stormberg without being asked twice: on foot, seven to eight days; by carriage, four and a half at a breakneck pace, five and a half to six at a normal one, seven or eight taken as a leisurely tour. Flying, pushing a bird past what it should be pushed, a day and a half to two. Bella, the griffin, was his to offer: one rider only, transferable to any guildmate, two at most if they didn't mind the weight.

Then the part that made him uneasy. The herds were coming down out of the north country early this year, and heavier than the season should send. He didn't know why, and said as much, plainly, without guessing at it.

Nanuk asked whether flying ahead would help find the grove faster. A nature check, rolled well, gave him the honest answer: the grove would be hard to spot from the air no matter how good his sense of direction got. It isn't the kind of thing you find by looking down at it. It's sensorial, the closer he gets, not a mark on a map.

### Gates That Keep Moving

[[Min Ji-ah]] spent her morning at the [[Ether Scryer's Academy]]'s archive, not looking for anyone in particular. **[[Wystan]]** found her instead, the way he seems to find everyone eventually, setting down a ledger he clearly hadn't been carrying a moment before. He didn't deny watching her at the Jade Spire. *Old habit,* he said. *I watch what the Archive can't yet explain, and I couldn't explain you.*

What he had for her was a page: a rubbing from a solitary ranger's journal, roughly a century and a half old, tracking someone who'd set out following what she called the Oak Father's pull, heading generally toward what would become Stormberg. The entry ended mid-thought. *Never found, neither was the grove. Draw your own conclusions.*

Min Ji-ah read the page in full with Elminster's Footnote and got nothing beyond what was already written; the missing lines weren't hidden, they were simply gone. So she turned to Elminster's Inquiry instead, working the question sideways through several failed phrasings before landing on the one that mattered: what was it waiting for? The answer came back in two words. *Gates. Moving.*

Between what the page said and what the party already knew of the planes, the shape of it came together at the table: the grove isn't a fixed place at all. It sits behind gates into other planes, gates that never hold still, and all of them cluster in the same general direction, north, which is exactly the pull Nanuk has been following without being able to name it. Finding the grove was never going to be a matter of walking to a spot on a map. It means finding a gate while it's open, or guessing right about when one will be, and there was enough in the reading to suggest that passing through, even found, might cost something.

Wystan left a folded note behind on his way out, unprompted: a pointer to the exact stack the rubbing had come from. He never asked what she found. He didn't need to.

### The Nightly Watch Ends

[[Regius]] found **[[Ostrin Feld]]** waiting at the House Verenthal side gate before he'd even knocked, fingers already tapping his signet ring twice, the tell that something was wrong. *Reggie,* he said. Not Regius. He only used the short name when things had gone sideways.

Ostrin paid him in full, fifty gold up front and fifty more promised later, and told him flatly that the nightly watch on [[Sellas Verenthal]] was over as of tonight, no further explanation offered. Pressed on why, he wouldn't move: *whatever she knows, she's not telling me, and I can't afford to find out the hard way. Neither can you.* Regius tried once more, asking after the man he'd been paid to look into. Ostrin's fee bought him nothing further there; the topic was closed, plainly, and stayed closed.

What softened it, a little, was the last thing Ostrin handed over: a sealed letter and token, stamped with the Verenthal crest, for whoever still answers to that name at Stormberg. Don't open it. Deliver it if he reaches the Wall; burn it if he doesn't. *Consider your account with this house settled, either way.* The gate closed behind him the same way it had opened, without ceremony, and without anyone waiting to see him out.

### The Summons They Never Received

Back at the Guild with the day's shopping still ahead of them, **[[Perrin Quickquill]]** caught up in the corridor, a little out of breath, his raven riding his shoulder. He held out a folded parchment sealed in green wax, a summons to appear before the Council of Seven for a determination of status. *Whether you're people, under the law,* he said, when asked what that meant. *I know how it sounds. I don't love it either.*

He didn't pretend the vote was settled. The Council stood split fifty-fifty: [[Doran]] and [[Nerise]] against, [[Garm]], [[Wystan]], and an unnamed gnome for, [[Oriel Blackstaff]] holding the deciding vote and giving nothing away yet. **[[Istar Silverlock]]** stepped in before the party had to answer for themselves, took the summons from Perrin's hand and told him plainly they weren't obligated to go, not unless they wanted to.

They didn't want to. A persuaded Perrin, worked over gently and well, agreed to carry a different story back: that he'd never found them, that they'd already left the city by the time he came looking. He didn't relish it, and he did it anyway, because he liked them more than he liked the paperwork. Istar backed the story without being asked twice. Whatever the Council makes of an absent party when it finally rules, it will be ruling on a version of events with a hole cut into it, and two people now stand a little exposed on the party's behalf if that hole is ever noticed.

### North of the Guild's Reach

The rest of the day went to shopping and small errands. **[[Tavia Orn]]** sold three vials of poison at a hundred gold each, along with antitoxin and holy water at the Claret's usual rates. At a shop called The Shining Wand, near the Academy, a scroll-seller named Kara sold a Mage Armor scroll and a Mirror Image scroll for the full asking price of three hundred and fifty gold between them; she wasn't moved by any argument that a rifted ought to get a research discount. Istar's own people saw to the rest without charge: cold-weather clothing, bedrolls, tents enough for shared pairs, and ten days of rations for the road ahead.

Before they left, [[Min Ji-ah]] pried a whistle loose from [[Jonathan Jordan|JJ]] and left [[Augustus Kennedy|Augustus]]'s old dungeon-find weapon in his keeping, carrying it no further north herself. Word came separately that Augustus meant to buy passage on a bird the moment his own business in the city let him go.

By the time they were ready, the roster for the road was set: [[Gill Bates|Gill]], [[Min Ji-ah]], [[Nanuk]], and [[Regius]], travelling with [[Terra]], [[Sadiah Ispahani|Sadiah]], [[Shen]] and [[Ember]] alongside them. [[Augustus Kennedy|Augustus]] and [[Jonathan Jordan|JJ]] stayed behind in Crystal City. One carriage and two horses would carry the bulk of them, Ember running loose alongside as a wolf, and Bella overhead with room for two more, eyes on the road ahead of anything that found them first.

They left the city gates on the third day of Nightal, cold-weather gear on their backs and the pull north still just a pull, nothing closer to an answer than it had been that morning.
