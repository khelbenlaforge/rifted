# Session 28 - A Name I Cannot Read

**In-world date:** the night of the Feast of the Moon, Year 222 PA, continuing directly from Session 27 and running into the first day of Nightal. [[Regius]]'s flashback covers the preceding nights, back to the night of the Claret meeting.

**Present:** [[Gill Bates|Gill]] (Chian) · [[Min Ji-ah]] (Ming) · [[Nanuk|Nanuk]] (Francis) · [[Terra]] (Simar) · [[Sadiah Ispahani|Sadiah]] (Veronica) · [[Regius|Regius]] (Clarissa)

**Absent:** [[Jonathan Jordan|JJ]] (Jonathan) · [[Shen|Shen]] (Avinaash)

## What Happened

### Seven on the Steps

Half an hour before midnight, sweat and blood still unwashed out of their clothes, the party came to stand in front of the [[Jade Spire]]. The green stone held its colour even at that hour. Two hundred and twenty-two years of building had gone into a city whose people mostly cannot remember anything before it, and the spire was the argument for all of it.

[[Garm]] stepped out in the purple robes of his office rather than the brown ones the party knew him in, and behind him came six more. The [[Corranor Council]] had never stood in front of the city all at once before, and none of the party had ever laid eyes on more than one of them.

Garm spoke first and said outright that he had been asked to and thought it was a mistake. A cult of Shar had taken the First Gravelord off his own vigil during the Deeds of the Dead, and had failed to finish him. He was alive, standing behind Garm, and would close the Feast himself. *You can judge how he looks with your own eyes rather than take my word for it, which I prefer anyway.* The doors of the Spire would open the next day to anyone with a question, and Garm would answer as far as he was permitted, and say plainly where the line was rather than pretend there wasn't one.

The second to speak was a very old dwarf in robes, no armour anywhere on him, who did not care for the party at all. **[[Doran]]**, Keeper of the Bulwark, was exact and brief. Sixty of the Watch and eleven of his own guardians had moved through the [[Diamond District]] before dawn. The shrine beneath Cistern Street was closed and its officiant dead. Then the part he had come to say: there had been a summoning, it was bound at the site and dispersed there, a conjuration of that class cannot happen without a practitioner standing in the room to call it, and those hands were accounted for. *I have heard people saying something came into this city from outside it. It did not.* He looked at the party while he said it. Every one of them had watched the thing leave under its own power.

Then a man none of them had seen and all of them had heard of. Tall, lank, black hair and black beard, black robes lined in silver, an air around him that dropped the temperature a few degrees. **[[Oriel Blackstaff]]**, Keeper of the Writ, keeper of the city's laws, head of the oldest family on the continent. He declared the Cistern Street shrine unlawful assembly and its property forfeit to the city. Then he opened an inquiry into the [[Temple of Kelemvor]], and into how the [[Knights of the Eternal Order]] admit people to the precinct during a vigil of obligation. The square took in air all at once. Nobody touches Kelemvor's church. It is not feared, it is loved, because it is the institution that promises the dead get seen off properly. [[Augustus Kennedy|Augustus]], who belongs to the Order, went visibly grey. [[Ser Tamsin Rook]] was remanded to his Order for judgment, its findings owed to the Council rather than the public square, and the matter declared closed to discussion. The Council thanked the private citizens who had acted the night before. It did not name them, and said it would not.

### What the First Gravelord Said Instead

[[Gill Bates|Gill]] was not in the crowd. He was up on the platform, invited there by the man whose life he had torn off an altar, standing close in case the strain finished what the Sharites had started.

**[[Cadmus Tallow]]**, First Gravelord of [[Crystal City]], is seventy and passes for fifty. His voice was wrecked, so the Knights around him carried it, and he never tried to raise it. *The Keeper of the Writ has closed a matter. From this hour, he said. I heard him.* Then the smallest glint of mischief out of a man who could barely stand. *I am going to open it again before I am finished, and he may do as he likes about that.*

Eleven hundred and forty names had come to the temple since sundown. All eleven hundred and forty were spoken before dawn took the list. He wanted the number exact, he said, because the church is the ledger and a ledger must be precise. The recitation had not stopped while he was not standing in it. Others had kept it. In thirty-one years as First Gravelord he had not known until that night that it would hold without him. A bier stood in the square while he spoke, carrying one of the monks who died keeping it.

What Kelemvor offers, he told the city, is smaller than what people arrive at the doors hoping for. Not a gentle death, or a long one, or any promise that the deserving go last. Only this: you will be met, you will be counted, someone will say your name and what you did that mattered, once, aloud, and then you will be let go.

Then the thing he plainly wished he did not have to say. There was a name he could not read. **[[Alys Rook]]** died in this city four years ago and was never met. She never arrived. She is still here, and has been the entire time, and the roll has no line for a soul that never came to the temple, so it has no line for her. Her brother knew, and had known for four years, and the night before he had opened a door for people who promised him they could give her rest. *I am expected to say that he betrayed us. I have read the book. The book says we failed her first.* He took responsibility as First Gravelord for a soul that had not been returned to the cycle, blessed the city, and started to fall backwards. Gill caught him.

### The Names Going Up

Monks and clerics came forward with the papers in their hands and began to chant. The letters lifted off the pages and burned into a line of script across the sky, name after name after name in a hand none of the party can read yet.

[[Nanuk|Nanuk]] and Gill saw what the rest of the square could not. Terra reached for it and got nothing at all.

To Nanuk it was small lights rising off the ground, each one climbing toward a single line of text, a flash where the two met, then both gone together. Gill saw further, and saw what those lights actually were. People. Old ones, young ones, a dozen kinds of them, drifting up half-blind until the chant turned their heads, and then making straight for the words. Each soul reached its name and the name closed around it in gold. Some of the light was translucent blue, some red like fire, some black as night, and it made no difference at all to whether they were taken.

Not all of them rose. Down among the people praying and weeping and laughing, there were more that never lifted.

Beside [[Nanuk|Nanuk]], [[Inukshuk]] watched without distress and said that the spirits of Nanuk's own people are stuck here too, and so is the Great Spirit, and that there is hope in this. Nanuk was not moved to grief. This is simply what is supposed to happen.

[[Min Ji-ah]] spent the speeches reading the seven. Garm hates the platform and only wants to get things done. Doran is embarrassed, and it is the party's fault: things have been getting through the city's defences ever since they arrived, and he takes it personally. **[[Wystan]]**, Keeper of the Archive, a very tall elderly man with blue-tinged hair she recognised from the [[Ether Scryer's Academy]], did not hear a word anyone said. His eyes went back and forth between Gill on the platform and the rest of them below it, and the appetite in it was curiosity rather than malice. What can I get out of these people that I have not had a chance at yet. She stared back until it was uncomfortable. **[[Nerise]]**, Keeper of Exploration, sword at their hip and none of the usual mage about them, was not watching the Council at all but looking out past the crowd, thinking about the expedition sent to find other lands and now on its way home. Nerise flinched, slightly, when Oriel turned his guns on the Temple. Doran did not.

### Three Souls for Mine

Two nights earlier, on the night of the Claret meeting, [[Regius|Regius]] had gone to sleep and the dark had parted like a curtain.

A river black as night. Skeletal hands clawing at the banks, hunched figures wading in to drag bodies out and fling them behind. He was not on the bank. He was in the water, and the water was oily, and the longer he stayed in it the more of his mind went. Others drifted alongside him, four of them together, and he knew they mattered to him and could not reach why. What filled the space where the memories had been was not pain. It was despair, and grief, and lies.

Something with enormous wings came down and scattered the spiked, hunched things away from the group with one sweep. *No. They belong to my lord.* Regius reached toward her, grateful in the uncomplicated way of a man who has just been saved.

One of the others pulled him back by the arm. Her eyes were the only lucid thing in that river. She held up a scroll. *As we agreed. Three souls for mine.* The winged figure took it, read it, said all was in order and that her lord would be pleased. Then the compression started, and the people around him folded down into coins, every one of them except the woman who had handed over the scroll. She collected three, looked at him last, and he woke up shaking in the dark with nothing but the certainty that he was worse off than before.

He would not put it together until much later that night.

### The Verenthal Nights

Regius has spent his absence watching [[Sellas Verenthal]] for [[Ostrin Feld]], who wants to know what she does at night and does not want to ask her himself.

Night one: Ostrin leaves the house first, carrying a flat black case. Ten minutes later Sellas leaves, dressed for a party. Regius, who cannot tail anyone quietly in scale mail, talked his way down the street as a festival-goer instead and followed her to a shuttered florist's shop that was somehow still taking deliveries. She went in, came out inside a minute looking drunk, propped herself against the door frame and waited. Ostrin walked out a little later and did not see her. She straightened up the moment his back was turned and followed him home. She never went to the party.

Night two she went back, and this time she did not stop at the doorway. Through the gap Regius watched her talk to a rough-looking man with a single ruby stud in his ear, one of the Claret's, and then shove him out of view. A flash of light. The smell of cooked meat. By the time Regius got close enough to see, she was crouched over a smoking body. Then she stood, dusted herself off, and walked home calm as anything. One of the scrolls she carries was gone. The body was otherwise untouched.

He told Ostrin eventually, and badly, having first tried to establish whether the young lady had tortured animals as a child. Ostrin paid him fifty-two gold and asked him to speak plainly next time.

### Everything, Laid Out on the Table

Back at the [[Adamantine Guild]] that night the party sat down with their Guild masters and went through the threads one at a time. By the end of it the four Guild people in the room were staring at them.

[[Layla]] is still at large and still teleporting, inside the city and beyond it. Word has come back that the second batch of arrivals reached [[Stormberg]]; the Wallwatch there has them, and they will work the mystery from that end.

Then the shape of the thing. This world has no teleportation magic. Nothing that punches a hole through space and time can happen here, which makes the party's arrival not merely unusual but impossible, and they did not come from another plane, they came from another universe. Short hops still work, because Misty Step only skims the Ethereal, and the Ethereal, the Shadowfell and all four elemental planes sit *inside* the astral barrier. The barrier cuts off only what lies beyond, which is where the gods are supposed to live. That is why the Church of Kelemvor, whose charge is policing souls out to the afterlives, cannot do it, and has fallen back on a ritual in the hope that the ritual works.

The elemental spirits [[Inukshuk]] has been telling Nanuk about for months finally got their names. Between Nanuk, Gill and Uki, the answer came out: **[[Istishia]]**, **[[Grumbar]]**, **[[Kossuth]]** and **[[Akadi]]**, the primordial demigods of water, earth, fire and air, mapped onto Nanuk's four winds. They are weakening. All four are behind the barrier with everyone else, which is exactly why Gill has been hearing Istishia's voice while every other cleric in the city insists such a thing is impossible.

[[Silvanus]] is not one of the four. He is the Oak Father, a god of nature who ought to be in [[Arborea]] alongside [[Malar]], and is instead here, and in pain, and able to speak. Sylvara's gesture north was never vague.

Gods run on worship. A god without worshippers cannot hold up his end of anything, and if the primordials fail, so does the world they are holding together. Ember, working the same thread from the other side, felt out the shape of the Lower Planes: no gods there, but a **soul economy**, an appetite for souls in circulation, because souls are what can be traded, contracted and spent. Which is what [[Asmodeus]] wants, and why a name like his turns up in a ledger beside Layla's.

The party's growth items are pieces of their own souls and cannot be left behind. They follow. The same is true of Ocean, and of Fernando.

[[Shen]]'s cosmic vision and Nanuk's first vision are the same event at different moments. Nanuk saw the spirits descending to a world under construction. Shen saw that world nearly finished and under attack by things that sound a great deal like the shadow creatures, with a barrier going up around it, and the party worked out for themselves that the barrier was built to hide the new world as much as to protect it. Shen's patron was hired, mercenary-fashion, to help hold the line while it went up.

And [[Aenath]] is only the top of the rectangle. There is a second world underneath it: **[[Zenath]]**.

### How Magic Still Works

Which left the obvious question. If [[Mystra]] is behind the barrier, how is anyone casting anything?

It was [[Terra]] who had actually picked the three moons out on the Feast. Min Ji-ah put Elminster's Inquiry to them, and got back the sound of a pipe being smoked. [[Elminster]] again. She saw three beings kneel to Mystra, and then Mystra kneel to them with tears on her face, and an agreement made. Not gods. Demigods. Three names came with it: **[[Solinari]]**, **[[Lunitari]]**, **[[Nuitari]]**. They gave themselves up willingly to become Aenath's three moons and anchor the Weave in her place. *Sometimes the greatest sacrifices are done out of pure love for magic.*

Mystra also split her essence among mortals in the old world, and the party has met two of them. There is no particular reason to assume none of the Chosen are here.

Older still: [[Selûne]] and Shar were one bright thing, and when they split they made duality, the lit face and the dark, and the first Mystra came out of that split, and everything else followed from it. Returning souls to their place is a Selûne principle. Shar's people believe in the peace of nothingness, which is a philosophy before it is a crime.

For Terra, the last piece. [[Lolth]] is Selûne's great enemy, and the familiar presence Terra felt on the enemy side of her own vision was **[[The Unbound]]**. Her old cult leader from Earth was standing among Lolth's people in a memory of this world's distant past, which means he is either here or inside her [[Hollowstone]], and either way not on Earth any more.

### Sadiah, and What Fits

[[Sadiah Ispahani|Sadiah]] has not had a vision. She went back to her forms and her breathing instead, and something surfaced anyway. When she was pulled through, she watched herself come loose from her own body, and the first thing she felt was relief. Enormous relief. On Earth she had been holding on to yoga the way you hold on to the one thing keeping you upright, in a life she disliked, in a family that had come apart, counting money for the electricity. Here, a piece she did not know was loose has clicked into place. She is also the only one of them speaking Common natively, switching in and out of it without noticing she has done it.

That reframed Layla entirely. If Layla is bringing souls *into* Aenath, and those souls originally belonged here, then the party's own arrival looks less like an abduction than a return: taken from where they were never supposed to be, and put back where they belong. It is a theory the party is holding, not a fact anyone has confirmed.

Regius arrived at his own version out loud. The four people in the river were his family. His mother traded three souls for hers, and the buyer is the name that keeps surfacing beside Layla's. He announced his intention to strangle the Lord of the Nine Hells. He is level five.

### North

With more threads on the table than they can pull at once, the party chose. The Blackstaffs can wait; the answers there are second-hand and two days' travel away besides. Silvanus is here, in pain, and can talk.

The Guild offered what little is known. Silvanus has never in living memory taken worshippers, but the legend among the nature faiths says he sometimes calls a worthy soul to his grove, and the grove is not a physical place. The [[Belak|Ancients of Lava]] have been hunting for it for years and he has never once let them in, which the party took as a solid argument for telling them nothing. Nanuk can feel the pull well enough to follow it. It points north.

Which means [[Stormberg]]. A town behind a wall two to three hundred feet high running the entire northern coastline of [[Corranor]], built because every time the continent rises to float, the storms come in off the edge of the world and hit it. Past that, the Arctic, which is not a circle here but a line drawn from one end of the world to the other.

### The Brotherhood of the Four Winds

On the first day of Nightal, Gill went to see his mentor with a more accepting posture than usual, and [[Tidecaller Marin Deepcurrent|Marin]] sat him down and told him something he has never told anyone.

There is an ancient pact among the clergies of the four elements: never admit that you can hear your god. Marin does not know why. He knows his predecessor told him the secrecy keeps the world safe, that he has felt Istishia's voice getting fainter for thirty years, and that his brothers among the flame-callers, the earth-movers and the air-callers all report the same thing. The rivalries between the four faiths are theatre, maintained on purpose. Underneath it they serve one function, and Marin has been quietly funnelling etheric energy to Istishia on his own for years.

Then he pointed at the spire, which is visible from anywhere in the city, and told Gill how the world was made. The four elemental deities gave their materials, and then willingly let **[[Gond]]**, god of the forge, hammer their own divine bodies and essences into the fabric of it. A smith beating a blade into shape, except the metal was the gods. And one more besides the four, a fifth who gave himself the same way, to bring life into it. Marin does not know who the fifth was. Gill can make a very good guess.

The first level of the Sacred Sea, Marin said, need not take the full ten days. Come back, and it can be done in one.

## Key Takeaways

- **The Council of Seven, seen whole for the first time.** Five named: [[Garm]] (Jade Spire), [[Oriel Blackstaff]] (Writ), [[Doran]] (Bulwark), [[Wystan]] (Archive), [[Nerise]] (Exploration). Two remain unnamed to the party.
- **[[Doran]] lied to the whole city** about the fiend being summoned and bound at the site. The party watched it leave under its own power, and now hold a sitting Keeper's public falsehood.
- **An inquiry is open into the [[Temple of Kelemvor]]**—a magocrat reaching into religious law he has no standing in, using the Gravelord's abduction as the lever.
- **[[Cadmus Tallow]] defied the Writ in public** and named [[Alys Rook]], four years dead and never counted, taking responsibility for her rather than condemning her brother.
- **Some souls do not rise.** The recitation works. It does not work on everyone.
- **The four elemental primordials are named:** [[Istishia]], [[Grumbar]], [[Kossuth]], [[Akadi]]. Weakening, inside the barrier, and hammered into the world by [[Gond]] at its making. [[Silvanus]] gave himself the same way, to bring life.
- **[[Mystra]] is outside the barrier.** Magic runs through [[Solinari]], [[Lunitari]] and [[Nuitari]], three demigods who chose to become moons. Chosen of Mystra may walk Aenath.
- **[[Selûne]] and Shar were one being.** Their split birthed the first Mystra, and everything after.
- **The soul economy.** Gods run on worship, the Lower Planes on souls in circulation. This is the frame that finally makes "Layla and [[Asmodeus]]" mean something.
- **The party may be Aenath's own, returned.** Held as a theory, not confirmed.
- **[[Regius]]'s mother traded three souls for her own**—his, and two others of his family, to something winged in a black river.
- **[[Sellas Verenthal]] killed a Claret man** with a spell scroll at a shuttered florist's, and Regius was seen.
- **[[Aenath]] has an underside: [[Zenath]].**
- **The party is going north** to find Silvanus's grove, by way of [[Stormberg]].
