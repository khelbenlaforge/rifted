# Session 30 - What Broke the Pack

**In-world date:** 5 Nightal, Year 222 PA, Day 2 of the Road North out of Crystal City.

**Present:** [[Regius]] · [[Terra]] · [[Nanuk]] · [[Min Ji-ah]]
**Traveling, DM-voiced:** [[Gill Bates|Gill]] · [[Sadiah Ispahani|Sadiah]] · [[Ember]]
**Off-page:** [[Jonathan Jordan|JJ]] · [[Augustus Kennedy|Augustus]] · [[Shen]] (stayed behind at the Ether Scryer's Academy)

## What Happened

### The Road North

The dream came apart before anyone could hold it. Cold, and something reaching, and a sense of a voice underneath the cold that none of them could later agree on. It didn't feel like sleep. It felt borrowed, the way the visions always do, and it was gone by the time the carriage wheels started turning.

[[Nanuk]] rode with the carriage for the first half of the day before sending [[Inukshuk]] ahead to run point, trusting the bear-spirit's nose over his own eyes. [[Regius]] took the reins. The Guild road ran straight where the land let it, the forest close on both shoulders.

It was Nanuk who broke the quiet, not Regius—an apology, low and a little awkward, for not having talked to him much since they'd met. He admitted the truth under it: he hadn't been sure Regius was even real, not for certain, until about a week ago. Regius didn't take it as an insult. He'd had Inuit guests once, back on Earth, and something about the way Nanuk carried the spiritual side of things made sense to him the same way. They found the thing they actually had in common fast enough—both of them needed answers out of [[Layla]], for different reasons that kept turning out to be the same reason. Regius laid his theory out plain: he thinks Layla's tied to [[Asmodeus]], and tied to whatever happened to his mother. Nanuk didn't argue the shape of it, only widened it—Layla's experiments were sanctioned, he said, which means it goes higher than her. **[[Garm]]** signed off on them. So, somehow, did **[[Perrin Quickquill|Perrin]]**, a name Nanuk clearly didn't think much of. Neither of them had an answer for what that meant for the woman who'd signed herself "E." They just had more names now than they'd had that morning. Somewhere in the same conversation, both admitted to the same quiet, specific feeling—that they belonged here, to this world, more than to the one they'd left.

Up on [[Torem]]'s griffin, [[Terra]] and [[Min Ji-ah|Ji-ah]] had a different conversation entirely. Terra talked about **[[The Unbound]]** for longer than she usually lets herself—a lawyer's suit, jet-black hair, a long face and a narrow nose, and eyes that felt like they saw clean through whatever you were trying to hide. He always seemed to know when something was wrong with someone before they said it. She'd felt safe under that, she admitted, in a community that didn't ask you to understand things yourself as long as he did. Then the mine town got bought out from under all of them, and he vanished the moment the builders arrived—no warning, no goodbye—and finding him again is most of what's kept her moving these past five years. Ji-ah didn't have The Unbound's exact shape in her own life, but she had the shape of what a charismatic leader does to a room, and the particular ache of having lacked direction for a long stretch of it. She said as much. It was the most either of them had told the other outright.

Everyone else's visions came up again too, the way they do whenever the road gives everyone too much time to think. [[Silvanus]], and the feeling that none of them belong to the last world—corroborated now by three separate people describing the same thing in their own words.

A Survival check found cloven hoof tracks crossing the road, fresh enough to argue about. The table weighed the Guild road against the narrower trail Nanuk's pull kept tugging toward, and chose the road. Slower, maybe. Known, certainly.

### What Bella Saw

Up on [[Torem]]'s griffin, the road unspooled underneath in a long grey ribbon, and what came into view past the treeline made the whole day's purpose click into place. Wildebeest herds, huge ones, moving south early and moving fast, with predators trailing the column the way predators always do when the prey is running scared. This was Torem's job made visible: the herds nobody in Crystal City could explain, seen from the one vantage point that actually showed the scale of it.

Further out, low along the horizon where the land met the sea, a wall. Grey, immense, running the whole northern coastline as far as anyone could follow it with their eyes. The **[[Stormwall]]**, still a long way off, more rumor than structure from this distance, but unmistakably there, holding a line against a coast that clearly needed one.

The party sent [[Ember]] off ahead of the tavern to keep watching the **[[Ancients of Lava]]** again—not her own call this time, but a task the group gave her on the strength of [[Belak]]'s known history with the faction. She left without much explanation beyond that, the way she does when something's caught her attention she isn't ready to talk about out loud.

Flying low past a watering hole, someone spotted the first of it: a deer, dead at the road's edge, clean-killed, no drag marks, no sign of a fight anywhere near it. Its antlers were gone. Nothing else about it had been touched. Nobody landed to look closer. The light was already going, and there was a tavern ahead with a fire in it.

### The Straggler's Rest

**[[The Straggler's Rest]]** sat where the road widened into a turnaround, a low timber building with a stable out back, smoke going straight up from the chimney into still air. [[Sadiah Ispahani|Sadiah]] had already scouted it ahead of them, unseen, before the carriage ever came into view.

Inside, a broad, sun-cracked woman was already reaching for cups before anyone ordered. **[[Ossa]]** ran the place the way she'd clearly run it for years: talkative, motherly, and about as forthcoming as a woman who genuinely doesn't know more than she's telling can be. A trapper had come through days back wanting to sell off everything he carried and buy passage south instead of finishing his season—bad luck all season, by his own account, and he'd finally had enough of it. His partner had kept going north instead. Labor inspectors out of Crystal City were cutting timber somewhere nearby, and a friend of hers had lost livestock to something she couldn't name either; she mentioned [[Coldford]] too, its permanent mist, further east than any of that.

She was the one who asked, half serious, whether people really could arrive from other worlds—the kind of question a woman who's poured drinks for strangers long enough eventually works around to. Someone talked her out of the idea entirely, Deception doing the heavy lifting, and she bought it without much of a fight: if anyone could manage a trick like that, she said, it'd be the wizards up at the Stormwall, and even they hadn't. Someone else asked after the Lord of the Hells, and got back the kind of answer a name like that earns from common folk: everybody's heard of him, nobody agrees what he looks like, and almost nobody worships him outright. Just a shape people invoke when they want to sound like they understand something bigger than themselves. Whoever's actually behind that name, **[[Asmodeus]]**, would have found the whole exchange beneath him.

They took rooms for the night. Nobody kept close count of who slept where.

### Two Trappers by the Fire

[[Nanuk]] found two trappers still up by the fire after most of the room had turned in, **Dev** and his unnamed partner, both of them cagier than Ossa had been about the same road she'd already talked about so freely. He caught more than they meant to give him. Something in the way they talked about the woods invoked **[[Mielikki]]**, half-prayer and half-habit, the kind of thing hunters say without thinking about it.

He warned them off heading further north, citing what the **[[Azure Tamers]]** had already reported about unusual movement out past the road. They took it seriously. Whatever their plans had been, they looked like men reconsidering them by the time the fire burned down.

Between them they gave him a working map of what actually hunts this stretch of country: dire wolves, sabertooth cats, one basilisk incident about five years back that everyone still talks about, and nothing that looked like a dragon, not recently. Nanuk floated the idea anyway. A dragon disturbed enough to send the herds running early would explain a great deal. The trappers didn't argue him out of it outright, but the shape of it didn't fit clean. A dragon on the move causes a fast migration, not the slow, dread-soaked crawl they'd all just watched from the air. It didn't rule the theory out. It just left it sitting there, unresolved, next to everything else that didn't add up yet.

### The Erased Crossing

Morning came, and the road ahead of the advance party looked wrong before anyone could say why. At a game-trail crossing, the ground had been scraped clean, not trampled, the way a broom clears a floor rather than the way feet wear one down. Tracks gone. Wheel ruts gone. Whoever had come through here hadn't wanted anyone reading what they'd left behind. Fresh blood still marked the ground around the deer itself, up close now, its horns sheared off with a blade clean enough to leave nothing ragged behind.

Nanuk knelt by a roadside conifer and turned his own Firbolg gift on it, Speech of Beast and Leaf, asking it what it had seen. What came back was a struggle, a splash of something warm, then movement heading generally west. On foot, the party found what the tree had described: broken branches, scrape marks in the dirt, and a lingering trace of magic that didn't belong to anyone they knew. Nature-touched, or divine, or both at once.

The direction didn't match anything Nanuk already had. His own pull toward Silvanus's grove had been sitting steady to the north, north-west, the whole way up the road. This was west. Two separate signals pointing two separate ways—whatever moved through here wasn't the same thing he'd been following since Crystal City, just something that happened to cross its path.

A High Religion check put a name to what they were all starting to feel out there. Nature has an opposing power, one that doesn't share Silvanus's patience or Mielikki's care, and its name is **[[Malar]]**. Whether the Beastlord's own hand was in this kill specifically, nobody could say for certain—the taking itself read as controlled and unhurried, nothing like the savage, blood-hungry sport his own creed usually demands, which left the theory feeling half-right at best. Word had also reached them, unconfirmed and secondhand, that the [[Ancients of Lava]] might count a few of Malar's own faithful somewhere in their ranks. It was the kind of rumor that explains too much to ignore and too little to trust.

Somewhere in the same conversation, a name came up that some of them already half-knew: **[[Jess]]**, elder of the Ancients of Lava, tending a grove somewhere out past where any road runs, known personally to only a handful of her own faction's people—the same elder who'd confirmed [[Belak]]'s own history with the Ancients, back when [[Ember]] first asked. Free information, offered rather than earned. Whatever she knows, she isn't the one keeping it from anybody.

### What They Left the Deer

They gave the deer the kind of send-off Nanuk's people would give it. Every part of the animal found a use, meat and hide alike, and a small marker went up where it fell, thanks paid to something that didn't ask for it. What meat they didn't need went back down the road on Bella's back, sold off to cover the trip's costs before the trip had even finished costing them anything.

The road went quiet again after that, the kind of quiet that swallows a day whole. Nanuk's pull kept pointing them north, no clearer than it had been in Crystal City, just steadier. Somewhere in a library stack, before any of them left the city, a hundred-and-fifty-year-old precedent had turned up: another ranger who'd felt this same pull, searched for months, and found nothing worth writing down. It didn't discourage anyone. It just meant they weren't the first.

### The Pack That Came South

They came out of the treeline on both sides of the road at once: six wolves, ribs showing through matted coats, and two saber-toothed tigers flanking them like something had broken the rule that usually keeps wolves and cats from hunting together. Whatever that rule was, hunger had eaten it.

[[Terra]] readied Spike Growth at the treeline the moment the pack broke cover—a ring of thorns held in reserve, in case Nanuk's offer went wrong. It never needed casting. She let it go the same moment the alpha sat down to eat.

Nanuk didn't reach for a weapon. He talked instead, low and even, hands open, to the pack and not to the party. An offer, not a command, made the way one hungry thing recognizes another. *(A stretch of what happened next is gone for good, a permanent gap in the recording, already logged and not worth guessing at.)*

It worked. Fed rather than fought, the pack took what was offered and settled, and the whole business took about twenty minutes that felt considerably longer. No blood. No bodies. Just animals too desperate to keep pretending they weren't.

### What Broke the Pack

None of the six wolves was ever Erky, and neither were the tigers. A golden glow passed through the pack first—scrapes and starvation easing, just slightly, all at once—and then something walked out of the treeline that hadn't been part of the pack a moment before: small, multi-tailed, midnight-blue fur catching what little light there was. A Velvet Vulper. The same disguise he'd worn once before, when the party knew him only as a caged gnome and the creature sharing that cage called Little Sister, back in the Sunless Citadel. It sat down, put a paw to one of the sabertooth tigers' heads, and shimmered into **[[Erky]]**, Tymora's holy symbol plain around his neck, looking entirely too pleased with the reveal for someone who'd just spent the evening as a fox.

He hadn't come alone, or rather, he'd come to stop someone else from arriving: a second pack of rangers, unnamed, closing on the same starving animals from the other direction. Erky threw up a scatter of his own illusory duplicates to run interference and drove them off before anyone came to blows over a pack of half-dead wolves. It worked cleanly, the way most of Erky's tricks do.

What he told them afterward mattered more than the trick itself. The pack hadn't banded together out of any normal instinct. They were fleeing something further north, something that might itself be running from something else entirely. It was the same pull Nanuk's been feeling since the city, said back to him from a completely different direction, by someone with no reason to lie about it.

Erky is with the party again, as of tonight. However he came loose from [[Luks Borg]]'s supervision, and whatever that costs him later, he's traveling north with them now, and everyone at that fire knew it was the biggest thing that had happened all day.

[[Min Ji-ah]] didn't wait to be asked before reaching for her Sending Stone—word to [[Luks Borg]], Erky's back with us. Luks's reply spent every one of its twenty-five words on profanity and not one of them on an actual answer.

Night fell before anyone got much further than that. Wherever tonight was always leading still waits somewhere up the road, unreached. Before anyone turned in, someone finally asked the question that had been sitting under everything since the kill-site: was the pull to Silvanus's grove still there, after all of it. The DM's answer was straightforward—yes, don't worry about that part. What's still open is the other half: something out there, still unnamed, going around and making a mess of things nobody can quite describe yet.

## Key Takeaways

- **[[Erky]] rejoins the Door Kickers.** Arrived separately from the wolf pack itself, disguised as a Velvet Vulper—the same illusion Little Sister once wore, back in the Sunless Citadel—revealed himself, drove off a rival group of rangers with illusory duplicates, and is traveling north with the party again, a major continuity shift from his last known status under [[Luks Borg]]'s supervision.
- **[[Malar]] surfaces for the first time to this party.** A High Religion check ties the kill-site's pattern, clean and deliberate, to the Beastlord rather than to [[Silvanus]]'s own creed. Unconfirmed rumor: the [[Ancients of Lava]] may count some of his worshippers among their ranks.
- **[[Torem]]'s migration job is advancing, not solved.** Early, heavy southbound herds, a drained kill-site trail, and the wolf pack's own desperation all point at something further north that even Erky's pack was running from.
- **[[Ember]] is now shadowing the [[Ancients of Lava]]**, sent by the party on the strength of [[Belak]]'s known history with the faction—not a solo call this time.
- **New location: [[The Straggler's Rest]]**, a waystation on the north road, innkeeper **[[Ossa]]**. New names on the board: **Dev** and an unnamed partner (trappers), and **[[Jess]]**, elder of the Ancients of Lava, named but not yet met.
- **[[Regius]]'s search for his mother widens.** [[Garm]] signed off on [[Layla]]'s teleportation experiments, with [[Perrin Quickquill|Perrin]] tangled in it somewhere too—Regius's suspicion that Layla is connected to his mother just picked up a name on the paperwork.
- **One hook closed, one stays open.** Nanuk's pull to Silvanus's grove is still there—the DM confirmed it, nothing to worry about. What's unresolved is the opposing force "going around messing things up," tied to the Malar/Ancients of Lava rumor above but not yet named.
