# Session 18 — Into the Roots

The violet pulse from below was faster when they woke. Not the slow patience of something that could afford to wait — a stutter-beat now, like a tree that had been counting breaths all night and had run out of courtesy. [[Balsag]] was already on his feet. Axe in hand, not raised. Just held. He'd been watching the door to the south since the last watch ended, and he was going to keep watching it until someone opened it.

---

## The Last Morning in the Sanctum

The murals hadn't changed. The same gods watched from the same walls. Somewhere below, the [[Gulthias Tree]] had stopped being patient.

[[The Exodus/The Door Kickers/PCs/Augustus Kennedy|Augie]] woke to a room that had grown while he was unconscious — a bugbear he didn't recognize, a gnome in the corner performing drunk with the precision of someone who had done it many times for many audiences, and a rat in a tiny rogue outfit that made eye contact and declined to look away.

The items found their bearers before their bearers found them. Something had settled against Augie in the night — not a physical weight, exactly, or not only that. The mark of [[Kelemvor Lyonsbane|Kelemvor]]'s attention, pressed into him from a ceremony he hadn't been present for. A god's claim made while he was somewhere else, with the particular form of patience that doesn't explain itself: a hand on the shoulder that doesn't quite lift. He didn't get a manual. He got the weight of it. The Unclassified Specimen was already in [[The Exodus/The Door Kickers/PCs/Shen]]'s hands when he registered reaching for it, his patron arriving in the clipped, barely-contained tone it uses when something has caught its interest and it is choosing not to say why yet.

Through the Brand's enhanced senses, Augie smelled the power from A56 before anyone spoke of moving. Something south. Something waiting. The violet pulse agreed.

## Into the Grove

The plan made sense on its face: [[The Exodus/The Door Kickers/PCs/Shen]] walking openly — [[Belak]] knew him, had kept him for weeks — while the rest of the party moved through the briars under stealth. A familiar face at the front. Some room to breathe before the conversation became something else.

Belak had already figured it out. He addressed the hidden members by their approximate positions when the party arrived at the clearing, his voice carrying the particular patience of someone who has been naming a thing they've known all along and choosing courtesy over accuracy until now. [[The Exodus/The Door Kickers/PCs/Jonathan Jordan|JJ]] fired a crossbow bolt before the speech could continue. Some things cannot be endured further.

The fight in the clearing was not clean. Belak was everything the party had spent weeks approaching — quiet, certain, with the Grove behind him and years of its service in every movement. [[Sir Braford]] advanced immediately, the way walls don't negotiate. Blights came when called. The party managed it, not comfortably.

What cut through the calculus was [[Balsag]]. He saw [[Gork]] the moment he crossed the threshold into the clearing — half-absorbed into the Gulthias Tree's roots, barely breathing, her chest moving in small wrong increments. He did not pause. He did not assess. He went through whatever was between him and her and dragged her free, physically, by force, with the determination of someone who had already decided they were not going to have to make that choice again.

## The New Hunter

Mid-battle. Something arrived that was not through a door.

His name was [[The Exodus/The Door Kickers/PCs/Nanuk]]. He came in three pieces, each bleeding through in sequence like memory reassembling itself from the wrong end of a fall.

A kitchen table in an Inuvialuit home. His mother explaining, with the exhausted pragmatism of someone who has run the numbers too many times, why the energy corporation's trades made sense — convenient goods for village relics, locally-sourced wildlife, the usual accounting of a community that has run out of alternatives. The [[Ancestor's Quiver]] hung on the wall behind her. He disapproved. He was powerless. Both were true at the same time, and there was no version of that truth that resolved cleanly.

Later. The Arctic, open sky, the particular quiet of a landscape that ends you without noticing. An adolescent polar bear moving toward him with the calm of something that had decided not to be afraid. He waited. He waited longer. When it was close enough, he raised the bear spray — and the blue light found him first.

And then the stone chamber. Before the party arrived, before the battle, before any of this. He came to on the cold floor near a woman bound in vines, her breathing shallow, her eyes still moving. She struggled to form words. She spent what she had left on two of them: *escape.* He lost consciousness before he could act on it.

He woke mid-battle and joined the fight without introduction. There was nothing else to do.

## A Word and a Door

[[The Exodus/The Door Kickers/PCs/Lea]] ended it — not with force, but with a word placed precisely in the right mind at the right moment.

*Suggestion*, landed clean: stop attacking the party, let everyone go. [[Belak]] considered this. Complied. His expression held something that might have been acknowledgment — not surprise, exactly, but the look of a man who knows when a negotiation has moved past his position. He had never been afraid to lose. He had always been afraid of something else.

Then he cast Travel Through Plants and stepped into the [[Gulthias Tree]]'s trunk.

The bond broke the moment he was through. Blights stilled. Braford went quiet, then slowly, strangely, himself again. [[Sharwyn Hucrele]] could breathe. The tree went dormant — its vast root network going silent in the way an argument goes silent when the person making it has left the room. The party was victorious, in the way you are victorious when the thing you were fighting has chosen a different door. One you cannot follow through. One that opens into root systems running miles under dark stone, toward destinations that cannot be mapped from above.

Belak's location: unknown. The clock on whatever comes next started in that silence.

## The Cavalry (Such As It Was)

A flash of silver-and-copper light. [[Luks Borg]] hit the ground mid-stride, already moving. [[Elmindaria L'Thorian]] was still adjusting her robes. Between them, held firmly by the scruff of his collar, was [[Erky]].

Luks had burned [[Rune of Recall]] charges — rare, non-renewable, the kind of resource you do not spend on a hunch unless the hunch is very good — on the theory that their guildmaster was somewhere in this dungeon. The theory was correct. [[Erkaniel Timbers]], guildmaster of the [[Adamantine Guild]], was set down with deliberate care and regarded by Elmindaria with the patience of someone who has been filing reports about him for a very long time.

*"You are going to work very hard,"* she said, *"for a very long time."*

*"I had a feeling,"* Erky said.

[[Little Sister]] — the Velvet Vulper who had traveled alongside them through every level of the [[Sunless Citadel]], foxy and small and entirely present — shimmered and resolved into nothing. Erky's Invoke Duplicity, maintained for weeks across floors of goblins and blights and druidic magic, a trickery cleric's sustained illusion worn as a companion. Not a creature. Never a creature.

The party sat with this for a while.

## Aftermath

The aftermath was quick. Time had been what it had been.

They came out of the Sunless Citadel and walked back north to [[Sunhold]], where the [[Hucrele Family]] paid what they'd promised for the retrieval of Sharwyn and Braford — both alive, both recovering, neither one entirely the person they'd been before the vine-roots got into them. [[Balsag]] said his farewells the way he does everything: not sentimental, but weighted, a promise attached without ceremony. *When you call.* Then he was gone back into whatever the bugbear's business was, leaving the promise behind like a stone set in place.

Two weeks of road northward. [[Crystal City]] waiting at the end of it. And the rest of their lives as people who had walked into a dungeon and come out knowing considerably more than was comfortable.
