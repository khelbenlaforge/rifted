It sat in a drawer in Stellenbosch for six years, a notary's signet a senior partner pressed on him the week he made associate — *sodat mense onthou wie se naam op die lêer is* — so people remember whose name is on the file. He never wore it. Rings got in the way of counting cash under a table, and by then the file that mattered most was the one he kept in his head: which street, which predator, which week. The wish took him mid-thought, cataloguing a city he was about to leave. The ring came with him anyway, reforged into something that fits a claw better than a finger.

## Description

A flat brass signet, the elaborate law-firm crest worn down to a blur — deliberately, like someone took a coin to it for years out of habit rather than damage. What's left in the metal isn't legible as any language a court would recognize. When !D'bleh manifests a Psychic Blade, the seal's blurred face catches a cold blue light for exactly as long as the blade holds, like a stamp coming down on something that was already decided.

### Tier 1 — Standing Judgment *(L3 — current)*

**Verdict *(passive)*:** The first time each turn !D'bleh hits a creature with a Psychic Blade attack, add 1d6 psychic damage. The blade doesn't just cut. It rules.

**Closing Argument *(passive)*:** Whenever !D'bleh deals Sneak Attack damage, the target has disadvantage on attack rolls against him until the start of his next turn. The verdict lands. They hesitate before they swing back.

**Practiced Stillness *(passive)*:** !D'bleh has advantage on Stealth checks while in dim light, foliage, or a crowd — anywhere a city offers a place to stand still and be overlooked. Vineyard rows taught him this first; every city since has just been a different kind of row.

**The Audit *(1/Short Rest, action)*:** !D'bleh studies one creature he can see within 30 feet. He learns whether it has, to the DM's judgment, knowingly caused serious harm to someone weaker than itself within the last year — nothing more specific than that. The seal doesn't hand him proof. It hands him a reason to keep watching.
