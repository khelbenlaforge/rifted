It was mid-shutter the day the pull took him — an old instant camera, lens housing cracked and held together with gaffer tape from three separate repairs, the kind of gear that should have died years before he did. He'd been three seconds from pressing the button on something he'd chased across two continents to prove existed. The film cartridge inside still reads one exposure left. It has read one exposure left every day since.

## Description

Battered, scuffed at every corner a shoulder strap could rub, patched more than it's whole. The film counter is stuck on 1 and has never moved, no matter how many times Kay fires the shutter out of habit. When he takes a shot in earnest — not fidgeting, actually looking for something — the flash doesn't fire white. It fires the pale, cold blue of a held breath, and whatever it catches stays caught a beat longer than it should.

### Tier 1 — Contact Print *(L3 — current)*

**Caught in the Flash *(passive)*:** The first time each turn Kay hits a creature with an attack, that attack deals an extra 1d6 radiant damage. The shutter goes off whether the subject is ready or not.

**Already in Frame *(passive)*:** A creature Kay has hit with an attack this combat can't benefit from being unseen against him — no advantage for attacking from hiding, no bonus from Invisibility, nothing behind cover he's already gotten a clean shot of. Once it's in frame, it's in frame.

**Old Instincts *(passive)*:** Kay has advantage on Investigation and Insight checks made to read a scene, catch an inconsistency, or tell whether someone's lying to him. Old habit, new stakes.

**Last Frame *(1/Short Rest, action)*:** Kay raises the camera and takes the one shot it's always had loaded, targeting a creature he can see within 30 feet. He learns one true thing that creature is actively concealing — the DM decides how specific, and it's an impression, not a confession. The picture never comes out. He just suddenly knows what would've been on it.
