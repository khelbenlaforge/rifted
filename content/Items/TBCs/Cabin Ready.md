It survived the crossing the way she did — bent slightly out of true, gold plating scuffed thin at one corner where a thousand safety demonstrations knocked it against a jump seat, and still entirely legible. XIAOXIAO, it says, in a typeface built for a language and a company that no longer exist anywhere she can reach. She has never once considered leaving it off.

## Description

A rectangular enamel badge, gold-plated brass gone soft at the edges, pinned now to the underside of a paladin's breastplate where a passenger would never see it and she can still feel its weight against her sternum. The pin has been resoldered twice. She checks that it's fastened the same unconscious way she used to check her lipstick before a boarding announcement — automatically, without looking down.

### Tier 1 — Final Boarding *(L3 — current)*

**By the Book *(passive)*:** The first time each turn Xiaoxiao hits a creature that is within 5 feet of one of her allies, she deals an extra 1d6 radiant damage. She never made an exception for a difficult passenger. She doesn't make one now.

**Between You and the Aisle *(passive)*:** The first time each turn a creature within 5 feet of Xiaoxiao is hit by an attack, the attacker has disadvantage on its next attack roll against that same creature before the start of Xiaoxiao's next turn. She spent a career putting her body between a passenger and turbulence they couldn't see coming. Old habits.

**The Right Tone *(passive)*:** Xiaoxiao has advantage on Persuasion checks made to calm, reassure, or de-escalate a hostile or frightened creature. Every airline drills the same lesson into new crew: the room takes its temperature from you.

**Please Remain Seated *(1/Short Rest, Bonus Action)*:** Xiaoxiao chooses one — a hostile creature within 30 feet that can hear her must succeed on a DC 14 Wisdom saving throw or have disadvantage on its next attack roll; or she ends one Charmed, Frightened, or Poisoned condition currently affecting a willing creature within 30 feet. It isn't a request. It never was, really — just phrased like one.
