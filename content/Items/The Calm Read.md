It rode in a lab coat pocket through more clinic waiting rooms than she can count, pressed against a hundred small chests that were too scared to hold still. She never once had to fake calm for an animal — that part was always real. Whatever pulled her out of a sales quota and into Aenath brought the stethoscope with her, still warm from the last exam she ever gave.

## Description

A plain steel stethoscope, the tubing worn soft and pliable from use, the chest piece scratched dull. It looks exactly like what it is — a working tool, not a treasure. When Frankie shifts into a beast's shape, it settles into her rather than her clothes, a steadiness that has nothing to do with ears that can no longer wear it.

## Properties

**Carrier:** [[Frankie]]
**Attunement:** Required (Frankie only)

**Wildshape note:** the item bonds to Frankie herself, not to her hands or a wearable slot — every Tier 1 ability below functions identically whether she's in her own body or wildshaped into a beast.

The Calm Read grows in power as Frankie levels.

### Tier 1 — First Exam *(L3 — current)*

**Reading the Pulse *(passive)*:** The first time each turn Frankie hits a creature, she deals an extra 1d6 damage of the same type as the attack. Years of reading a body under stress taught her exactly where it's already failing.

**Calm the Animal *(passive)*:** Beasts within 10 feet of Frankie, including Frankie herself while wildshaped, have advantage on saving throws against being frightened. A clinic waiting room full of scared animals taught her how to hold a room steady. It still works when the room is a battlefield and she's the one wearing claws.

**Vitals Check *(1/Short Rest, no action required)*:** Frankie instantly senses one creature's exact current hit points as a fraction of its maximum, and whether it is diseased or poisoned. No spell, no words involved — the same diagnostic instinct she's had since before any of this was magic.

**Bedside Manner *(passive, narrative only)*:** Wild beasts that see Frankie, in any form, read her as safe by instinct and are markedly less likely to attack her first unless directly provoked (DM's judgment). The gap she used to wonder about, between herself and the animals she loved, was always this thin.
