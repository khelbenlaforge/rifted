Seiko never gave him a title, so he made his own tools instead — a loupe ground to his own eye, tweezers balanced to his own hand, both carried on him longer than most people carry a name. He was still *deshi* the day the Rift took him. He didn't bring anything else. He calls it *mekiki* — 目利き, an eye that knows genuine from false at a glance. It's the one word from the trade he never had to earn.

## Description

A jeweler's loupe on a fine steel chain, the brass housing worn smooth at the thumb-grip from a decade of the same motion. The tweezers ride in a slim leather sheath beside it, tips honed to a precision no smith in Aenath has matched yet. Neither piece looks like it belongs in a fight. When Kento finds the flaw in an opponent's guard, the loupe catches light that isn't there — a cold, exact gleam, like a jeweler confirming a stone is genuine.

### Tier 1 — First Inspection *(L3 — current)*

**The Flaw *(passive)*:** The first time each turn Kento hits a creature that has already taken damage this combat — from him or anyone else — he deals an extra 1d6 radiant damage. He doesn't need to be the one who found the crack. He just needs to see where it already is.

**Second Offense *(passive)*:** From the second time Kento hits a given creature onward (the two hits don't need to land on the same turn, just anywhere in the same combat), that creature has disadvantage on attack rolls against anyone but him until the end of its next turn. A verdict, once rendered, doesn't leave room for other business.

**Craftsman's Eye *(1/Short Rest, action)*:** Kento studies an object, mechanism, or plan within 30 feet (a locked door, a forged letter, a battle line) and instantly identifies its one critical flaw (DM's judgment on what's useful). The loupe never lies about where something is weakest.

**Bloodline Resonance *(passive, narrative only)*:** The loupe warms faintly, almost imperceptibly, near masterwork blades and the hands that forged them. Kento has never mentioned this to anyone. He isn't sure it means anything.
