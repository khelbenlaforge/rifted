Every hawker stall and dog-walking gig and clothing shoot came with the same small ritual: a chop pressed into a ledger, a receipt, a contract nobody bothered to read past the dotted line. She'd bought this one off a retiring stallholder in a coffee shop for two dollars, mostly because the carved handle was shaped like a coiled cat and she liked the idea of a stamp that looked like it was watching you. Half her jobs ran on chop, half ran on chope — a seat saved with a tissue packet, a claim staked before anyone else got there first. She was three jobs deep into the week and closing a fourth when the light took her mid-negotiation.

## Description

A small rubber-and-wood stamp, the carved handle worn smooth into the shape of a crouched, watchful cat. The stamp face itself shows no image or characters — just a blank, faintly warm oval — until it's pressed against something, at which point whatever mark appears is never quite the same twice.

### Tier 1 — Standard Terms *(L3 — current)*

**Closer's Instinct *(passive)*:** Kili has advantage on Persuasion and Deception checks made while shape-shifted via Shape-Shifter. Stacks with Silver Tongue — a 9 or lower is still treated as a 10, and now she's rolling twice to get there.

**Rider Clause *(1/Short Rest, bonus action)*:** Kili grants an ally an extra Bardic Inspiration die (1d6) without expending one of her daily uses. Every deal has fine print nobody notices until it's already working in their favor.

**Standing Ovation *(passive)*:** Whenever a creature Kili can see reduces an enemy to 0 hit points, she regains one expended use of Bardic Inspiration (up to her normal maximum). Someone else closed. She still gets her cut.

**Second Face *(1/Long Rest)*:** Kili may press the chop against anything standing in for an agreement — a letter, a chit, a handshake still warm on her palm, a promise made across a table with nothing signed at all — and know, without asking, whether the deal favors someone other than the person who agreed to it, and in broad strokes how. The stamp has never once told her the answer she wanted about her own arrangements. She presses it on other people's instead.
