The screen cracked in a very specific pattern — the kind you learn to read after ten thousand hours of watching one, a spiderweb radiating from a single point of impact that a coach once told him meant the difference between a clutch and a choke. His teammates used to call a desync like that a *yuryeong* (유령) — a ghost — the input that should have landed and didn't, the frame that vanished between the click and the screen. He never found out which this crack was. He was mid-match, headset still on, when the light took him. The phone came with him. It doesn't turn on anymore. It doesn't need to.

## Description

A shattered smartphone, screen black and spiderwebbed, case worn soft at the corners from a hand that never quite put it down. The crack pattern doesn't move, exactly — but if Esca looks at it long enough mid-spell, faint blue-white lines seem to trace themselves along the same fracture lines his Arcane Deflection throws up under his skin, like the same signal running through two different circuits.

### Tier 1 — First Contact *(L3 — current)*

**Frame Advantage *(passive)*:** The first time each turn a creature is hit by one of Esca's spell attack rolls, or fails a saving throw against one of his spells, it takes 1d6 force damage. He's already seen the pattern before the effect lands — whether that's a blade of force or a wall of webbing.

**Practiced Opener *(passive)*:** The first spell Esca casts each combat gets the benefit of a read nobody else got to make: if it requires an attack roll, he has advantage on that roll; if it forces a saving throw, the first target has disadvantage on that save. Openings are the one thing he's never stopped reading correctly.

**Patch Notes *(1/Short Rest)*:** When Esca fails an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw, he may treat the die result as a 10 instead of the number rolled. Every build has a known exploit. This is his.
