# Session 08—Ash and Ice

## What the Four Were Holding

Fern's own night came the same way it had for the others, weeks apart and never quite the same shape twice. Four figures, too large to be anything but felt rather than seen, straining to hold up something that wanted badly to close. She went to the nearest one on instinct, the way she'd always gone toward anything burning rather than away from it, and touching it was like touching every fire she'd ever catalogued at once: careful where it wanted to be careless, banked where it wanted to spread. She asked what it needed. It let her in close enough to answer without words: flame needs rock to lean on, rock needs water it can't hold, water needs air to carry it anywhere at all. For a moment she wasn't watching the shape anymore, she was standing inside it, burning the way it burned.

Then the dream went somewhere older. A blacksmith too large for any forge she'd ever imagine, working a hammer over a shape made entirely of fire, and the fire didn't fight him. It said something that sounded, as near as a dream lets anything sound like anything, like *I accept,* and the hammer came down anyway, and the flame's own light didn't go out so much as spread, out and out, into a flat nothing of a plane that hadn't decided yet what it would become. She woke with her hands aching, warm at the palms, and no clean answer for how many hours the night had actually spent. Whatever she was holding when she woke, she didn't have words for it yet.

*Cross-reference: [[Gond]]'s "Making of Aenath," and [[Session 07 - The Missing Element|Session 07]]'s own forge dream: Samson watched a figure made of water accept the same hammer. Fern's own vision puts a name to the fire in it, later this same session, at a temple door that didn't want her.*

## Four Days Overland

The storm that caught her and [[!D'bleh|Gaslight]] short of the Wall left neither of them in any state to argue about it afterward. Their bat came down hard in country that had no name either of them knew, and the two riders who'd carried them decided, in the scramble that followed, not to wait around and find out how bad it was. Four days on foot after that: Fern reading water and wind the way she read anything living, Gaslight running his own habit of auditing the ground for what wanted to hurt them before it got the chance. Two strangers who'd known each other maybe twenty minutes before the fall found they didn't need much more than that to keep each other alive. When the Wall finally came into sight, Gaslight peeled off to check something on the approach he didn't like the look of, told her to keep walking, and hasn't been seen since. Fern reached Stormberg alone.

She'd had a week of it by the time this session actually starts, long enough that the worst of the disorientation had already worn off. The catching-up happened over breakfast and old stories, the Feast of the Moon's ghosts and unburnt names retold for a woman who'd missed all of it. Nobody in the room made her feel like she'd missed as much as she had.

## A Debt to Kossuth

Her own ten days ran the same shape the rest of the party's had. A near-perfect roll put her in front of Rhona asking after her own kind, and Rhona, who doesn't know what a changeling is either and has never asked, pointed her toward a fey enclave tucked inside the city's own streets: **[[Sylnathe's Hollow]]**, a district that smelled wrong before it looked wrong, green mist and old honey and hawthorn grown straight through cobblestones that had never asked for it. A satyr going by **[[Hazelwit]]** found her there before she found him, coin pouch going up and down in one hand like a held note he hadn't decided to finish. He wanted a trade, not a coin, not an object, "something you don't think you could ever lose," and after she talked her way past the parts of the deal she didn't like, she gave up her own ability to change shape, for the length of the week she'd already spent nine days into. He said he'd give it back. Whatever it was she'd been trying to catch in exchange never came home with her.

The vision itself confirmed nothing new under a nature and religion check beyond what she already half-knew: whatever she'd touched in that dream was real in a way faith usually isn't. What actually put a name to it was her own work track, offered up to a fire god's temple she found simply by following her own hands, still faintly warm days later. **Kossuth**'s shrine sat plainer than the others around it, tended by a man who didn't look made of fire so much as lit from underneath by it. She meant to help. What she managed instead was a sneeze, fey powder catching on him at exactly the wrong moment, that snuffed one of his sacred flames outright. He didn't shout so much as go very quiet and very final: out, and not welcome back. A second attempt at mending it, propped up by Roberto's guidance and a fair amount of liquid courage, rolled a bare one and closed the door a second time. She's Kossuth's own vision-bearer and Kossuth's own temple wants nothing more to do with her. Neither half of that has resolved.

## What Came Back Frostbitten

The ninth of Nightal opened on Samson and Keith shouldering through the common room door, still shaking in a way the warmth inside couldn't touch. Frostbite doesn't announce itself gently: Keith's cloak had gone stiff with ice at the hem, Samson's knuckles had lost their color past anything a normal winter explains. Between them they had a report, once the room stopped fussing over blankets long enough to hear it: a breach in the Wall dug clean through from the inside, twenty feet high and change; something cold and white-winged that filled a stretch of tunnel with air that felt deliberate rather than merely bitter; neither of them waiting around to learn more about it than that. Rhona bandaged them both to full health, heard the account, and put the breach up on the Guild's own board as a job, one more thing on a growing list she's clearly tired of writing reports for.

## Into the Hole, Again

The party went back the next day, the whole of it this time, Fern included. Keith didn't wait on the four days a proper crafting job would have cost him. He leaned on his own standing at the [[Iron Seam Guild]] instead, and came away with a rush-forged set of chainmail in exchange for a favor he now owes them, unspecified and outstanding. They chose to arrive after dark, against Rhona's own preference, and rode out half a day along a Wall that never stops being enormous no matter how much of it you've already seen.

The breach itself hadn't changed since Samson and Keith first found it: frost-rimmed, cold enough to leak visible mist even from outside. They took the narrower of two passages in, and it was Roberto and Fern's own eyes, flying above the rest, who spotted a small shape pressed into a side nook before it spotted them. **[[Meepo]]**, a kobold, and a frightened one, asked, before anything else, whether they were here to hurt her. Given jerky, a gentle question, and a persuasion roll that landed cleanly, he decided they weren't, and led them through a room of his own making: ice shelving rigged to give way underfoot, a trap he was plainly proud of, navigated clean by everyone who came through behind him.

## The Den

What waited in the fourth room wasn't the wyrmling Samson and Keith had glimpsed in passing. Whatever time has done to her since anyone last saw her, **[[Calcryx]]** has grown into a young dragon now, fifty feet of white scale filling most of the chamber's far wall, old scars still visible at her wrists and throat. She sighed before she spoke, not an attack, just breath too cold for a room already this cold, enough to cost everyone a level of exhaustion they hadn't been braced for, and once Meepo made it plain these particular two-legs weren't the kind that hurt anybody, she talked instead of fought.

She's not hiding from guilt. She's hiding from weather she can't fly through and a home she can't currently reach, and this hole in the Wall is the safest thing she's found since she left it looking for either. What the party worked out, piece by piece, is that her staying here isn't purely a cost: a dragon's presence works itself into the ground it occupies the longer it stays, and the ice she's already laid into this stretch of Wall is holding it up better than anything the work gangs have managed with mortar and obsidian. Samson, of all people, called it outright: better than concrete. The same effect that's reinforcing the Wall is also spreading, slow and patient, toward a stretch that will eventually run half a mile and touch anyone who has to walk through it.

The deal that came out of the negotiation is real, and it's hers to hold them to: she stays, the Wall gets stronger for it, and in exchange the party carries the case back to Stormberg: convince the Guild and the city to let her, and keep her fed on something other than the goodwill of anyone who might otherwise come looking for her with a spear. She doesn't perform gratitude. She counts debts the way she always has, and this is one more entry in a ledger she's kept since long before anyone here met her.

## What's Owed Now

Keith came out of the night having leveled up, the only one of the five who did. Something passed between Kili and Fern in the same stretch of time that neither one has named yet, felt rather than seen, the kind of thing that doesn't resolve just because nobody's asked about it out loud. Whatever it means, it's theirs to define later.

Everything the party carried into this session that didn't touch Fern's return or the breach is still exactly where it was left: Verenthal's forum, Cinderjaw's offer, Rhona's training, Corwyn's angle on the Loom, Keith's Shar research, Roberto's own quiet hunch about the Choir. Six threads, still live, still waiting on whichever one the table reaches for next.
