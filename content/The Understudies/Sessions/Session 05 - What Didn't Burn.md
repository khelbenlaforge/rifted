# Session 05 — What Didn't Burn

## The Square, Before Midnight

The square outside [[Stormberg]]'s Temple of Kelemvor filled slow and quiet. Robed figures of every race stood under white walls carved with a man in peasant's clothes, a strange image for a god of death that none of them could explain yet. Knights and monks and priests moved through the crowd bearing the same bone-and-scales symbol. Word came down the line the way it always does at a festival too large for anyone to fully explain: at midnight the First Gravelord himself would give the blessing in Crystal City, and every town in Corranor would sync to it at once. Kili asked a young priest for something to remember the lost by. He came back flustered, whispered urgently to the monks, and returned with parchment and an apology. *You're new here, aren't you.*

## The Names

**Esca** took a piece of paper, looked at it a long moment, and tore it in half in front of everyone. *I do not want to commemorate or remember anyone from my past life. They are dead to me.* Saying it, his face slid past disgust into something colder. The priest, a young man the party would come to know as **[[Balder Skeld]]**, went visibly aghast. **[[Rhona Voss]]** stepped in without much fuss. She caught his collar in one hand and drew him back, more steadying him than restraining him. *I don't know what you went through. I'm not going to judge you. Just — not in front of everyone.* Esca let himself be pulled back and let it happen. Afterward Rhona patted his shoulder and told him the offer to talk still stood.

**Kili** took the opening in the other direction entirely. Mourning wasn't the play; she skipped it, turned to Balder Skeld, and charmed him with a warm half-tease about hoping one day to have someone worth grieving that hard for. He took it kindly, thanked her, and told her she'd be held in the temple's prayers regardless.

**Keith** wrote a name down and gave it to the monks without telling anyone whose it was. Nobody there could have told the difference, and some part of him needed to put it on the list anyway. **Roberto** gave one name: someone dead, explicitly *not myself*. Two names went to the monks that night. They were chanted together.

The rest of the square did the same, one by one, until what had been a long line dwindled to whoever wouldn't leave before the reading finished.

## Names in the Sky

At midnight the noise of the square stopped all at once. The moon came full, and Aenath's other three moons lit with it. The monks held their parchments in a wide meditative silence while the priests began a rhymed, rising chant, in a language none of the party understood and that didn't translate even to Roberto's ear.

The papers began to glow. One by one they lifted from the monks' hands and burned fast and clean, the flame gone again almost as soon as it caught. From the burning, names rose into the sky and gathered into long dense columns of script, more of them pouring up from the south than anyone in Stormberg's square could account for. Esca watched with a wizard's eye and felt the unmistakable pull of divination magic under whatever else was happening. Keith clocked something too: the same southern light the party had seen that same night, now confirmed as the same working, grown larger.

Roberto saw the most. Two pieces of paper never burned and never rose, left sitting in the monks' hands while the rest went up. As the names climbed, translucent figures rose out of the crowd itself, some of them clearly not aware of where they were going, blind and drifting until the chant turned their heads toward a name. One by one they reached theirs and struck it. Gold light closed around some. A clean burn took others in flame, or in colors he had no other word for. Nobody else in the square could see any of it. When the wave of names finished rising, a handful of those figures were still standing there, unclaimed, until the chant itself stopped and they simply weren't. Whatever the two unburnt papers mean, they weren't the only thing that failed to resolve that night.

The full rite ran the better part of an hour. When it finally broke, the whole square let out one collective breath, and the drinking and the music started at once.

## The Turn of the Year

That same night, before the party turned in, [[Rhona Voss]] walked them through Corranor's calendar. Twenty-four-hour days. Ten-day weeks. Three weeks to a month, every month a clean thirty days, and then the days that don't fit anywhere — the intercalary days, sitting outside all of it. The Feast of the Moon was one; the last had been High Harvest Time, between summer and autumn. They were entering **Nightal** now, the start of winter, and the cold was already finding them where they stood in the square.

## Four Directions

The next day, Rhona made good on her introductions and split the party across Stormberg to let each of them follow what actually interested them.

**Esca** went to the **[[Ward Works]]**, the Ether Scryer's Academy's Stormberg branch: a cramped, humming annex near the Wallwatch at the city's center, not built into the Wall itself. **[[Ostrun Kell]]**, the dwarf overseeing the floor, came across plain-spoken and a little put-upon this early in the day. She warmed up fast once she saw genuine interest and gave Esca a real tour — instruments humming at different pitches, alchemists testing materials nearby. One test sample of obsidian-colored concrete detonated loudly enough to send a burst of flame across the room while Esca watched — a materials test, the actual Stormwall untouched by any of it. The Ward Works' library ran heavy to abjuration theory and alchemy, his to browse in downtime once Rhona cleared it. Ostrun mentioned, almost in passing, that the Wall isn't just architecture. It's an active magical project, maintained continuously against whatever the Arctic and the storm keep throwing at it.

**Kili** asked Rhona for something closer to ground level than the Guild's own job board. She got walked southeast, near the fields, to a noodle stall run by **[[Yenna Marrow|Yenna]]**: a fast-moving line, a huge pot, a woman feeding the morning rush as fast as she could ladle. Kili worked the line and picked up genuine goodwill for pulling her weight. Then she spent the rest of the morning drawing out the neighborhood's real opinions — on taxes, on the Verenthal (or "Barenthal"; nobody in the party ever got a clean spelling) noble house, on how little the common folk actually see of the people funding the wall that keeps them alive. A well-rolled persuasion check made an already-irritated table of laborers more vocal than they'd started. Kili closed the morning offering the group a shared bowl of noodles as an act of "true harmony." Mostly a joke. Not quite entirely one.

**Roberto** had three places to spend his day and used most of them. Rhona walked him first to Stormberg's own modest chapel of Lathander. Sister **[[Sister Priya Dawnwell|Priya Dawnwell]]** met him there, younger than he expected, humming off-key and entirely unbothered by it. She recognized his god's symbol before he said a word. She told him what his church stands for out here: renewal, healing, and a plain kind of faith — what happens to a soul after death is between it and the sun, nobody else's business to manage. Asked about his arrival vision, she confirmed what he'd started to suspect — he'd been called by Helm and by Sune too, not Lathander alone. She asked Rhona, in his hearing, whether there was word yet of "the ancient floating temple of Lathander recently discovered." The party already knows that place as [[The Sundered Choir]], though neither Priya nor Rhona has made the connection yet. Standing in last night's ceremony, Roberto had already realized something himself: he wasn't the only Lathanderite in that crowd. Lay worship in Stormberg, confirmed before he'd set foot in a chapel at all. He went on afterward to the Temple of Kelemvor, where Brother **[[Brother Silas|Silas]]** — one of the few monks not given the Feast's traditional day off — walked him through the old doctrine of the Wailing Wall, the godless souls once mortared into it before Kelemvor broke it down and promised every soul somewhere to go, worshipper or not. Silas confirmed, carefully, that Lathander has no claim on a soul that never pledged to him. Priya was sitting right there when he said it. The line landed.

**Keith** pushed past the Wallwatch's own job board toward the artisan and manufacturing side of the city, then never actually got there — he stayed with Rhona over breakfast, and came away with the fullest civic picture of anyone for it. Rhona walked him through Stormberg's economic triangle: the Academy funding research, House Verenthal running the mills and forges, and the Blackstaff family (Blackstar, in one telling; the name shifted more than once) bankrolling the rare floating-stones that keep Stormberg's airships aloft. Nobody in the room had a name for the mineral yet. He also learned about the **[[Iron Seam Guild]]**, the artificers and engineers who keep the wall's non-magical machinery running: lifts, scaffolding, siege engines, diversion works for creatures the storm drives against the Wall. Rhona described them as working "in conjunction, but not under" the Academy — the Academy and its alchemists produce the materials, and Iron Seam's artificers put them to use. A natural home, she pointed out, for an artificer looking for real work.

## What Rhona Heard

As the light faded, Rhona sat the party down with a stone pressed to her ear. Confusion crossed her face first. Then concern. Then something closer to dread. Word had reached her from the Guild in Crystal City: some of the Rifted there had been attacked by the **Cult of [[Shar]]**, [[Selûne]]'s antithesis, and the **First Gravelord** had, apparently, been kidnapped and nearly sacrificed on an altar to her — during the very ceremony the party had just watched play out from the other side of the continent. She struggled to get the details right as she relayed them, plainly aware she was out of her depth.

She also passed on something stranger. The party's arrival wasn't just unexplained; it might be unprecedented. The tiefling who'd brought them here, **[[Layla]]**, had worked as an assistant to a member of the Council of Seven named **[[Garm]]**, "the Keeper of the Spire," researching ways to reinstate teleportation. As far as anyone in Corranor knows, no such magic has ever existed in this world, whether between cities or across whatever gap brought seven strangers in from another one entirely. Layla vanished from Crystal City around the time the first group of Rifted arrived there, and surfaced again only when this batch did. There was a more concrete lead too: reagents drawn from the blood of planar beings, tieflings and aasimar, are apparently able to punch a hole through space and time, and Crystal City has a black market trading in exactly those reagents that nobody's been able to trace. Nothing like it has turned up in Stormberg yet. Whether that stays true is something the party knows to watch for now.

## The Test

With the day's business done, Rhona led the group — minus [[Samson Flint]], still out with the wall's work gangs — through a shimmering doorway into what looked like an endless training ground. *The Guild's work is dangerous,* she told them. *I need to know what you can actually do.* She pulled a greatsword one-handed off her back, empty-handed on the other side where a shield would go. Then she called for initiative.

The roll landed, Esca leading with a 31. The fight itself didn't happen. It's held for next session, along with the party's read of her: practiced. Dangerous. Visibly reluctant to hurt newcomers.
