# Session 09—Ice in the Ledger

## The Den, Settled

Calcryx breathed out again, slower than the sigh that had cost everyone a level of exhaustion the night before, and this time most of it stayed in her chest. The cold stayed in the room anyway. She lay curled against the far wall with her tail along the stone like a second foundation, and where it touched the Wall the black glaze had cracked. White frost ran into every crack and forked outward like lightning that had frozen mid-strike. Some of the veins were thicker than an arm.

[[Meepo]] stood between the party and the dragon with his chest puffed out. He had brought them here and wanted her to know it. Calcryx went down the row of them with the eyes of someone reading a ledger. She stayed, she said. She didn't like their people. The party could make its deal with the people in the city, and they would keep her fed. To [[Fern]] she added that there would always be something for the hoard, and that she would remember exactly what had been said. Nobody had named an amount.

The longer they lingered, the harder the cold worked on them. The DM set a Constitution save against another level of exhaustion for anyone who stayed on. Calcryx gave [[Fern]] a scale, a molted one.

## Proof

The city would want something it could hold. A block of ice would be a puddle by the time it reached the Wall, and ice is ice. [[Roberto]] tried Arcana (a 19) to conjure an image of the den, but nobody present had anything stronger than a minor illusion, and the party's wizard was back at the Academy with his head in a book. [[Kili]] could draw. She rolled a 22 on Dexterity, hobbyist work but enough for the gist, and the other two told her what to put in: the Wall, the glowing ice crystals hanging in the air, Calcryx against the stone. For a minute the table wondered whether a sudden level-up and an artist's feat would have helped. Nobody took it.

Somebody suggested an open house, with the nobles invited down to see the dragon. [[Keith Poe|Keith]] rolled a natural 20 on Insight and saw exactly how that would end. Calcryx had already driven Samson and Keith out once, and she did not want visitors. Kili, whose own Insight roll was a natural 1, dug in her heels anyway.

The table wanted her footprint on paper, a signature that would show a working relationship. He conjured a sheet of cartographer's paper with Tinker's Magic (Intelligence plus Cartographer's Tools, a 16), and the DM ruled that a young dragon's foot runs two to three feet across. Roberto, meanwhile, asked about her family and whether dragons were distant cousins of birds. That was a natural 1. Calcryx took a breath in, told them to get out, and everyone rolled a Constitution save. Keith's was a 9. Everyone took another level of exhaustion and slower speeds with it. Keith had his footprint before the dragon lost her temper.

Meepo offered his own room. The party slept there beside the frozen rats, and the long rest took a level of exhaustion off. Roberto was down to one. Meepo never shivered in any of it. Roberto's Medicine check came to a 9 and told him nothing, and the DM's answer was that something was either shielding Meepo from the cold or slowly changing him. By morning Keith's paper, and the print on it, had vanished with the long rest.

## Rhona, With a Scale

They reached Stormberg around three in the afternoon of the eleventh of Nightal. [[Fern]] went off to the fae community in the city, and Samson stayed with the others. [[Rhona Voss]] was at the Wallwatch for once, out of her armor, the sword on its rack, and she asked whether anyone wanted to catch her up. They handed over the scale and Kili's drawing. Rhona looked at the drawing and was impressed.

Her eyebrows rose with every sentence. A dragon in the Wall, a young one, and she called it an adolescent, which she said could eat any of them in one bite. [[Samson Flint]] and Keith both confirmed that Calcryx's ice held better than the patchwork, and that it kept seeping into the smaller cracks. Keith offered to draw up a cost-benefit analysis of what the Wall's upkeep on that stretch ran each month. When the terms of the deal were read out, Rhona's face fell line by line. Food she could see. Treasure was another matter, and nobody had named a figure. She wasn't angry. She was working out how to explain it to a room of nobles.

Kili proposed that the nobles go and talk to the dragon, and Rhona leaned toward it like rain from heaven. Nothing would have pleased her more than sending them to settle it themselves. Keith did not like it. Any noble who died in that cave would be on the party's account, and dragon or no dragon, nobody would care whose teeth did it. He talked her down. Dragons don't like trespassers, Rhona admitted, and she would send nobody until it was certain to be safe.

She laid out the local lay of the land while she was at it. Influence in Stormberg ran through three hands: the Verenthal nobles, the Magocracy's representative, and the [[Ether Scryer's Academy]] working alongside the [[Iron Seam Guild]]. Rhona expected the Academy to be delighted to study a dragon. The Iron Seam loved building things to kill them, so she meant to keep it out of this. Stormberg had only ever driven dragons off. A ballista with magical ammunition on the Wall was painful enough that adolescents didn't press the matter, and no ancient dragon had ever come by. She would call a meeting within a few days with everyone at the table, and until then the work gangs were not to hear a word about it.

## What Stormberg Eats

That evening [[Kili]] took a different question to [[Yenna Marrow]]: how does a city that might be a floating rock feed itself? The rock goes down at landfall, Yenna reminded her, and the fishing boats go out again. The forests on either side keep hunters and trappers busy, but it was winter. Hunting had nearly stopped, the fields lay fallow, and the city was eating through its stores. Yenna bought the lesser cuts cheap from trappers for the laborers who ate at her stall, and she offered to ask her wife, who knew some of them. Kili said she was buying for the speakeasy. The price would have to be good enough, because they had a living to make.

## The Iron Seam

Keith went to the Iron Seam to sound out his colleagues, and the man he owed for the chainmail was there. [[Brannoc Tull]], broad and soot to the elbows, looked embarrassed to be collecting from a friend. He asked for a copy of the Wall readings Keith had taken on an earlier survey, and they would be square. Keith's Arcana roll was thin, and Brannoc took the readings as confirmation of what the Guild already knew. He said to send them his way soon.

Keith asked who signed off on the budgets. The Iron Seam, Brannoc said, was paid by the Magocracy together with the Academy, according to output. The noble houses dropped in now and then to act noble, and the Guild sent them upstairs. Its guildmaster was usually away on research. For all their airs, the nobles invested in the city and gave people work, and Brannoc was grateful for that.

Brannoc told him the archives were his to go through. Keith wanted plans for four things: a +1 shield, Sending Stones, an Alchemy Jug and a Bag of Holding. Brannoc rated them by difficulty, with the Stones the hardest. Keith rolled a 25, and the DM let him leave with the plans for the Alchemy Jug and the Bag of Holding. Someone had the plans for the Stones, but finding them would take work, and the shield could be asked about. The Bag of Holding got made. Strength was low across the party, the DM warned, and encumbrance would start to count.

## A Day Before the Meeting

Keith reported what Brannoc had told him. The Verenthals were in charge of the dock workers, the Magocracy stood behind the Iron Seam's output, and the Academy did the enchanting. Rhona filled in the rest. The Magocracy ran from a council of seven archmages, and anyone seated on it renounced every tie to the house or school they came from, though, Rhona admitted, you never know for sure how faithfully. One seat was held by a Blackstaff, from Corran's line. Noble houses governed like fiefs. The Magocracy leased the land, and the houses had to make it pay with forges, mills and kilns, and pay workers who were free to leave. Almost every city had one noble house attached to it, every house kept a manor in Crystal City, and the Blackstaffs held no territory at all.

Kili's night in the Guild's ledgers turned up the rest. The budget came down from headquarters in Crystal City, and a memo from Istar to Rhona at the bottom said they were broke, and to save what she could. Money came in from culling work, mercenary jobs, big bounties from the city, the Magocracy's retainer, jobs for the Academy, and northern expeditions for the stones that keep airships flying. A good share of it went back out to pay the members. The ledger roughly balanced. She named the people above her: [[Istar Silverlock|Istar]], the assistant guildmaster; [[Erky]], the guildmaster; [[Luks Borg]], a half-orc lieutenant. None of them was a Rifted, and neither was anyone else on the Guild's upper rungs.

She had gossip, too. The Council had wanted the Rifted for a hearing about the shadow creatures, and officially the Guild had sided with the Council. Unofficially Istar told his people to stay out of politics, and they left to follow a lead in the wilderness north of Crystal City and south of Stormberg. Other Rifted had landed in Coldford and Westward, every group wearing Guild bracers and all of them sent straight to the Guild. Whoever was behind it was smart enough never to use the same place twice. When Roberto asked whether she knew where Layla might have found the bracers, Rhona had no answer, but she knew more about [[Layla]] than she had said before. Layla had been an assistant to [[Garm]], and Garm was the Guild's patron on the Council. Roberto caught real warmth in her voice for him. Garm trusted too much, she said, but he was a good administrator. Layla had vanished from the city right after the first Rifted were dumped into it. Kili had been in those ledgers all night, and Roberto, awake at four in the morning, found her still at them.

## Quill's Office

Roberto went to see the man from the docks, whose name, Rhona had told them, was Quill. His office smelled of brine and lamp oil and roses, and Quill sat behind a desk of rosters with his hair swept up in a coif like a duck's bill, a rose pinned to his coat. The forum had slipped his mind, and Roberto, who had brought some of his own brewing, reminded him. Quill said he never forgot anything he had paid for.

The forum would be at the end of Nightal, in Quill's own office, and Roberto was now running it: the RSVPs, the agenda, all of it. Quill would send money to the Guild for the work in advance. Quill said pay is a sum, and sums have limits. He could raise one line in a ledger by lowering another. If the workers wanted hazard pay, they would have to say which line to lower. Roberto suggested looking for savings in what it cost to repair and maintain the Wall. Quill listed his costs: equipment, meals at the Wall, transport when he could manage it, and the salaries of his administrators. He loved saying the word ledger.

Roberto read him on a 28. Quill disliked trouble, liked being obeyed and liked money. He was evasive about the real reason families in the city got the safer posts, and the Guild irritated him, since it took noble commissions but was no one's to command. His servants looked well treated: a platoon of them making tea and polishing brass. When Roberto asked for a copy of the ledgers, Quill said his servants handled the numbers, and that Roberto could talk to one of them during the week but could not take anything away. Roberto made a note to bring Kili along to decipher them.

## Dragons, and a Chapel

Keith spent the morning on dragons at the Academy, where Esca gave him advantage, and rolled a 26 on Investigation. Dragons are intelligent and magical, and the chromatic ones are selfish and never work together, which is why none of them has taken the world. A dragon's lair grows with its age, reaching miles for an old one, on a scale of a century or more. Stormberg held very little on tribute in 222 years. Hoards ran from coin to art to magic and rare materials, and Calcryx's pile of leather armor, swords and tooth necklaces was a very modest beginning. Every so often, a dragon gets bored and comes to look.

That afternoon Keith joined Roberto at the chapel. [[Sister Priya Dawnwell]] had just blessed a birth and came in with bread and dried goods, half-elven, in her early twenties, her hair cropped short and uneven from cutting it herself. Roberto made his pitch: the Guild was about to propose a cheaper and safer way to reinforce the Wall. Priya said the temples had little sway in politics. She would speak up at the forum if he wanted her, and he could speak for the chapel at the meeting with the Magocracy, as long as he remembered who they were. Roberto floated a pop-up worship service with free food and drink, and asked for priests and paladins to attend the forum and offer blessings. Other clergy watched Roberto from the doorway, and the owl turned his whole head to look back.

Keith asked about Shar. The chapel kept no records and had no library, Priya said, and he might try Oghma's temple. The [[Selûne|Selûnites]] sat nearer Mystra's temple, in the corner of a small temple district. Shrines for gods without clergy stood together in one complex, and a shrine to Shar was among them. She told them what she had heard about the Cult of Shar in Crystal City: their real aim had been to disrupt a ceremony, not to hunt the Rifted, and the Rifted there had somehow saved a great lord from the attackers. In the temple's own stories, she added, [[Lathander]] had once been married to the first goddess of luck. Shar killed her, the goddess was split in two as [[Tymora]] and Beshaba, and Lathander took another wife, a goddess of growth. The stories taught lessons more than they kept facts, she said, though there might be some truth in them. Visions of the gods happened, but no cleric had ever spoken to one.

## The Salt Line

[[Kili]] went looking for the Wall workers who went farthest out. The afternoon's errand led to [[The Salt Line]], a damp dockside cabin with a low ceiling black with lamp smoke and the smell of brine and wet wool. A chalk tally board by the door counted the days since the last injury on a far-ground shift, and it read three. This was where the far-ground crews drank when they weren't at her speakeasy: closer to the gates, cheaper, rougher.

She found [[Oskar Brell]] there on a break, a green-tinged orc in his late thirties or early forties, built broad, his brown hair streaked white, his hands callused from rope, a bandage on his forearm. Kili put a meal from Yenna's in front of him and gave him a coupon for the speakeasy. Twenty years a far rigger, he said, with a wife who helped at the mills and no children. The arm had come from a slip on the rope, coming down the Wall. The far crews went out four days and rested a week a month, in small settlements the supervisors sent workers to build. He'd love to work nearer the city, he said, but there were rules. Kili's Insight (a 17) found a spark when he said it. Something might be in the works for him, and he would never say so in front of the man beside him. Kili whispered that one stretch of the Wall was safe now, that something was guarding it.

## The Night Before

In the evening Rhona got them together for a quick word. She had Magister Vestra Coln of the Magocracy and Zarantha Verenthal into a meeting. The head of the Iron Seam was away, and the Academy's proctor, who only cared about his books, had told Rhona to handle it, so she would stand for the Academy too. Zarantha headed House Verenthal's Stormberg branch, ran the mills and forges, and funded the Wall's work. Rhona warned them not to be fooled by her warmth, since there was a lot of ice in her. Quill was her nephew and took most of the flak. Vestra, Rhona said, was hands-off by policy and would be reserved. Start with the facts as they stand, Rhona said, and push for things afterward, because an opinion put in front of people too soon turns into a fact in their heads. She liked the temple's support and wanted the Lathanderites represented. She'd need alcohol later.

The party talked it through. The dragon could stay until it became a problem. It would be hard to keep hidden for long, and the cold would spread. The workers on that stretch were about to lose their work, so the pitch would have to name new work: feeding the dragon, hauling for it, perhaps a new industry around the ice. The meeting was a day off.

## What's Owed Now

The deal has been carried to the first person who had to hear it, and she will carry it further. No one has named a price for the hoard or found the food to fill the den. The work gangs were told nothing, and Kili's whisper to one of them has already gone out. Keith owes the readings, and still wants the Sending Stone plans, the Shield plans, and some answer on Shar. Roberto has a forum to build at the end of the month. Rhona's offer to teach Kili to fight and Cinderjaw's invitation stayed untouched, and Corwyn came up only as a possible supply route. Keith named a big one: why they were teleported here at all.
