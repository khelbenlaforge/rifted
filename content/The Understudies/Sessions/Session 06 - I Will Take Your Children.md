# Session 06 — I Will Take Your Children

## Three Dreams

Sleep took [[Kili]], [[Roberto]], and [[Keith Poe]] the same way it always did, and let go of none of them cleanly.

Kili's dream had no ground under it. Translucent wings cut past her in a blur of colour that her own body wasn't wearing: colourless, skin gone nearly to glass, everything around her lit from somewhere she couldn't find. Pixies flitted close enough to touch. Small horned faces, more goat than devil, bounded past calling her by a name that wasn't Kili at all. A voice she didn't recognize but loved anyway filled the noise; a Queen, the small creatures called her, and the word alone filled the dream with a warmth that had nothing to do with anything Kili from Earth had ever felt. *We can't go back to the material realm anymore.* She reached for horns of her own and grew them without asking how. Then the forest changed under her. An ovoid portal burned at its edges in front of her, and past it two colossal shapes met in a clash that read on some level deeper than sound, one silver as the moon, one a dark gunmetal grey. The shockwave of their meeting put her on her back before she understood she'd been standing. The dream didn't end there. She came to among dying fairies, a portal closing behind her a heartbeat too late. A towering golden-haired figure planted himself between them and bolts of dark energy that wilted whole stands of trees where they struck; Corellon, someone near her said the name. *Children, leave this place. Leave. We will hold the line.* Elves stepped forward one at a time and knelt, offering their own light up to him; each sacrifice folded into the bow forming in his hands, brighter with every soul it took.

Roberto's dream put him back in robes he half-recognized, gold filigree at the cuffs, a morning-sun symbol on his chest. Around him, a temple full of people dressed the same way, and every one of them in some state of collapse. A man he knew, though the name wouldn't come, only the certainty he'd known this man a long time, tried a simple light cantrip and failed, and folded to his knees. *Our Lord is gone. They've been kicked out of the heavens.* Roberto reached for his own holy symbol and found nothing where his god should have been — a direction he could still point to, just nothing there to touch. The dream carried him forward — a caravan of tired, dust-caked Lathanderite clerics trudging through wasteland, then a mortal figure ahead of them flickering gold and human by turns, clerics running to fall at his feet while something in Roberto held back to watch first and feel later. Then night, and the most beautiful staircase he had ever seen, circling up out of the ground toward a sky his god was climbing. He wept before he realized he'd started. An unnamed voice broke the staircase apart mid-ascent: *Thou hast mistreated your powers and your stations. Thou art not to be trusted.* His god fell, and so did others beside him, and the voice that shattered the stairs said the only thing it needed to say. *I will take your children.* Roberto felt his own soul lift out of him before the vision let him go.

Keith woke inside someone else's burned body, taller than his own frame, the whole left side of it singed past feeling. The smell of sulfur and smoke was everywhere, and a lady knelt over him insisting he not cast anything else. He'd only meant to conjure a simple campfire; the fault lay with the spell itself. The gods were walking Aenath, she told him, and it was doing something to magic everywhere. He spent the dream on crutches, gathering maps with peers who all looked younger than they should, marking down where each of the old powers had gone: Sune here, Corellon and Sehanine racing to get the fae home to the Feywild, Helm standing the celestial stairway, Sehanine and Lolth somehow locked in open battle.

The dream drew all three of them together for its close, at a second stairway none of them had seen before. Lathander stood before it, arguing his own case to return; a figure carrying two shields — Helm — blocked him. *You dare approach when you have lost what you were supposed to keep.* Lathander protested it wasn't their fault, that the tablets had been stolen from them by trickery. Helm didn't move. *I will not let you return. My business is to protect every single staircase.* Word reached them, distantly, that a new goddess of magic had been reborn, and that it changed nothing — souls were still being siphoned to other worlds even as they watched. Keith caught himself mid-prayer to her, and felt his own soul pulled loose the same way Roberto's already had.

Roberto and Keith had each felt their own soul pulled loose before the vision let them go; Kili's simply went black, no gentler an ending than theirs. All three woke the next morning with the images intact and no idea what any of it meant. Whether it had been dream, memory, or something else entirely was left for each of them to work out on their own.

## Six Years

The party's second day in Stormberg closed the way the first had opened: with Rhona. She led the group — minus [[Samson Flint]], still out with the wall's work gangs — through a shimmering doorway into the pocket-dimension arena where Session 5 had left them mid-initiative, and picked the fight back up where it stopped.

She drew her greatsword one-handed and gave no ground for the first several rounds, parrying and retaliating in equal measure while the party found their footing against her. [[Esca]] opened with a mage hand gripped around her ankle for difficult terrain, then blanketed part of the arena in web, catching an ally in its difficult terrain along with his intended target, before a dagger finally found her later in the fight. His player wasn't at the table, so the party more or less improvised his choices between them; the slippery, reluctant-to-engage read they settled on felt right enough to keep. Roberto flew above the fray casting Shield of Faith, then a lair effect caught him twenty feet up and dropped him. No padding on the damage, no special mercy for a flier who'd left the ground. Furious about it, he stood back up and answered with Scorching Ray. Kili refused outright to hit her: a Vicious Mockery that actually connected, Silvery Barbs mostly spent granting others advantage, healing words for whoever needed them, one failed attempt to talk the whole exercise to a stop with a Suggestion of her own. Anything but a weapon aimed at the woman she'd already started calling Mom. Rhona noticed, and worked the hesitation into her own read of the fight: *Think of me as a bear coming at you in the woods. Are you going to just stand there?*

Samson was there from the opening roll of initiative, joining the call online mid-vision but well before the fight itself began, and did more damage than anyone else at the table — an Echo Knight's extra blade finding purchase again and again where his own swing missed, a Necrotic Shroud fright effect burning through Rhona's second legendary resistance, until a double strike from Rhona put him flat on the ground. She caught him going down and laid him out gently, careful with the flat of the blade rather than the edge. Keith's own catapulted caltrop had already cost her the first; Kili's second attempt at a laughter spell cost her the third and last, late in the fight, closer than either of them expected. Keith's own offense petered out well before the end — a last lightning bolt parried clean, nothing left to try but standing between Kili and the fight. It was Esca — still the table's collective read of him rather than his own player's — who closed it out: a landed Suggestion telling her to *stand down*, then one final Silvery Barbs that put her rigid and grinning through gritted teeth.

She ended the fight herself, dropping to one knee at four hit points with her sword laid down. *You guys seem good enough for Brass.* They'd already held that rank since Session 4 — this fight just proved they'd earned it. She let the party heal her afterward without much protest, and admitted, sitting bruised at the guild's common table with ale in front of everyone, that this was the first real fight she'd had in six years. *I was supposed to have retired. I watch kids come in and out, and sometimes we lose kids along the way. I do not want to lose any more of you.* She noticed, too, what nobody in the room had bothered to hide from her: every one of their bonded items carried something beyond the ordinary. A counterpart of hers in Crystal City — [[Luks Borg]], relaying word through the Guild's own channels — had apparently flagged the same thing from a distance. Rhona promised to keep it quiet on the party's behalf, for their own safety as much as anyone else's.

## What Rhona Told Them

Winding down for the night, Rhona answered whatever questions the party still had left in them.

Corranor's wall opens for shipping only on landfall — the moment the flying continent descends back to the ocean and the sea gates can unseal. Until then, nothing comes or goes by water. White dragons live in the cold reaches to the north, and while the old ones mostly keep to themselves, the young and belligerent occasionally test the wall for sport; the engineers keep ballistas mounted for exactly that. The wall itself can't be broken from outside — but Rhona was quick to add that things can still eat their way in from within, which is half of what the Wallwatch actually does. A southern continent exists, confirmed and reachable, though nobody present had seen it themselves: Crystal City sent an expedition out a couple of months back on one of the realm's only two crystal-powered airships. Teleportation, she confirmed again, has no business working in this world at all — which made it worse, not better, that someone had apparently found a way to make people cross into it anyway.

She closed the night by laying out downtime — ten days, three tracks available (Relationships, Work, Investigation), each roll harder than the last and each failure costing a point of exhaustion for the week. Level 4 was on the table for anyone who wanted it, with the guild's own house allowance to bank multiple prepared options and commit only when the moment called for it. Nobody actually locked in a downtime pick tonight; the fight had eaten more of the session than anyone planned for, and the tracks themselves are due next time the party sits down, the 9th.
