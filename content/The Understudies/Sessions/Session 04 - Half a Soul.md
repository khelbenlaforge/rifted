# Session 04 — Half a Soul

## Digbeth, One Last Morning

Six-thirty, grey light that never quite committed to being morning over the Digbeth canal. Samson was three lifts up a scaffold outside a warehouse conversion, hauling cement bags with Dean, who'd spent six months half-convinced something wasn't right about how clean his load calcs came out. Dean asked him to explain it — the joist, the two-inch call with no tape measure in sight. Samson didn't have an answer worth giving.

The coupler went before he had to find one. Not a bang, just a wrong-sounding creak, and then Dean was underneath the drop line. Samson moved before he decided to, caught him — and the grey Birmingham morning stopped being solid at all.

The blue that took its place peeled back on a hellscape: a spike of pure white light a thousand feet high, flying humanoids circling it, a black-clad mage and four constructs holding the base against falling rock. Samson sent his Echo to intercept a boulder aimed at the mage and failed. The constructs held anyway. The rocks closed in, the spike went dark, and the vision buried Samson alongside everyone else caught in it — the same fall the Door Kickers know as Corran's last stand.

He came to just long enough to be dragged through a portal into [[The Hexad]] by a Fused Shadow — and then, some stretch of time he still couldn't account for, he was walking again, under his own power, out of the Hollowreach with four people he hadn't properly met yet.

## The Bracers

A shape peeled off the dark overhead before the trees had fully thinned — Esca, off a giant bat before it had finished landing, already talking. He'd come from the goblins with five brass bracers, Guild-marked, and a message to wear them before the gate — nothing about who'd sent word ahead, just that someone had. He handed them out without much ceremony.

The walk to Stormberg gave the five of them the first real stretch of road together: no combat, just conversation, Samson meeting the others properly for the first time.

## Three Moons and a Long Wall

Past midnight, the ordinary moon simply wasn't there. In its place: one light burning pure white, one dark and arterial red, one black enough it shouldn't have registered against the sky at all — and did anyway. Everyone felt the same stillness land at once, like something enormous had just taken a breath. Far south, past the curve of the world toward Crystal City, a thin crescent of gold motes lifted and danced, the same shape and size as the dark half of the rising moon — the same night the Door Kickers were living through in their own corner of Corranor.

Roberto flew up ahead of the group and got the full shape of it: a flat world, the Stormwall running the length of the northern coast, and Stormberg itself a scatter of lights small enough to miss at the wall's base. The storm hammering the wall looked like violent aurora sheets, rolling in from the edge of the world itself.

## Guild-Marked

Stormberg's outer gate was lit for trouble — torches doubled, two watchmen holding a shift they clearly hadn't chosen. A hand drifted toward a belt and then eased back off it the moment the bracers caught torchlight. *Guild-marked. Brass.* That earned them a pass and directions: three streets in, straight to the Wallwatch, don't wander. The town around them was mid-Feast — names of the dead repeated in doorways, a line already forming to hand parchment names to Kelemvor's shrine, the same rite Crystal City had kept weeks before.

The Wallwatch was one room doing the work of four: a lamp, a contracts board nobody had cleared in a while, and **Rhona Voss** behind the counter, Stormberg's Branch Leader, running the desk alone. Her eyes went straight to the bracers. *Brass.* A short laugh with no real humor in it, then the question that mattered: *who sent you?*

## What Rhona Knew

The moment English came out of someone's mouth, Rhona's guarded procedure gave way to open curiosity — the Guild in Crystal had already warned her a batch like this might turn up. What followed was less an interrogation than an unburdening: Corranor, the Magocracy, the Council of Seven, the Blackstaff family whose founder gave the whole kingdom its name, the noble houses angling for standing, the academy that keeps the Wall's magic running. Nothing she hadn't said to strangers before. Everything the five of them needed to hear.

One piece landed harder than the rest. Word had reached Stormberg from Crystal City: the Rifted carry only half a soul each, and the other half grows in only the longer they stay in Aenath. Rhona passed it on as plainly as everything else she knew, one more line in the induction rather than a confession. Nobody in the room understood yet what it meant. She offered them the same deal the Guild had given the first Rifted in Crystal: a cot, a meal, coin against future work, and the freedom to look into whatever they needed to.

## Settling In

Still deep in the small hours of the Feast, Rhona walked the five of them to a portal at the end of her own hallway — a pocket dimension like the Guild kept in Crystal City, five doors to rooms they could make their own, and a common room where a floating disk filled itself with whatever anyone asked for. Five of the seven, in one place, with a roof over their heads and nobody trying to kill them.
