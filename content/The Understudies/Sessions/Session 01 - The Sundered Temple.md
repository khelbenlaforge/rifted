# Session 01 — The Sundered Temple

## Four Doors Closing

Four lives ended mid-sentence, an ocean and a continent apart from each other, all at once.

In Stellenbosch, [[The Exodus/The Understudies/PCs/!D'bleh|!D'bleh]] was walking his aunt Ounooi Petro's padkos stall shutter down under the last of the oak-filtered light on Dorp Street. It was a good afternoon, the kind he didn't get many of: the university kid who'd tried to talk her down on price sent off with nothing to show for it, Ounooi Petro not backing down an inch. She'd just started ribbing him about a woman she wanted him to meet when the streetlight over the road didn't flicker. It folded.

In Mokpo, [[The Exodus/The Understudies/PCs/Esca|Esca]] was walking his mother home along the harbor after a prickly, loving dinner of hongeo neither of them fully enjoyed and neither would have skipped. Eleven years since the match he threw, the betting log, the escort out past his own sponsor's banner, and she still hadn't fully forgiven him, still hadn't let him pay for the meal. The cable car's red cabins caught the last light over the bay and reflected wrong in the water a half-second before the whole shoreline followed.

Outside Ürgüp, [[The Exodus/The Understudies/PCs/Fern|Fern]] had just watched a forestry official and the landowner's son roll up on the flight enclosure she'd spent weeks building with Hüseyin Bey and two other volunteers, waving a phony transfer document and a threat to tear it all down within the month. She didn't get the chance to argue it out. She got one clean punch into the young scion's face, right as the tuff cliffs went soft and unreal at the edges of her vision.

In Singapore, [[The Exodus/The Understudies/PCs/Kili|Kili]] had just talked her way successfully through an entire extended family's Chinese New Year rehearsal dinner, cooking secrets and all, playing a girlfriend who didn't exist for a client too scared of his mother to tell her the truth. She was stepping out of the lift lobby, five-star rating already pinging in, when the void deck's fluorescent light started humming at a pitch that wasn't electrical.

All four resolved onto the same stretch of broken stone at once.

## The Sundered Choir Answers

The platform under their feet belonged to a ruin adrift over the wild country east of Crystal City: a great cracked rose window overhead, a disc of pale blue crystal pulsing brighter than it should, worn guardian statues flanking a stairway leading nowhere anyone living had climbed in longer than anyone remembered. Beyond the edge: no ground. Cloud, and smaller broken shelves of the same ruin hanging at a distance, threaded back by narrow stone causeways that didn't look safe to cross and weren't.

They had a handful of seconds to take in the cold and the height and the wrongness of it before something moved in the dark stairwell behind the window.

Shadow Creatures came first as shapes without edges, then, as the fight wore on, as something worse: solid, humanoid, resistant in a way the party's stories of these things (whoever had told them, wherever those stories came from) hadn't prepared them for. They ignored everything that wasn't an Earther and came for the four of them specifically. The causeways turned into chokepoints. Someone worked out, mid-fight, that the outer platforms carried unlit braziers, cold and dead, tied by invisible threads of etheric light to the magic circle at the ruin's heart. Severing those threads lit the braziers. Lit braziers hurt these things badly, and burned nothing else.

Before any of it started, a red-skinned tiefling the party would later learn was called **Layla** — perched on a higher shelf, taking notes — told them without ceremony to run if they knew what was good for them, then swan-dived off the platform into the clouds below and was gone before the fight even began. She never engaged directly.

A couple of rounds in, a griffon's wingbeats became audible over the wind: [[The Exodus/The Door Kickers/PCs/Regius|Regius]] and [[Lea]] arriving as reinforcements, drawn by the Choir Window's flare. They didn't clear the field for the four of them. They held a lane, peeled a Spawn off a struggling PC, bought room to breathe. The fight still belonged to the four of them, and they won it themselves.

## What the Stone Used to Be

In the quiet after, someone worked out what the ruin actually was: an abandoned, previously undiscovered temple to [[Lathander]], hollowed out and drifting long enough that no one living remembered it had a name. Nobody could say what tore it loose from the ground in the first place, or why it never finished falling.

Lea and Regius couldn't stay. They had their own meeting the next day — with [[The Claret]], in Crystal City, two days out from the Feast of the Moon — and a single griffon between them wasn't enough to carry four more people home. Lea reached [[Luks Borg]] back at the Guild for guidance; his answer was to get the four of them to the nearest Guild presence, wherever that was. Before either question got settled, the four found their answer made for them: Layla had already arranged transport before she'd even left. Giant eagles under goblin riders came down for the four of them — no destination offered, no destination up for discussion. They flew northeast, to **Stormberg**, on the coast of Corranor. Lea and Regius turned back for Crystal City and their own appointment.

Four people who'd never met each other a day before, now standing in a Guild outpost on a coastline none of them had chosen, with no idea yet whether that was an accident or the point.
