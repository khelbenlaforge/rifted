The Soul Economy is the cosmological principle that gods draw power from worshippers while the Lower Planes—the [[The Exodus/00_My Notes/Locations/Cosmology/Nine Hells|Nine Hells]], the Abyss, and Hades—run on circulating souls as currency; warlock patrons participate, but warlocks work by contract and sit outside it.

## How Kelemvor Routes the Dead

[[Kelemvor Lyonsbane|Kelemvor]] judges the dead and forwards them rather than keeping any: chaotic evil souls go to the Abyss, lawful evil souls to the Nine Hells. Both are Lower Planes, both sit inside the astral barrier, and both are reachable directly through the [[Temple of Kelemvor]]'s Threshold Gates.

Neutral and godless souls are a different case. Their destination lies in the Outer Planes, beyond the barrier, and nothing can send them there directly—not even Kelemvor's own Church. They wait instead, held behind the barrier, until their name is read aloud during the Deeds of the Dead vigil at the [[Feast of the Moon]]. Reading the name somehow sends the soul on to where it belongs. An unread name stays exactly where it is. [[Brother Silas]] describes the judgment itself as resting on deeds and allegiance; freely made soul-contracts, including bargains with the Lower Planes, are honored regardless, and a god who never held a claim—[[Lathander]], by his own account—cannot claim an unpledged soul after the fact. The old Wailing Wall, once a punishment for the faithless, has since been dismantled.

## Souls as Currency

The Lower Planes' appetite for souls in circulation is what makes them a market rather than just an afterlife. [[Regius]]'s cold-open vision placed his mother in a black river, handing a scroll to a winged figure he reads as [[Asmodeus]]—*"three souls for mine."*
