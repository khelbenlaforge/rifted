## Description

Feast of the Moon falls as Uktar closes and Nightal's cold arrives — the last great gathering of the year, and the one night everyone in [[Corranor]] stops working sundown to sunrise, regardless of district or debt. Markets shutter early. The airship docks go quiet. Even the [[The Claret|Claret]] keeps its business off the streets tonight, or so it's said.

At dusk, households walk out to wherever their dead are marked — a headstone, a name carved into a courtyard wall, a plaque in a guildhall for those the sea or the sky never gave back — and bless it: salt, water, a coin left in the frost, whatever the family's own custom calls for. Then everyone moves indoors, or around a fire if they haven't got walls worth mentioning, and the telling starts. It runs until dawn.

Most families in Corranor can't trace a bloodline past [[The Awakening]] — nobody who lived before it remembers who they were, and their children only have 222 years of history to draw on. So the stories reach as far back as they can and keep going anyway: grandparents who first opened their eyes on a heaving, reforming land, the heroes of the Council's early years, half-legends of what Corranor might have been built from, before. By the deep hours of the night, nobody's quite sure which parts are family history and which are just good stories that got told often enough to stick. That's rather the point.

Not every custom that night stays reverent. Past midnight, once the telling's gotten loud enough, someone raises a glass to "whoever we were" — the **Nameless Toast** — and the whole room drinks together, no story required, no name needed. It's the one moment all night where not knowing is the point instead of the ache.

Clergy keep their own vigils alongside the household ones, and Corranor's mixed pantheon means three very different nights unfold under the same moon.

Kelemvor's priests hold the **Deeds of the Dead** — an unbroken vigil where every name given to them since sundown gets spoken aloud once, in full, with whatever the dead did that mattered. It runs in shifts so the recitation never stops before dawn breaks it off; a temple that lets the list go silent, even for a breath, is considered to have failed its dead for the year.

Sehanine Moonbow's faithful gather for the **Mystic Rites of the Luminous Cloud** — witnesses describe worshippers becoming streaks of pale radiance that cross the sky in loose, weaving formations before sunrise. More than one drunk night-watchman swears he's seen them go low over the rooftops, and for once nobody in the guardhouse argues the point.

Shar's faithful keep no public shrine for it, but the night has a name in their circles too — the **Rising of the Dark**, the year's last full dark before Selûne's light comes back. What happens is rarely witnessed and never confirmed: a sacrifice, the saying goes, and a year's worth of scheming set loose once it's done. Most of Corranor treats it as a story told to frighten children. A DM running Shar's cult should treat it as neither rumor nor coincidence.

Not every corner of the night stays somber, either. Lliira's shrine keepers close their service by casting weapons to the ground and burying them under fresh flowers before dawn — the Swords Cast Down. Out past the walls, Shiallia's grove-tenders throw the tree line open to anything that wants in — beast, fey, or otherwise — for feasting, dancing, and songs that don't stop for anyone's grief.

## Role in the Story

This year's Feast of the Moon hasn't happened yet from the party's current vantage — Session 25 closed with the clock still reading 30th Uktar, Year 222 PA, and the holiday falls the night that follows. Two threads are already ticking against it: [[Corvin Adder]] doesn't actually know when [[Layla]] returns to [[Crystal City]] — only that he won't try the sending stone linking to her until the party's back from the [[Sunless Citadel]] trip with the Blackstaffs. "Near the Feast of the Moon" was his guess, hedged, not a date he's tracking. [[Lady Blackstaff]] confirmed the Blackstaff family departs for the Sunless Citadel with the Director of Antiquities only after the holiday passes. Session 26 could open right into it.
