# Law in Corranor

A council rules, and a noble house administers underneath it. That split repeats from Crystal City down to a mining camp barely a season old.

## Governance

Across [[Corranor]], the same pattern holds in every city: a council of seven archmages rules, and the noble houses serve underneath them as administrators, holding the resources and manpower the council's decisions actually need to land. In [[Crystal City]] that's the [[Corranor Council]], seated at the [[Jade Spire]]. One seat in particular carries the law directly: **[[Oriel Blackstaff]]**, Keeper of the Writ, head of [[The Exodus/00_My Notes/Factions/Corranor/House Blackstaff|House Blackstaff]] in name only, since every seated Keeper renounces their outside affiliations the moment they take the chair. Oriel genuinely honors that renunciation. The city still calls it the Blackstaff seat anyway, a habit nobody's bothered to correct.

That renunciation rule is the one piece of Corranor law the party has actually watched hold under pressure: a family head, barred by his own office from speaking for his own house, staying home from a meeting that mattered to him more than most.

## Justice in Crystal City

The [[Hall of Justice - Temple of Tyr]] is where the Writ becomes something you can walk into. A courthouse built as a temple, or a temple built as a courthouse, depending who's asking: paladins, clerics, and monks of [[Tyr]] constitute the entire law enforcement apparatus of Crystal City as the party has seen it so far. The **Arbitration Chamber** hands out free counsel to anyone who can't afford better. The **Oathstone**, a pillar of black marble, binds a sworn oath the way steel binds a debt. Petitioners come here to be heard. So do the people they're petitioning against.

## Elsewhere in Corranor

Not every city runs it the same way. [[Stormberg]] splits its authority across four cogs currently working in harmony, the Magocracy's official oversight, the nobles who fund it, the [[Ether Scryer's Academy]]'s technical hand on the Wall itself, and the [[Adamantine Guild]]'s standing contract to cull whatever burrows into it. [[Sunhold]], barely eight months old as a settlement, makes do with the **Miners' Council**, prospectors and merchants improvising order on a frontier that hasn't earned a proper government yet.
