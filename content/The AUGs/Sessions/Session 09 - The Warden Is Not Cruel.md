# Session 09—The Warden Is Not Cruel

## The Rope Line

At [[Cinderstay]], [[Ansel Krane]] laid out what the shaft behind his forge would allow. [[Mike Kanbei|Mike]], [[Tobias Atticus Rockefeller|Beau]] and [[Sumida Makoto|Makoto]] would go down, and [[Gregory Yang|Gregory]], who had promised Ansel his work at the bench, would hold the rope. Ansel's own expedition rope ran about 120 feet, and the climb to the bottom was about 90. He had never been down himself. He did not want to meddle with the heat that fed his forge and every other forge in the district, and he asked them more than once not to switch it off. [[Dessa Kestrel|Dessa]] had told them she had lost people below, which was probably the only reason she had said no at the start.

Ansel also told them how the place behaved. About once an hour the forge exhaled: his furnace brightened, and a surge of heat followed up the shaft and along the passages. Anyone caught in the shaft or one of the narrow passages when it came would be burned, and the rooms themselves were safe. Whoever stayed topside would watch the furnace and tug the rope to warn those below.

Gregory built the line. He tied the lengths of rope together to about 220 feet and hung a block and tackle from the frame over the shaft. He made two hourglasses of about forty minutes each, and he replicated a pair of sending stones, which work once a day for twenty-five words each way. He kept one stone and Beau took the other. Everything he made would vanish when he next finished a long rest. The work took forty-five minutes. The party waited for the next exhale, watched the furnace glow, and five minutes later saw a jet of flame come up out of the shaft and leave cinders on the ceiling.

They had their reasons. Mike wanted to know whether ruins lay below and what they could tell the party, and he did not want to wait for a proper expedition. Beau, with a new life and little holding him back, wanted something fun to do with it. Makoto said curiosity. All three knew they were going against the advice of everyone above ground.

## Going Down

Mike went first. Beau gave him a few minutes and followed, and Makoto came after. From the fifth knot of the rope the walls turned black and glassy, in long frozen runs like metal that had been poured and never stopped pouring. Makoto slipped and fell during the climb, taking 18 bludgeoning damage, which left him on 2 HP. The whole climb took about fifteen minutes.

The first chamber, at the foot of the shaft, was warm underfoot and the air tasted of iron ore. The black walls glowed a dull orange, and anyone without darkvision saw only dim light. Six niches stood in the wall like stable stalls, and rows of tally strokes climbed the wall beside them, ten at a time. Nothing was left to find, since earlier expeditions had cleared it. The passage east was charred black, and so was the rim of the shaft above, where the stone had melted to glass.

## The Low Hall

Mike went through first. The hall beyond was long, low and scorched black. Four round pits in the floor had crusted over like old pans, the benches were bare, and a huge anvil stood among them, built for something far bigger than a person. At eye height the walls carried carved cogs of four spokes, each with a row of short strokes beside it like a prisoner's tally. Mike's Investigation told him that none of it had been used for hundreds of years, and that anything worth carrying had already been carried out. A sealed plug in the south wall looked like an old dig, and it bore the stamp of the [[Adamantine Guild]], the scroll, the gauntlet and the coin. Beau's Investigation of the four round things on the floor gave him the sense that two of them had once been very large workbenches.

Mike put his strength to one of the cogs. It shifted and sprang back. Chalk ticks ran along its side, and after sixteen to twenty of them someone had drawn a box and filled it in. Mike's Insight read the box as a rough estimate of when something happens. Checked against the hourglasses, one tick came to about three minutes, so a filled box meant roughly the hour between exhales. Beau's pseudodragon counted the ticks.

## The Panels

The next room was a long hall, about fifty-five feet by fifteen, with dry quench channels cut into the floor, four alcoves along the north wall and a sealed plug at the north end. Seven carved panels, each about the size of a door, stood along the walls. Beau's Detect Magic found only a general wash of magic all through the room. The doorway they had come through was charred, and the edges of the far door were charred too, but the alcoves were not, so a person in an alcove or standing beside the doorway would be out of the way of the heat.

The script was unlike any the party knew, yet its meaning came to Beau at once. The panels read:

> 1. The work is kept.
> 2. What is opened is closed.
> 3. What is taken is counted.
> 4. What is started is finished.
> 5. The warden stands where work stands open. The warden is not cruel. The warden is not finished until the work is finished.
> 6. The heat is lent. The line breathes at the turn of the hour. Stand in the lee.

The seventh panel carried no words. It showed two diagrams, one above the other and mirrored, the lower one hanging upside down beneath the first, like unfinished works. One of the party thought it looked like their floating continent, and another said it was only two mirrored shapes. A history check turned up nothing.

Then they saw the forge exhale. The doors opened by themselves one after another, and a rush of fire passed through each, converged on the next and ran on out of sight. The metal lining the frames glowed, and every door shut again behind it. The cog's count had reached about seventeen ticks. The hourglasses were now useless as a clock.

## Topside

Gregory worked the bench. Ansel looked over his work and was pleased, and agreed to tug the rope before each exhale. He thought about walking to the Guild, an hour away, to ask [[Teira]] about her experience, and in the end he stayed. A steward of the [[The Foundry-Owners|foundry-owners]] walked into Cinderstay in a clean leather apron with a ring of keys at his belt, and remarked that the bench stood cold that day.

From the panel hall the party used the sending stone to ask Gregory to find out who the warden was and to go to Dessa for it. She was an hour's walk away, and the DM noted that [[The Deep Index]] was half an hour away. Gregory answered that he would ask, and in the end stayed at the rope.

## The Wheel Room

The party opened the next door. It led into a square room with four fat pillars. An iron gate stood raised in a channel across the floor, with a wheel about as big as a cart wheel beside it, and a line of metal inlay ran across the stone. Another door led east. Every door frame was charred. The doors were built to open on their own for the heat and shut right after, and the party had now opened them by hand. Makoto's Investigation showed that levers there chose which gate the mechanism worked. Mike found slight charring on the floor, much heavier around the doors.

Mike and Beau turned the wheel, and a door opened. A metal automaton came out. It hit Beau for 6 bludgeoning and 2 fire, and Beau failed a Strength save and was held beside it. Makoto answered with thunderwave. The automaton took the thunder and was pushed back, and so were Beau and Mike, who each took 8. Both were left bloody, and at the top the rope was jerking violently. Two more constructs came out.

Mike used Step of the Wind to run for a door and cast Darkness over the automaton so that it could not see them. Makoto went invisible. Up top, Gregory hauled on the rope, and the DM gave Beau advantage on his Strength save to break the hold. Beau rolled 19 and broke free. He dashed for the door, and the automaton's attack of opportunity missed.

The automaton sniffed Mike out in the doorway and struck at him. Mike used Deflect Attacks against the bludgeoning, though he could not deflect the fire in it, and its grab missed him. He disengaged, dashed through, and shut the door. They heard the metal grind for about a second longer, then go quiet, then walk away. Mike guessed that opening a door woke the warden, and said that next time they should shut every door behind them. That fit what the party knew: only two rooms were safe, because nothing happened inside them.

## Out

Mike, badly hurt, wanted nothing more to do with the place. With about an hour before the next exhale, they decided to go up. They climbed out one by one, two of them badly bloodied, and Ansel looked them over, with a lot of explaining ahead.

With all four together again, they pooled what they had. Gregory's Arcana said something must exist to switch the heat and the defences off. The warden guarded a work that was still unfinished, and it rested only once it counted the work finished. It had not come at them as killers. The shifting stone Tavian had shown Mike at the Deep Index carried schematics of one work built above and one below, and the last panel matched them. Only the Rifted had been able to see the symbols move, so very likely only the Rifted could read the panels. That might be why earlier expeditions had failed: they had copied lines they could not read and bashed their way through. It was reason enough to bring [[Dessa Kestrel|Dessa]] a case for a bigger expedition.

Beau lost his colour and looked more like the changeling he was. The party started slowly back toward the Cliffwatch.
