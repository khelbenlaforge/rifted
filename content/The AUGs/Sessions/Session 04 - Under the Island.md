# Session 04—Under the Island

## An Hour in the Pocket

The pocket dimension behind the Cliffwatch's back door let each of them shape a room out of whatever they carried in their heads, and the hour before Dessa's test went into those rooms.

[[Rismawati Bayu|Risma]]'s came with a personal wine dispenser, a bed upgraded from single to super-single, and a bookshelf that ran half accounting texts and half historical romance. Yellow highlights ran through the room to match her glasses, and her window looked out on a street. [[Rachel Tan|Rachel]] built hers around fire: a hearth-themed room with real flames in the windows, a luxury she had only ever owned a knockoff of at home. [[Ruan Myrt Zaltana|Ruan]] reached for the familiar and got her old workplace, folded down to a one-room studio. Chemical compounds floated over a table buried in notes on what she had observed, neon lit the walls because she was picturing things that made her happy, and the photo frame by her bed held four silhouettes with no faces in them. [[Mike Kanbei|Mike]] got a wooden floor and a clear skylight, and past the doorway a balcony with a single cherry-blossom tree growing out of its floor. There was no scenery beyond the tree.

By the end of the hour the strangeness in their bodies had begun to feel like theirs. For the first time since arriving, none of them was afraid they would lose control of it.

## The Proving Floor

Dessa led them into the training grounds, set up as she had left them for the test. Mist lay ankle-deep on the worn stone, the open sky held more stars than the sky over [[Westward]], and lanterns burned around the rim. She walked to the center. The party chose where to stand inside the white circle, and [[Gregory Yang|Gregory]], who had caught up with them by then, put himself in the middle of the group. Dessa waved off any head start and told them to go all out.

Nobody out-rolled the floor itself, which took its own turn at the top of every round. Its first move turned the ground into uneven cubes, some rising, some sinking, none higher than about five feet. Everyone made a Dexterity save to stay on their feet.

Gregory climbed out of the cubes and set his Eldritch Cannon in the empty air above Ruan, then wrapped himself in a ward of cold. Rachel raged. One swing of her glaive, thrown in with Reckless Attack, drove Dessa down. Risma's Starry Wisp crossed the floor in a blue-and-purple streak and hit for extra psychic damage. Mike put Faerie Fire on Dessa and worked around to flank her. Ruan spent a Wild Shape to wake her Symbiotic Entity, walked up, and touched Dessa with necrotic spores, which she shrugged off.

Dessa answered with Bane on two of them, and each would subtract a d4 from everything they rolled. The floor sent four slabs of wall up around Rachel and cut off her sight of everyone else, until Gregory's cannon broke the wall down. Then a wide circle of the floor went pitch black, and only those with darkvision could see Dessa at all. A Faerie Fire laid over the circle and Mike's Dancing Lights gave the rest something to aim at, and Dessa dispelled the dark herself. Ruan's second touch, a small greenish-purple cloud of spores, frightened her, and she ordered one of them to flee.

Between turns Dessa spent a legendary action: she put her sword away, pulled on a glove, and punched twice with advantage. The blows dropped Rachel unconscious. Rachel claimed her pending Level 4 right there, as they had been told they could, and got back up on the new level's hit points, prone. She raged again, and her next hit pushed Dessa back. The PC Dessa had ordered to flee scrambled out of the cubes and clear of the fight. Ruan cast Cure Wounds on the worst-hurt among them.

At the end of the fourth round Dessa stopped it. She was breathing hard and very low on hit points. She said that was enough and thanked them for the information.

## What the Test Left

The short rest healed bodies and returned a few spent resources, and the rooms were still waiting. Gregory's turned out to be art deco, with a fireplace and an alcohol bar for friends only (he did not drink, but liked how it looked). It also had shelves of history, politics, finance and some literature, a window onto the distance, a large mirror and an aquarium.

Afterward each of them took stock. Risma was still coming down, a little wild. Ruan was in disbelief, staring at her spores, a scientist watching cause and effect trade places. Mike felt like Superman and was annoyed at the spear thrust he had missed. Gregory said it was good to be back with familiar people, that the dark had frightened him because there was nothing he could do about it, and that the flight in, on a great winged mount, had been the best part of his day. He had looked down at open countryside with no great city anywhere, and the air was clean.

When they stepped out of the pocket dimension, the incessant noise of the town rolled through the Cliffwatch's long front room. Dessa was waiting at the counter, immaculate, the satchel at her hip with its red wax intact, tapping her baton against her palm. "That's the evaluation done," she said, and nothing after it would change that. She welcomed them to the [[Adamantine Guild]]. They stayed Brass in rank.

What she said next was not part of the test, she told them, and they could decline it without losing anything in her eyes. Below the Cliffwatch a stair ran down the cliff face to a place called [[The Keel|the Keel]]. It could be reached only while the island rode aloft, which it would for another week or so, she hoped. She would walk them there by way of the town, and brief them at the bottom.

She warned them first about what they carried. Whatever each of them had grown into, people would want it. She had already noted what each was carrying. They should keep their tier items out of sight and not use them in public. A cleric especially would sell their own grandmother for something special.

## West Along the Ridge

The outer door stuck, then gave. The ridge road was packed earth and slag. A hundred hammers rang out, never quite landing together, and char sat at the back of the throat. The [[Westward|Cliffwatch]] stood behind them, tall, pale and half-finished, like a lighthouse that had stopped partway up. The path ran west along the cliff to a stone lip with an iron rail.

From the lip, Westward lay open below. The harbor was gone. Where the river mouth should meet the sea there was only gray mud, cracked at the edges and dark in the middle, with dozens of hulls tipped on their sides like dropped shoes. The river still ran, threaded across the flats and over the cliff's edge into open air, with no spray coming back up. Dessa had counted thirty-one hulls; the [[The Ferry-Masters|ferry-masters]], she said, would not say the number aloud. Westward lived in two seasons. While the strait carried water the ferry-masters ran the crossing to [[Sunhold]], whose quarries fed Westward's smiths. When the island lifted and the strait drained, the [[The Foundry-Owners|foundry-owners]] took over. She put the gap between landfalls at about twenty days on average, sometimes ten, sometimes twenty-five, and trusted nobody who gave her an exact number. If the ground shook and people cheered, they should grab something.

Someone asked whether they would ever go home. Dessa repeated what she had said before: perhaps twenty or thirty Rifted were walking around, each asking the same question and holding nothing but clues. The party could thrive, she told them, if they gave it time. The land was governed by seven councillors, she explained, with each city run by a noble house. [[Sunhold]] was a brand-new settlement, under six months old, and it had made Westward richer.

Her shortcut ran through the Deep Index. A hooded lamp lit a narrow passage, with a rustle of paper and a young voice reciting numbers to itself somewhere in the back. Crates by the door were marked with heights, dates and drawer numbers. It was the [[Ether Scryer's Academy]]'s westernmost annex, she said, with its own charter and a handful of researchers. They cataloged whatever turned up and sent couriers to Crystal City with the reports, where the [[Director of Antiquities]] read them. The Academy often hired the Guild.

Past it she pointed out a tall wooden tower with wings, beaks and tails showing through its frame: the Aerie of the [[Azure Tamers]], where most of the air transport in the country was arranged. A druid site lay nearby outside the city, and she offered to introduce Ruan and Risma to both. She called that their downtime on this side.

The forges stood below, stacked in order around a huge pit at the center. She said they burned on fire and lava from beneath the city, that she had no idea where the heat came from, and that she was leaving the question to the dwarves, with a trace of old tension in her voice.

## The Gate and the Stair

Beyond the forges the path ended at a rock wall with a gap cut through it. In the gap stood a gate of black iron bars, chained, and a rope rail ran along the edge of nothing. The guard on duty, Big Ulrich, came to attention, and the wind came up through the gap in one steady push.

Here she stopped to talk about the shadows. They had appeared at High Harvestide, about three months ago, she said, and at first they had nearly cost the Guild four or five of its best. Those people survived. That was all of the public record. Where the shadows came from and what they wanted was not in it. "I hate theories," she said, and offered facts: they came only after Rifted, and after what she called planar aberrations, where reality shifted in space.

The Keel, she said quietly, was older than the memory of anyone in the kingdom, which reached back only about two hundred and twenty years. Someone had found it by playing a prank: they jumped off the ledge, cast Feather Fall, and drifted down past ruins under the continent. Nobody had ever told anyone what was down there, in writing or by word. The Guild kept watch over it together with the Academy.

Someone asked her age. "I should be six hundred and forty-seven," she said, and someone worked out that about four hundred years of that were gone. She took it as a researcher would: she knew exactly how much knowledge was missing, and it hurt on that level. She added that Layla had been an assistant to one of the Crystal City counselors. That counselor, [[Garm]], was one of the Guild's patrons, which was probably one reason the Guild had the autonomy it did and, by extension, why the party had a little protection. She would not force them to stay, because this was a free world, albeit a dangerous one.

The stair went down the cliff face in long switchbacks, each cut into the rock and roped along the upper edge. Below the first turn the cliff fell away into open sky, the sea far below was a flat gray plate under cloud, and the island's own shadow lay across it. At the fourth turn the rock began to curve in and stop being natural. The stone turned flat and worked, dark and vast, sweeping out beneath the island like the belly of an upturned hull. It ended on a white stone lip with no rail. Old rope lay coiled against the wall, and a row of rusted pitons was driven into the rock. Ruan, nervous at the height, held on to herself the whole way down.

On the lip, Dessa set the satchel against the wall, took off her glasses, and gave them the rules while she still could. The walkway ahead was ten feet wide, stone and dark metal, with open sky through every gap, and the gaps were real. The wind never stopped. The only light came from below, sunlight thrown back off the ocean, so they should look down to find their bearings. About fifteen feet past the landing, sound began to arrive late: her voice would reach them three or four seconds after she spoke, and some people lost time there. They should not linger. She herself had lost three days in that stretch before coming back out at this spot. "I have no route to give you," she said, and she would not be crossing with them. She wanted to see five of them back here, with open eyes, breathing.

She gave them one of a pair of sending stones, which would not work without its twin, and Gregory took it. His offer to copy it was refused. They checked their gear. Risma sent a sparrow ahead into the wind, and the bird struggled through it.

## The Quiet Chamber

They crossed in a line with Ruan at the back. At the threshold Dessa's mouth opened, and the words arrived three seconds late. Then every one of them went dark, with no save, and the ribs and the wind were gone.

They woke on the floor of a round chamber under a shallow dome. The air was dry and still, and the room swallowed every sound. At its center stood a woman with one hand raised, a star in her left hand and nine stars over her head like a crown. A muscular man with a hammer knelt in front of her. A five-headed dragon faced one way, and a metallic dragon reared up facing the other, and the four figures ringed something in the middle. On the floor were four symbols. One of the party called out their names without knowing why: [[Istishia]], [[Grumbar]], [[Akadi]] and [[Kossuth]].

The slab was a map of the flat world, Corranor's mountains and rivers in the north. A second land lay beneath that one, with forests and mountains below and the trees upside down, gravity pulling from both sides. Along the walls ran a dark figure in one corner, perhaps a devil with its back to a way out, and countless small lights streaming away from it, led by figures. At the back of the flight one large figure stood facing the dark one.

Then came the choice. They could touch the wall, or one of the figures, and carry on. Or they could stop here. Touching the wall meant the mind would split and wake in another body, among four adventurers named [[Midnight]], [[Kelemvor]], [[Adon]] and [[Cyric]], in a tavern. The party would be watching a past, through a stranger's eyes.
