# Session 01 — Five of Six

## Five Doors, One Pull

In Shimokitazawa, [[Mike Kanbei]] picked up his mother's call for once instead of letting it ring out — barely three words in when the light started spilling wrong past the edges of his monitor.

In Glendale, [[Ruan Myrt Zaltana]] chose to walk toward a doorway burning with no heat to it, at the edge of a dream field, instead of waiting to find out if it was walking toward her.

In Singapore, [[Rachel Tan]] was three feet from her father's wallet and a flight she'd been told she couldn't take when her mother caught her — she was still staring at the booking screen, sent back to her room, when it went blue.

Also in Singapore, [[Gregory Yang]] was telling his girlfriend the truth about how OCS actually felt, maybe for the first time all year, when he opened his own front door onto a wall of numbers that turned into blue light.

And in Singapore again, [[Rismawati Bayu]] had one hand on her front door on an ordinary Monday when the MRT platform ahead of her stopped being a train and started being light.

All five went under at once, mid-motion, before any of them chose to be anywhere else.

## What the Rock Remembers

They didn't land. Not yet. First came a shared vision none of them had asked for: a world with no horizon, flat and edge-terminated, its ocean boiling rather than storming, city-sized stone falling out of a sky on fire. At the center of it, a single spike of light rose out of the ground, circled by a helix of spiraling rock too precise to be anything but deliberate.

Each of them lived a piece of it from the ground up. Mike failed to pull a panicking child clear of a falling boulder and watched them both go under it. Gregory tried to calculate the impossible physics of a floating landmass even as the assistant beside him didn't survive the next one. Rismawati found herself airborne, climbing higher out of pure panic than the rest of her flock dared, and it was from that height she first noticed the world beneath her had no curve to its horizon at all. Just an edge. Ruan stayed grounded, tracing a mycelial network through the chaos to keep whatever forest and animals she could find away from the worst of it. Rachel flew too. A voice that "sounded like fire become sound" bound her mid-vision to a bargain she never agreed to out loud: *save the souls, because the souls are mine.* She hauled fleeing halflings clear of the lava one by one anyway. Later, she'd remember it as the first time raging ever felt like relief instead of shame.

The vision ended the way these things do: a black-cloaked figure, alone at the base of the spike, buried under the last falling stone as the light went out.

## The Drowned Dawn

They woke inside a temple no map either world would have named for them — an underground chamber, water pooling across worn stone, a ring of crystal-topped braziers gone cold around a rune circle at the center. A tower climbed out of the middle of it, five floors of spiral stair reaching for a crack of daylight none of them had found yet.

New bodies came with new things to learn about them. Mike woke pale-skinned and sharp-eared, cursing in Japanese, asking after a kid he couldn't have named even sober. Ruan stood up at seven feet and worked out her own height by how far everyone else had to look up. Rachel came to crumpled on the floor, veins glowing red under skin that hadn't been hers yesterday. Gregory cast Shield before he'd even placed where he was. Rismawati's head turned a full circle before she noticed the talons where her feet used to be.

Each of them had brought something with them, too. Rachel's polearm arrived already named, in the moment it was handed to her: **Permission**, for a hand that had spent a lifetime waiting on someone else's. Ruan's wedding ring had grown into a breastplate, and Gregory's pendant sat quiet until touched — nothing showy about either one until it mattered. Rismawati's spectacles only manifested when she reached to actually study something. Mike didn't have to explain his at all; the CCR record was simply there, the one thing in the room that needed no introduction.

A woman watched them from above the whole time — red-skinned, robed, a book and scroll in hand, clearly mid-experiment and in no hurry to justify it. She counted them and came up short. *"It was supposed to be six."* She never gave a name. She pointed them up the tower, promised transport waiting at the top, refused to say why any of them had been pulled here, warned them to run if they saw shadows — and vanished before the fight even started.

## What Woke With Them

The dark answered before the five of them had finished getting their bearings. Shapes rose out of the cracks in the floor — roughly five feet of roiling mist and claw, more of them than expected, closing from more directions than the room seemed to have. Mike cut a path through with Step of the Wind, calling the others to follow him by sound instead of sight. Rachel went reckless and raging: one shadow dropped outright, a second nearly finished in the same swing, and the fire-voice from the vision found her again mid-fight, still unnamed. Ruan turned invisible once, then found her footing and lit a dead sconce with Starry Wisp. Between them, Gregory built his eldritch cannon and helped light a second sconce with Fire Bolt while Rismawati fled upward and lit a third.

One shadow, larger and fused from two smaller ones, nearly finished what the rest couldn't. It clawed Rachel down to the edge of bloodied before Rismawati's healing word pulled her back. By the time the last of the creatures froze in place, the chamber had gone from dim and dead to something close to daylight — the temple, briefly, remembering what it used to be for.

## Unfinished Climb

Five strangers, alive, stood at the base of a spiral stair with a transport promised somewhere above them. The DM skipped ahead to a montage instead of playing the climb scene by scene: the party working their way up, getting ready for whatever's next. Nobody actually confirmed whether that top got reached, or who the sixth arrival was ever supposed to be.
