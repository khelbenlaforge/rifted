# Session 05—Three of Five

## The Landing

Wind pushed at their faces, cold and steady, and rough white stone lay under their backs. They were on the lip below the end of the stair, beside the same coil of rope and the same rusted pitons, with light coming up off the ocean far below. To them it had felt like about five minutes.

[[Dessa Kestrel|Dessa]] sat against the wall with her satchel beside her and her glasses in her hand, like someone who had been there a long while. Her hair was a mess and there were dark bags under her eyes. She counted them and stopped. "Three. I said I wanted five of you back." [[Gregory Yang|Gregory]] told her the others were dead, and his Deception came to 18. Her calm began to crumble, and she started to cry.

[[Rismawati Bayu|Risma]] cut in: she was sure the other two were fine. The party had come to a wall, and the other two had touched it. Gregory had touched it too, and he had come back.

Dessa pulled herself together. They had been gone 26 hours, 14 minutes and 47 seconds. That was less than the three days she had lost herself, but she was missing two people. She could sense the missing bracers somewhere farther in. Dessa chose to believe that three days was the longest the place would keep anyone before it pushed them out.

She asked what they had seen. They described a map of the land with a second land beneath it, and a room with carvings and statues. Dessa remembered a room of about the same size, with a balcony in the middle and smooth marble walls, and she was puzzled. Where she remembered smooth marble walls, the party had found relief carvings all around and four statues. Gregory had touched one of the carvings. With a History total of 13, he kept a foggy memory of mugs clinking and four figures laughing at a table in what seemed to be a tavern, with a storm going on outside. That was all he had.

## What Dessa Told Them

She would stay on the Landing for the next two days to wait for the others. It was too dangerous for the rest of them to come back down, and she did not want anyone else disappearing. Gregory still had her sending stone, which worked only once a day, and she wanted a report from him every evening. She wrote a short note of introduction to the Aerie on parchment, and Risma took it.

She had also heard back over the Guild's sending stones, she said: not every Rifted stays. Some fade. She did not understand how it worked, but she described it as a person going pale and translucent. It might mean they were going home. Nobody knew. She knew of one case, a man called Erwin. A colleague who had read the magic on him thought his body had been brought here by mistake, or on purpose by someone who took the wrong person. Apparently he had been sent back, or at least she hoped so. Risma asked where he might have gone otherwise. Dessa said she knew less than she wished, and she would pass on anything she learned.

Before they left she turned to [[Rachel Tan|Rachel]]. The last fight had proved one thing to her, she said: Rachel got back up. A post at the Cliffwatch was hers if she wanted it, and the Guild bracer was hers to keep either way. She would try to arrange an introduction to the [[Ether Scryer's Academy|Academy]] too, though no Academy scholars or teachers were stationed in Westward, and she was the only one in touch with it. Gregory asked about money. The weekly pay was part of the deal, and the week was not over yet. Quests were fine, but they were new to the town and should meet people first.

[[Ulrich|Big Ulrich]] was upstairs if they needed help, and the [[Azure Tamers]] at the Aerie would look after them. Then the party started up the stair.

## The Rooms

They went back to the Cliffwatch, and a roll of 15 let Gregory remember the way. They took a long rest. The rooms gave back whatever a guest pictured, though nothing could be carried out of them.

Each of them spent the rest with something from before. Rachel thought about what it meant to be seen as someone who stood back up. Risma poured herself a drink from the room's jug, and worked out how an owlin washes. Owl feathers were soft and not waterproof, and she dried within the hour. By the end she was thinking that this might be a chance at a new life. Gregory read a local book on the Academy: it was founded when the colony was, to find out why the islands float and to study magic. The same book gave him the first description of what an artificer was here, an enchanter who puts magic into items.

About eight hours later it was afternoon.

## The Aerie

The tower stood west across the city above the forges, tall and the only thing in Westward made of wood. The lift rope ran down the outside, but the winch was on the platform, eleven stories up. Gregory checked it with his tinker's tools (25) and could not work it from the ground, and cutting the rope would bring everything down. Rachel's shout missed its DC. Risma's Message needed a target she could see. So Risma flew up.

About seven griffins were perched at the top, eagle in front and mostly lion or tiger behind, each bigger than a horse. A woman in a harness was writing in a ledger, a long bow on her back, with tribal markings on her face and pointed ears. This was Pell, and she was glad of any excuse to put down her quill. A man named Odo was bent over the winch, furious. Risma said she was with the [[Adamantine Guild]], and Pell asked after Dessa. Risma asked about the lift. Odo, still at the winch, said he had asked for help ten times, he was no engineer, and without help it would stay broken. Pell pointed her at the stairs. Risma said she had an engineer downstairs and went down to tell him.

The stairs would take five to ten minutes. Rachel offered to throw Gregory up instead. She raged, and her roll came up a natural 20. Her hand closed on his collar and belt, and she hurled him up the tower. His saving throw came to 13, and he caught the ledge and pulled himself over. Rachel had to take the stairs.

Odo stood there with his hammer raised. Gregory told him he was the engineer Risma had mentioned, and that her friend had thrown him. Mending took the rust off the pulley and the winch in front of Odo's eyes. Gregory read that Odo was frustrated at the lack of support rather than angry at him, and that he was no engineer. Odo had grumbled "wizards" under his breath. Gregory said he could not enchant the winch to keep it from jamming again. Odo gave him bits of scrap metal and some enchanted ore. Gregory used the lift to bring Rachel up, with Odo helping on the winch, and it took about three minutes. Odo saluted her. "Make sure you stay friends," he told Gregory.

Pell read Dessa's letter and said it was high praise, because Dessa did not write for just anyone. She knew they were Rifted, and she would keep that to herself until Dessa announced it to the city's leaders. She passed the letter back. The Tamers flew people between cities, she said, because teleportation did not exist beyond jumps of thirty or sixty feet. There were few birds and plenty of demand, so the flights were costly. Druids and rangers were welcome among them, and Dessa had been specific that the offer was Risma's only if she wanted it.

The work came in three parts. Risma would start with the animals and the administration: ledgers, feed, buying food. Later she would join the patrols, which watched the land around the cities, protected travelers on the roads from wild animals without killing them where they could, and looked for anyone being teleported against their will. The capital had sent word to watch for it. A tiefling came up in the talk, and Pell said the Tamers meant to find her. Pell agreed with Dessa that each of them should find something in the city to attach themselves to. Risma's Insight came to 15. Pell was kind, but one eye kept drifting back to her paperwork.

Gregory, meanwhile, talked shop with Odo and asked about the Academy. There were no Academy teachers or scholars in the city, and Dessa was in touch with it about once a day. Odo also gave him an ore that gives off light in whatever it is used to make, and said he had no idea why they had handed it to him. Between Risma's two glide attempts, Gregory told Odo that as an artificer he could make rope and other useful things, since the Aerie was short on engineers and on materials for maintenance. Odo declared him a new best friend and offered to pay him if he came by once a week or once every two weeks to help.

## The Voice

Rachel stood a little apart, listening, and looked out toward the forges. She was thinking that her next move might be to go down and talk to the smiths. She rolled a 3 on a Wisdom save. Heat gathered at her back, though the forges were too far away to feel. Then a low voice spoke in her head, one she knew, as if fire had learned to talk. "Two of mine are still under the island."

It said strange that a wayward soul should come back without memory of its contracts. All souls belonged to it as long as the land was safe, and the way it said "safe" was not kind. Her Insight was a 10. Rachel said she was done being bound. They rolled opposed Wisdom checks, hers with advantage against the voice's 23. It did not compel her, but she could not ignore it or resist it completely. The voice said it had saved everyone, her family included, and had burned the contracts with the Hells. It asked only that the two souls under the island come out safe, so that it could fulfil its contracts. It added that she would want something sooner or later.

## The Second Landing

Pell, seeing how Risma's wings sat, offered her a joyride up to the second landing, where the wind was best for teaching new hatchlings to glide. From the top they could see a huge spike standing in the middle of the continent, thousands of feet high, the one from their shared vision. When Risma concentrated on it, her spectacles settled onto her face, which surprised Pell. Pell took Risma's hand and held it over the eastern side of the tower, where heat from lava pockets under the city rose in columns, and then over the western side, where wind from the ocean blew in to replace it. She told Risma to turn her wings in, let the warm column take her, and straighten them slowly without flapping.

Beside them sat Bertha, a giant eagle the size of a house, the only creature on that landing, with two or three huge eggs tucked under her.

Risma failed her first glide. On the second try the dice failed her again, but Bertha, exasperated, beat her enormous wings and lifted the warm air beneath her. Risma decided to stay with the Azure Tamers. She flew off into the sky with Pell riding Bertha, and her orientation began.

As Risma flew off, Gregory and Rachel talked over whether to explore the forges or to go after Mike and Ruan. Rachel pressed for going after them, and she did not mention the voice. The session ended there.
