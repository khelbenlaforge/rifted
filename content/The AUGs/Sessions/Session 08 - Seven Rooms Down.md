# Session 08—Seven Rooms Down

## The Walk to the Guild

Mike, Gregory, Beau and Makoto set out to carry [[Teira]] to the Guild. Dessa had warned Mike and Gregory earlier not to talk openly about the blood business or about teleportations from another world. The capital did not understand any of it, and too many questions would follow. The Guild's protection held while people knew the Guild was looking out for them.

The walk took half an hour to an hour through winding streets. They crossed a river 25 or 30 metres wide, and on the far side the mud-brick houses gave way to carved stone fitted close. Then they climbed a cliff at the edge of the harbour to an unfinished lighthouse of bright white plaster. From the top Beau and Makoto saw the harbour for the first time. What lay below was not water. It was air and cloud.

## Dessa

The door opened on warm wood and candlelight, a fire with a small animal turning on a spit, long tables under the windows and a corridor at the back. [[Dessa Kestrel|Dessa]] sat at the counter in her glasses, doing paperwork, with dark bags under her eyes. She looked up, saw Teira over their shoulders, then heard Beau and Makoto speak English. "Oh, fuck me," she said. "Two more." She was not angry with them, she added.

She gripped a symbol on her chest that neither newcomer recognised, and told them to bring Teira in. Teira went down on a pallet on the floor. Dessa touched her forehead, a glow showed in her hand, and she said Teira seemed fine and would wake with time. Then she ran into a small door, pots clanged, and she came back with two huge mounds of steaming meat that smelled of honey and strong drink. It was the last of the hot meat in the house. She also cast a mass healing over all four of them. Their injuries had been psychic, with no wounds to see, and each got eleven hit points back. Stew and bread followed, and the party took a short rest.

## What Dessa Made of It

Dessa sat down, took her hair out of its bun and asked what had happened. Gregory said they had gone looking for [[Rachel Tan|Rachel]] because Dessa herself had warned them of a group trying to kidnap people, and that they had found Teira with her blood being used to power the circle. He told her about the two dead [[The Claret|Claret]], the one who had jumped out of the window, and the two new Rifted who had come out of the circle. Dessa asked whether they had explained everything to Beau and Makoto. Gregory said they had: what Rifted are, that they cannot go back, and how they got here, and they had introduced themselves.

Her guess was that the circle had been drawn in a great hurry. She did not think it was a Layla job, or a job for Layla's people. She thought the Claret were trying to get in on whatever was happening, and that they had managed a rudimentary version of what Layla had done. It had worked, so Beau and Makoto were fine. They could easily have died, though, if the circle had put them in the open sky or inside the earth, and they were lucky to have landed in a tower. She stayed calm on the outside, but her eyes sharpened, and she asked whether something had come and attacked them. The party said yes: the shadows she had been told about before. Mike said they suspected the shadows came with any Rifted who were teleported. The shadows aimed at the Rifted and stopped following once the shadow was gone or the Rifted had moved a certain distance away, as Mike and Gregory would already have told the newcomers. Dessa thought this would scare the local Claret and that they would be more careful, and that Layla was now wanted by more than the Guild.

She explained the Claret to Beau and Makoto. They had started as small thieves and were now among the biggest criminal organisations on the continent, with their main base in Crystal City. The two dead Claret wore garnet studs in their ears, and rank went up with the number of studs. What her own people told her was that whoever had been teleporting Rifted in had been asking the Claret to procure blood. It now looked as if the Claret were trying to do it themselves. She hoped losing two of their people would slow them. She added that Layla was wanted because she vanished when the first group was teleported in, with no word and no sound, and that anyone who plays with magic they do not understand deserves jail.

## Corranor, as Dessa Told It

Dessa laid out what the Guild knew. Everyone in Corranor remembers nothing from before 222 years ago, and her account of the first day matched what the party had seen in its vision: everyone woke at once while the world was being torn apart, and survived through sheer willpower and resilience. She did not know how the Rifted came here, and neither did the capital. There were only theories. The first was that Layla, whom Dessa plainly could not stand, was running around the continent dropping people in from another world with no thought for who they had left behind. About nine had come in the first group, and the Guild decided then to take in as many of them as it could and protect them. The Guild was not rich, so each of them would also have to find a way to earn a living.

She named the town for Beau and Makoto: Westward, mostly a forging town. Two ancient sites lay in or under it. One was a complex underneath the town, beneath Slagrow, that gave off unlimited heat for the forges. Nobody understood it. The other was the one the party had visited, which she called the Keel. It could be reached only while the island was in the air, by a stair down the side of the continent, and the Guild had already lost one person there. She pointed across the water to another island, where most of the ore was mined and sent over. Beau and Makoto would be tested tomorrow, she said, as the others had been. One of the party asked if they could join in. She said, "We'll see."

She told them about the Guild: a scroll, a gauntlet and a coin above the mantel, and three tenets. The Guild looks for the knowledge that has been lost, protects those who can pay it, and promotes free trade and the livelihood of ordinary people. It is sponsored by the highest government and not controlled by it, and it answers to no one except that government. It also needs money to run.

## What They Can Do

Dessa grew frustrated. She had studied for years in a temple to reach what the Rifted did without being taught, and she could not explain it. She guessed their souls were attached to these powers, the way Mike was attached to the discipline of monastic ascetics, which only a few temple monks keep, and the way Gregory had built an Eldritch Cannon in front of her. When Gregory said he still had it with him, she told him to put it away. Do not be afraid of what you can do, she said, be afraid of where you point it.

The Guild would give them protection and a place to live. She could not pay them much unless they did Guild work, and she pointed to the board of postings. She suggested they settle into the city, though not tomorrow, and they did not have to do it the day after either.

## The Studs

Mike showed her the two garnet studs and asked how Claret ranks work. Dessa said a recruit with no stud was a hired hand, and one stud meant a little more knowledge and authority. The saying was to avoid or intimidate anyone with one stud, to be scared of two, to run from three and to expect to die at four. One of the party pointed out that someone could wear a stud to get in. She answered that they all know each other, so it might work in another city. Mike asked about a safe house or hiding place. The DM reminded the party that nobody knew whether the Claret had learned that two Rifted had appeared, since one man had escaped through the window. Dessa told them to lay low for a few days. They should expand their own networks and, after a few days, see if they felt up to going out, since the researchers wanted to meet them.

## The Pocket Rooms

At the end of the corridor an oval hung in the air with a black sheen. Dessa touched the wall beside it, the sheen glowed and went back to black, and Mike and Gregory walked through. Dessa pushed a hand through to show it was safe and told Beau and Makoto to follow. Beau, tired, walked straight in. Makoto hesitated at the edge until Mike called to him from the other side. A band of cool air went across his face, black and then bright.

Inside was a round space floating in a field of stars and nebulae. A disc held jugs and platters over the floor, small discs floated around it as chairs, and six doors ringed the walls. Dessa gave Beau and Makoto the last two. Mike's and Gregory's were already taken. [[Rismawati Bayu|Risma]] was out working and Rachel was training in the mountains. She showed them the table: pick up a jug, think of what you want and pour. She touched a platter and sausages appeared. Nothing from inside could be taken out unless they had brought it in. Makoto asked why they needed money at all. Dessa said this was why quarters cost the Guild so little, but everything else still did. Each room could look like anything its owner wanted, though nothing could be carried out through the oval. Everyone took a long rest.

## Morning

Mike and Gregory, who usually woke at about half past eight, were up first. Dessa had been up since six or seven, scribbling. Mike, Gregory and Beau sat at the floating table. Beau pulled a steaming Colombian coffee out of a jug by imagining it. Makoto came out last and in a mood. He had checked the mirror and his tattoos were gone, all but one strange mark he had never seen. He showed Dessa the mark. She had never seen the symbol; it appeared to be unique to him. She wished she could explain it. He sensed the mark pulsing and giving him some of his abilities, as if from a time he did not know. Beau had forgotten that nothing could leave with him, and his clothes became what he had arrived in as he went out.

## Beau Asks About Jackals

Beau asked Dessa about jackal-headed creatures. She asked whether this had been in a dream, then thought and said only one kind came to mind. She told them there are lower planes and upper planes, separate dimensions all connected to this world. Devils belong to the Nine Hells and demons to the Abyss, and by the histories the two have fought for millions of years. She thought the world lucky that they were busy with each other. Between them stood a third group, the yugoloths, who owed nothing to either side. They were mercenaries who played both sides for their own profit, and she doubted they cared about this world. Both demons and devils had tried to steal souls from the afterlife in the past, she said, and the yugoloths, she thought, were sneakier about it, if they did. If any of them met one, she told them, they should be very careful. Devils lie, demons only want destruction, and a yugoloth can bend truth and lies to great effect.

## What Dessa Would Not Do

Mike wanted to explore the ruins. Gregory said his boss Ansel Krane had told him they could visit the ruins below Cinderstay. Dessa knew Ansel and said he fixes her things. She would not come: a tall stack of paperwork stood behind her. Beau offered to help with her accounts, which was his specialty, and she said he definitely could. The paperwork had grown since the party first saw her eight days earlier, and she had put it aside for three days to wait for them below. Once the island landed, the other Guild people would come back across the ocean and she would have more time.

She was firm that it was a dangerous place. The Keel had cost her three days. She had walked through a few columns and appeared outside again with three days gone, hungry and thirsty. People who went in came out an hour later, three hours later, a day later or two days later. Gregory's time lost came to a little over 26 hours and Mike's to a little over two days. The Director of Antiquities was coming soon, and the Director's people asked for the Guild's expeditions. If they came, she could ask for money and equipment. If the party wanted to go down the Old Forge's shaft now, she could not stop them. They should keep it short, though, and come right back up at any sign of danger. The only way down was by rope. A ladder would let anything big or hairy go down it, she said, and would tempt a child walking by to wander in. She told them to ask Ansel for the long rope the Guild keeps for expeditions and to arrange for someone to stand at the top to pull them out. That was a Guild matter they would have to work out themselves.

Gregory said he did not feel ready and wanted to help the others settle in first. Beau said these people were the last living connection to his home world, and asked whether he could at least go to the library and meet Mike's friend first.

She added what she knew of the government. Corranor is a magocracy: seven wizards rule the continent, and Westward has one representative of them. Most towns are run by noble houses, and in Westward that house is Ferrowe, which comes from a long line of blacksmiths and master smiths and became a naval house. Trade, and the carriage of ore from the mining towns across the strait, belongs to the ferry-masters. Much of the Guild's money comes from expeditions into the Old Forge, since the Director of Antiquities pays well for artifacts. Nobody enters the Keel now, because it has been judged too dangerous. Her one concern was that anyone who goes down comes back alive.

She said she did not want to lose anyone else after Ruan, whom she spoke of as lost. She looked at Mike, an older man, and said she was not a fan of letting kids die. Rachel and Risma were kids too, she said. Rachel had joined the city guard and Risma worked with the Rangers, who raise griffins and great birds to carry people across the continent. Both, at least, would be safe. Then she put her glasses back on and said she had a great deal of paperwork to do.

Makoto said he had not seen any live entertainment in the city, and mentioned that he was technically a bard. Dessa said there were taverns, asked whether he played an instrument or sang, and offered to take him to one she knew that night.

## The Deep Index

They all went to the Deep Index. It stood beside the river, a building with two columns at its entrance, in a strong cold wind with a thin winter frost. The air inside was cool and dry. Crates lined the floor in an unbroken row from the main room all the way out of the building, stencilled with heights, dates and drawer numbers, and with words such as clay, obsidian, unknown, Old Forge and Keel. Mike knew the line well. The room beyond smelled of paper, and it was packed with filing cabinets, drawers and heaps of loose papers.

[[Tavian Wick|Tavian]] stood in the middle of it, ink on everything, pale, pink-eyed and white-haired, with glasses, carrying a crate to a drawer. That was crate number four of the morning. He said he needed help, and Mike said he had brought friends. Tavian already knew they were Rifted and welcomed them. He told them to do their research freely and to tell him if they took anything out, and not to mix up his papers. His system was about eight piles of paper, each in a different part of the room, and he remembered where each one belonged. The drawers and cabinets at the back held what had been sorted, and the real problem was the backlog of crates that ran from his counter out the door. He would love a wizard to do it, if the Academy had the budget. He knew of no spell that does paperwork.

Beau went through the shelves marked planar studies and rolled a 15 on Investigation. Tavian saw him at it and asked if he was interested in the outer planes. He gave them a long talk on the lower planes. Devils and demons run on what he called a soul economy, where souls are the currency. People make contracts with devils or demons for what they want. When they die, the soul is taken in and processed into a coin, and their body becomes a devil or a demon. The most powerful demons and devils hold many souls. Devils fight demons because law hates chaos, even among the evil, and the yugoloths sell their help to either side for money.

## News from Crystal City

Tavian told Mike that word had come back from Crystal City. The Director of Antiquities would not come in person, but was sending a large contingent of researchers, who were powerful wizards. The Director was very interested in what the party had seen and, Tavian added, very sad that the Rifted in Crystal had gone off to do their own thing and left him no guinea pigs. Mike asked for a direct line to the Director, and Tavian said he had only the work sending stone to his boss at the Academy. It worked once a day and carried twenty-five words. He would ask the Academy and, if they agreed, they would send a stone. The news itself had come in a letter carried by griffon from Crystal City. The Director was planning an expedition to Sunhold, to the first ruin the Rifted had seen, and might make the Keel a second stop. A member of the Council might come too.

Tavian said only Rifted seemed able to see the statues at the first ruin, and that the party could describe them to the researchers. The DM described them for the table: a figure standing over a map of a world with a hammer raised, a robed woman below, two dragons to either side, and four elemental symbols around them. Tavian asked them to bring him what they found and put it at the end of the line of crates.

The continent had two airships. One was planned for the Sunhold expedition. The other had left a couple of months before to try to break through a great storm to the south, so only one was left, and the Council owned it. The Aerie had at most seven griffins and a roc, enough for fourteen to twenty-one passengers, not enough to move many people.

Sunhold, he explained, was a new settlement inside an extinct caldera under a large island to the west, where dwarves were mining some of the best ore found in centuries, and Westward was the one getting it. The continent rose and fell on a cycle, and the whole economy followed it. When it came down, people would cheer in the streets, and the advice was to cheer along and hold on to something, because the landing would be rough.

## Slagrow

Tavian said goodbye, and the party went to Cinderstay. The streets of Slagrow ran hotter, and frost landing on the slag melted into small puffs of steam. Cooled slag lay in rivulets over the street. Beau fell at least three times.

Ansel Krane was inside, stoic and broad, with biceps the size of his head, in a plain grey workshop. When he opened the furnace there was no coal or wood in it, only a red and blue glow. He said it was too hot and shut it. He asked Gregory if he had told Dessa he said hello, and Gregory said he had. At the back stood a metal ring a foot off the floor, five to ten feet across, with a tremendous heat coming off it: the shaft. Only one could go down at a time. Ansel put Gregory to work on a table of broken knives and farm tools.

Ansel said he looked after the shaft so no child would fall in. A maul twice his height hung on the wall, and he lifted it one-handed to show them. He had never been down. The forge gave him heat to work by, and he would not meddle with that. The Guild went down in expeditions of about ten people with a week's food, when the people in Crystal asked and paid. Mike asked if anyone had gone down and not come back. Ansel's face dropped. Dessa had lost some of the people she had trained herself, he said, and it was still raw for her, so they should not mention it. People went down for the same reason anyone climbs a mountain, he said, and because nobody knew much about their own history.

Expeditions had excavated six or seven rooms, from what Dessa had told him. Guardians met them there, and he did not think the guardians were evil. At least three rooms they avoided, because the guardians came out of nowhere and people died. New materials and schematics had come up, mostly unreadable, and he guessed half of them were at the Deep Index and the more important ones had gone to Crystal. Nobody knew what generated all the heat. The first couple of rooms past the shaft were fairly safe and mostly empty, though the symbols on the walls remained. He thought another expedition was due once the continent landed. A little birdie had told him the party had found a ruin somewhere, and he hoped there would be more interest there and here.

Mike said he did not want to go down without information, and that the schematics might at least show where the death traps were. The party rolled Persuasion as a group. Gregory rerolled his with a feat and got 16, and the median came to 16, which passed. Ansel went to the back, took out a huge scroll and unrolled it on a cleared table. It was a map of the first seven chambers excavated, the passages between them, the sections of wall not yet dug, and places where the wall was thin enough to make a new entrance. He said he kept it there because Dessa told him to. Then he pulled Gregory back to the table, because Gregory had promised to work that day. Gregory would stay up and pull the rope. The session ended there.
