# Session 03—The Proving Floor

## Bryn, and the Road to Westward

The two figures crossing toward them in the sunlight resolved before long into a woman leading a cart-horse by its bridle, with a second shape a half-step behind her. The woman gave her name as Bryn. [[Layla]] had arranged the transport and paid for it in advance, she said, and didn't seem inclined to explain much past that. She had plenty else to say, though—world, calendar, currency, the Guild's own crest, all of it delivered in the same unhurried, matter-of-fact voice, whether anyone had actually asked or not.

[[Jack XLR|Jack]] still hadn't woken. He lay wrapped in the low, stubborn fire that had clung to him since the night before, and there was no good way to get him onto a cart bound for a town with no healer who knew what he even was. Bryn's companion, silent until then, said he'd carry Jack instead, on foot, toward whatever druid circle sat nearest. [[Gregory Yang]] went with them. No scene was played for the choice itself; he was simply gone by the time the cart moved, following the bodyguard's back down the road. The party going on to Westward was down to four.

The road out was quiet enough that the wheel gave everyone something to do before the real conversation started. It gave out somewhere in the night, and the party woke to Bryn already down in the dirt beside it, hammering wedges into the spokes and swearing at wood that didn't behave the way she expected wood to behave. Watching her reseat it and drive it home with a mallet took the better part of an hour, long enough that walking beside the cart afterward felt less like a decision than the natural next thing to do.

That's where the real talking happened. New bodies were still strange enough to their owners to be worth commenting on: skin gone tough as leather, senses turned up loud enough to notice, a hand somebody kept turning over just to look at it. Bryn, walking alongside rather than driving, didn't find any of it strange at all. This was just what people looked like where she was from—some of them elf, some of them worth a second look for other reasons, none of it worth a first one. When the talk drifted into who lived where in Aenath, she brought up dark elves without lowering her voice the way people do when a word is supposed to carry weight, and said the same of blood. Infernal, celestial, it made nobody good or bad by itself. Just interesting to ask about.

She also handed over a wineskin, warm from where she'd been sitting on it, and it went around the whole group—[[Mike Kanbei|Mike]], [[Ruan Myrt Zaltana|Ruan]], [[Rachel Tan|Rachel]], [[Rismawati Bayu|Risma]], a new body's first taste of it for most of them, though somebody remembered partway through that Risma, at least, had done this before. It did not go well for everyone equally. The first mouthful got delighted reactions and a second round to match it; by the third, stomachs turned, and for a stretch of the walk more than one of them rode with a head hanging off the side of the cart while whoever was steadiest tried to talk the others through it with water that only made things worse. Someone suggested fire would cure it. Someone else suggested fresh air. Bryn just watched the whole debate with the patience of someone who'd seen this exact scene play out on this exact road more times than she cared to count.

It was somewhere in the middle of all that that Risma ended up talking. Between refilling a waterskin and giving up on the water helping, she found herself answering a question nobody had quite finished asking, about home, about whether adult life got any less tiring with age. Hers hadn't been bad, she said. Not really. She'd never wanted for a roof over her head or a steady income, and her father was still around, even with her mother long gone from the picture. On paper it was a good life, and she'd have sworn to it under oath. It was only under a little more pressing that the seams showed: a life that had needed her to become someone slightly different to be taken seriously. She didn't linger on it. She handed the admission over the same plain way she'd handed over the water, then let the subject close.

Rachel's turn came the same stretch of road, in the same loosened mood. Her own admission ran the other direction: not a life she'd made peace with, but one she'd tried to leave. Not long before she'd crossed over, she'd been mid rebellious-phase, booking a flight to anywhere that wasn't Singapore, no destination in mind beyond away. She said it like a joke on herself rather than a confession, and let it land that way.

[[Ruan Myrt Zaltana|Ruan]]'s turn came quieter, almost sideways, in the same stretch of road. She didn't need much drawing out. Her mother was gone too, she offered—had been, even before the crossing over, and there wasn't a great deal more to say about it than that. She said it the way people say things they've already made their peace with, and the road carried the conversation on without dwelling.

## Westward, and Dessa

By the time that settled, the air ahead had started to change. Char first, a sharp burnt smell riding ahead of the smoke that made it, and under that a ragged chorus of hammers on metal, dozens of them, none in time with each other. Two miles out, the party could already pick a river out of the northern hills, a wide silver vein spilling off a high peak and breaking into a waterfall on the mountain's western face. By the time the cart rolled the last stretch, the haze over the rooftops had thickened into something between fog and smoke, and the hammering had stopped being background noise and started being a headache.

The town itself was built from fitted stone, no mortar, no bricks, just rock cut and set so close the seams barely showed. Heat came off every open doorway they passed, forge-heat, the kind that reached out and touched a face in passing. North of the crowded market district stood row on row of small colonnaded buildings, more workshops than temples despite the look of them. West, where the ridge climbed toward the harbor, one building broke from the pattern entirely: pale-plastered, tall, shaped like someone had started building a lighthouse and lost interest halfway through the tower.

Bryn took the cart up the ridge road without being asked and stopped it in front of the building's door. "Welcome to [[Westward|the Cliffwatch]]," she said, swinging down to help with bags nobody actually needed help carrying. "I've done my job. Dessa's inside, I hope she's awake—she runs the place alone most days, which is criminal, if you ask me." A round of quiet thanks, a coin offered and waved off with a grin, and the cart eased back down toward the city, leaving the party at the door of the [[Adamantine Guild]]'s Westward branch.

Inside was one long room, and it did not belong to the same town the party had just walked through. Westward outside was noise and char and hammers out of rhythm; the Cliffwatch's front room was long tables, long benches, and paper—stacks of it, squared at the corners, pinned in neat drifts against one wall. A fireplace with something sitting on the mantel nobody could quite place. At the far bench, a heap of paperwork rose high enough to hide whoever sat behind it.

The heap moved. An elf stood up from behind it, old in the way that showed only where she let it show—fine lines at the eyes, nothing else. Auburn hair nowhere near going grey, skin an even, deep gold. Small reading glasses came off in one hand as she looked the party over, then went back on, like punctuation at the end of a sentence she hadn't spoken yet. A shortsword and a bow sat slung across her back, guild-issue and clean, the kind of weapons that saw more polishing than drawing.

"I know this is going to sound strange," she said, crossing to sit at the nearest bench so she wasn't looming over anyone, "but welcome." She said her name was [[Dessa Kestrel|Dessa]], nothing more, and didn't offer it twice.

She took the party's wrists in turn, or near enough, checking the brass bracers already on them against something only she seemed to be reading. She didn't reach for a fresh set. "These are already real," she said, mild, like she was confirming a shipment count rather than a rank. "Whoever issued these to you did it properly. I've no reason to replace what already works." That settled, she offered to show them where they'd be staying, and led the way to a plain door at the back of the room.

The door opened onto somewhere the building had no business containing—a pocket dimension folded into the branch itself, a floating platform under an open sky that wasn't Westward's sky, with a scatter of smaller rooms drifting off from a central table. Cheese, bread, empty jars. Dessa closed her eyes, touched a platter, and it was full. "The trick isn't the food," she said, catching someone's expression. "Anyone can conjure bread. The trick is making enough of it that nobody notices they're eating the same loaf twice." She mentioned, almost as an aside, that some branches had lately taken to using the jars for something called coffee. She hadn't tried it herself. If the party wanted to, she wouldn't stop them.

The rooms themselves were theirs to shape however they liked, furniture and walls both, so long as nothing physical left the pocket when they did. "Every branch keeps one of these," Dessa said, latching a door shut again. "Best-kept secret the Guild has, not that it's much of a secret once you've seen it." She allowed, with the closest thing to warmth she'd shown yet, that their guild master was difficult to work with and very good at his job, in roughly that order, and left it there.

Settled back at the long table with a fresh pot of something and her glasses pushed up into her hair, Dessa answered questions the way she clearly preferred to work: slowly, and only as far as she actually knew something. The [[Ether Scryer's Academy]]'s Antiquities department, she said, had spent years chasing the same standing mystery—nobody on this world remembered anything from before roughly two hundred years back, not a single verified account older than that, and the Academy's politics ran hot over whose theory explained it best. "Everyone there has already done the calculations," she said, dry. "It's everyone else's job to be wrong about them."

On [[The Claret]], she had less to offer and said so plainly. A criminal syndicate, active more or less everywhere, no trouble reported locally, no name attached to whoever led it—not even the tier directly under that leader knew who gave the orders. "I hate theories," she said, taking her glasses off again. "I'm not going to hand you one dressed up as a fact. But the reports agree the Claret trades in blood. Rare bloodlines, difficult to source, worse to ask about."

That was where Layla's name came up, unprompted from her side. Word out of Crystal City had her once working under [[Garm]], one of the Council of Seven, on some manner of experimental operation moving blood between places it shouldn't easily go. Then she vanished, and a manhunt ran for two months trying to find her, one that Dessa said had likely reached as far as Westward without anyone here fully realizing it. She was careful to frame it as secondhand, reports relayed through reporters relayed through more reporters, nothing she'd verified with her own eyes.

She was blunter about the next part. The blood trade the Claret ran in had a short list of bloodlines it wanted, and tiefling blood was on it. She said it plainly, looking straight at Rachel when she did, no cushioning. It read as a warning she felt obligated to give in full, delivered flat with no softening in it.

Somewhere past the worst of it, she noticed the party's two druids properly for the first time, and mentioned—lightly, an offer rather than a promise—that she knew people in circles that would be worth introducing them to eventually, the [[Azure Tamers]] among them. Nothing came of it that day beyond the offer itself, filed away for whenever she next had reason to make the introduction.

Last, she moved to practical matters: the party, she said, had earned their fourth level, and could take it whenever they chose to, mid-fight if that's when it mattered most—claim it the moment a body actually needed it, not before.

An hour and a break later, Dessa met them back at the portal she'd sealed behind the pocket dimension's door, changed now into a full set of robes over the shortsword and bow, dressed for something more than paperwork. She touched the seam of the portal, and the space beyond it rippled and reshaped itself: the sky held, open and real overhead, but the floor below it had become an arena, mist curling low across worn stone.

"Welcome to the training grounds," she said. "Whenever you want to spar, or drill, or just get better with what you've got, this is where you do it. Some of the younger ones live here for stretches at a time." She said it without much ceremony, the way she said most things, and then turned to face the party properly, cold and unhurried in a way that had nothing to do with unkindness.

"My colleagues tell me they've been beaten down before, by others who came the same way you did," she said. "I believe in proof, not in what I'm told. So show me what you've got." She drew nothing, not yet, just watched them settle into the space and waited for them to be ready.

The session ended there, on the edge of the arena floor, Dessa waiting and the test not yet begun.
