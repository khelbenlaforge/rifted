# Session 02—Six of Six

## What She Finally Said

The climb Session 01 skipped past ended somewhere none of them expected: sunlight, open scrubland, and [[Layla]] waiting again, though not where they'd left her. She knelt this time at a seam torn into the ground itself, one edge of it trembling like something on the other side kept testing it. [[Mike Kanbei]], [[Ruan Myrt Zaltana]], [[Rachel Tan]], and [[Gregory Yang]] came up out of [[The Drowned Dawn]]'s tower to find her still there. She didn't vanish before the fight started this time, because there was no fight. She talked.

What she told them was more than she'd ever given a party before. She'd been tasked to bring souls back, she said—not take, bring back, and she leaned hard on the word, like it cost her something to say aloud. Whoever she answered to wanted them badly enough to keep tearing holes like this one open. She followed instructions. That didn't make her a killer, and when one of them asked outright, she said so plainly: "I'm not a killer." Someone else asked her name. She'd refused it in [[The Drowned Dawn]]; here, she gave it. "Layla."

She'd seen the sixth too, or the edge of him. "If that foot I saw dragged into the portal fits my analysis," she said, "I have a feeling you guys can help." She named a city a day's ride out, [[Westward]], and someone to find once they reached it: whoever led the local branch of the [[Adamantine Guild]]. Transport, she promised, was hers to arrange. Then she left them to decide the rest.

They took a long rest first. Around a fire none of them had built with any real skill, they talked through what little of the vision and the new bodies made sense out loud, circling questions nobody had answers for yet. Nothing about that night resolved cleanly. It just got survived, together, until morning found them still awake enough to choose.

Westward could wait. The seam couldn't. They went through it instead.

## Six Rooms, One Wheel

Past the seam, underground, they found six chambers arranged like a lock nobody had told them how to pick. [[The Hexad]], though nobody in the room ever called it that.

[[Rachel Tan]] started alone, in a chamber where a voice like machinery reciting scripture named its Laws one at a time. The Laws stacked as the crossing went on: ten paces, then five, then a toll due every round after—at one point, a debate over who among them would speak a truth aloud and pay for it. Chains came down on whoever broke the room's own rules; someone had to fight their own way loose from them before it was done.

Elsewhere, guardian statues turned in unison toward the stone Lady standing at the room's center and opened fire. The party didn't shelter her—they tore the threat apart instead, lance and emitter both, and by the time the last statue stopped moving, the cracks running through it were already knitting shut on their own.

A garden grew the wrong way nearby, roots hanging from the ceiling, four ivory flowers that only wanted what was given freely. Blood went into more than one of them. Somebody tried to talk a flower into taking poison instead, and the room, for reasons of its own, let them try.

The floor gave out twice in another chamber—sideways first, then straight up—and they braced or leapt into it and came out the other side bruised regardless.

Something with too many legs and no fixed skull stalked them through waist-high grass. They killed it there, in the grass it had been hunting from.

And elsewhere waited a shop that had no business existing this deep underground—firelight, brewed tea, a faceless merchant selling miracles priced in health, due only if they made it out alive. Somebody bought healing, somebody else bought certainty—nobody left richer for it.

## What the Fire Left Behind

They doubled back to the Chain room running on fumes, and it wasn't empty anymore. A body lay slumped at the base of the wheel: skin like banked coals under a crust of ash, breath curling to smoke, headphones still hooked over one ear. It was [[Jack XLR]], unmistakably, though nobody in the room had a name to put to him yet. He didn't wake. The sixth seat was, for the first time, physically full.

Then the shadow against the wall stopped being just a shadow. It was [[The Sixth Shadow]], though none of them would ever learn to call it that. It thickened, and started reaching for a face. It didn't lunge. It stood there half-finished, wearing the rough outline of whoever it looked at longest, deciding. It moved the way the [[Fused Shadow]] had moved back in [[The Drowned Dawn]]'s chamber, two shapes folding into one that had never really been two. That stillness did more work than teeth would have. Six rooms deep and running on nothing, nobody in the party wanted to find out whose face it meant to finish wearing. Nobody argued when the rest of them turned and ran.

## Sunlight, Six Together

They scrambled for the seam, pushed through a doorway that didn't want to hold still, and broke into sunlight all at once—the first clean light any of them had seen since the wheel swallowed them whole.

Five of them stood upright in the open air. The sixth lay between them, breathing, unmoving, a stranger—the party whole in number for the first time since the tower, and no closer to whole in any way that counted. [[Rismawati Bayu]], still catching her breath, was the one who noticed Jack had a bag on him nobody had clocked before.

They didn't get long to sit with it. Two figures were already crossing toward them, too far off yet to have faces of their own.
