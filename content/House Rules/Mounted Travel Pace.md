# Mounted Travel Pace

## The Rule

RAW travel pace (XPHB p.20: Slow 18 mi/day, Normal 24, Fast 30) is built around a 30 ft base speed and applies unchanged whether the party is on foot, on horseback, or in a wagon. Here, pace instead scales with whichever mode is actually setting the party's speed, using its Speed stat as a multiplier against that 30 ft baseline:

| Mode | Speed | Multiplier | Slow | Normal | Fast |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| On foot | 30 ft | x1 | 18 mi | 24 mi | 30 mi |
| Carriage/wagon (draft horse) | 40 ft | x4/3 | 24 mi | 32 mi | 40 mi |
| Mounted (riding horse/warhorse) | 60 ft | x2 | 36 mi | 48 mi | 60 mi |
| Flying (griffon/giant eagle) | 80 ft | x8/3 | 48 mi | 64 mi | 80 mi |

Flight isn't constrained to roads or coastlines, so pair its pace against straight-line (air) distance rather than the overland estimate used for the ground modes above.

RAW's separate mount-burst clause (XPHB p.20: a mounted group can move at double its pace for 1 hour, then the mounts need a Short or Long Rest before repeating that burst) still applies on top of this table for a genuine one-hour sprint. It's untouched by this house rule, which only replaces the sustained multi-day pace calculation.

## Why This Works

Real-world sustained travel backs this up: a person walking day after day covers roughly what RAW's Normal foot pace already claims (about 20 to 24 miles), while a single rider on a fit horse, no relay mounts, can sustain something close to double that. A loaded carriage, limited by a draft horse's slower gait and the need for real roads, lands in between rather than matching a lone rider. Flight, tied to a mount's fly speed instead of walk speed, comes out faster still, and correctly so. This also fixes the part that never sat right: a mounted (or airborne) party defaulting to the exact same travel time as a party on foot.

## How It Works

Pick whichever mode is actually limiting the party's pace (same principle as RAW: use the slowest member or vehicle in the group). The pace trade-offs from XPHB p.20 still apply regardless of mode: Fast imposes disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception/Survival) and Dexterity (Stealth) checks, Normal imposes disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks, and Slow grants advantage on Wisdom (Perception/Survival) checks.
